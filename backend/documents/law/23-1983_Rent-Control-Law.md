## The Tenancy Law of 1983 (23/1983)

## Short title
1. The Tenancy Laws of 1983 to 1999 shall be referred to together as the Tenancy Laws of 1983 to 1999.

*(23/1983)*

## Interpretation
2. In this Law, unless a different meaning emerges from the text-
“property” means a building under or to be let for a residence or shop situated within the boundaries of a controlled area and completed by 31st December 1999;
"Supreme Court" means the Supreme Court established by virtue of the provisions of subsection (8) of article 3 of the Administration of Justice (Miscellaneous Provisions) Law;
"Supreme Judicial Council" means, pursuant to the provisions of subsection (5) of article 10 of the Grant of Jurisdiction (Miscellaneous Provisions) Law, established Supreme Judicial Council;
"preservable building" means a building for which a Preservation Order has been issued, in accordance with article 38 of the Town Planning and Zoning Laws of 1972 to 1999
"Court" means the Rent Control Court established pursuant to Article 4 of this Law and includes all Departments thereof
"controlled area" means any area of Cyprus that wanted to be declared as such by decree of the Council of Ministers, in accordance with the provisions of subsections (1) and (3) of article 3 of this Law
"lease" means a lease, whether by document or otherwise, or possession of property, by virtue of which the relationship of owner and tenant is created but does not include the lease of land used for agricultural purposes or the lease of stations for the sale of petroleum products or the lease of parking space for motor vehicles, or the lease of furnished houses or apartments for a period of less than six months or rental of hotels, hotel units or tourist accommodation
It is understood that the right of possession and the determination of the rent or compensation from the sublease or license between the gas station owner and the petroleum company are governed by and regulated by the provisions of this Law and, in the event of the station being moved to another property, these rights of the gas station owner are not affected, applying, in such a case, the provisions of article 14(1)(b) of this Law, in compliance with proportions
"tenant" means any natural or legal person who usually resides or has his registered office in Cyprus and who is a tenant of a property, in relation to which there is a tenancy and includes-
(a) tenant institution
(b) any sub-tenant or any other person acquiring a right to possession of the property from the original tenant or sub-tenant
(c) the surviving spouse who, or the tenant's child, resided or had his main occupation in the rental property with him at the time of his death or was regularly residing abroad or temporarily working abroad at the time of the tenant's death or, in the case of a rental residence, when the tenant does not leaves a spouse or the tenant is a woman, such member of the tenant's family as resided with him for a period not less than six months immediately before the tenant's death
(d) the estranged due to abandonment of the conjugal home by the husband of the tenant, his spouse as well as their children
(e) the Government of the Republic of Cyprus and any legal entity under public law,
but does not include a non-citizen of the Republic, excluding the spouse of a citizen of the Republic, or a legal entity controlled by foreigners
"rent" includes any amount paid as rent and rent for the use of furniture in cases where the property is rented furnished or in cases where the property is rented and the furniture therein is leased by the owner to the tenant but does not include communal
"works" means alterations or other building works in a listed building for which the necessary consent has been secured, pursuant to the provisions of subsection (1) of article 38 of the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Law
"Court of Appeal" means the Court of Appeal established by virtue of the provisions of subsection (1) of article 3A of the Administration of Justice (Miscellaneous Provisions) Law;
"institutional tenant" means a tenant of a property who, at the expiration or termination of the first tenancy, continues to occupy the property and always includes an institutional tenant prior to the effective date of this Law
"owner" includes in relation to any immovable property any natural or legal person, except the tenant, who is or would be entitled, without the provisions of this Law, to possession of the immovable property, and in the case of subletting, a tenant who sublets the immovable property or any part thereof
"shop" means immovable property rented for any work, trade or any other professional purpose and used as such
"dwelling" means a building or structure or part thereof that is rented as a person's residence or for housing associations, foundations and non-professional organizations, even if part of it is used for professional purposes
"shared" means-
(a) the use of common areas of the property by its tenants
(b) the use of common facilities of the property
(c) the use of shared sanitary arrangements and facilities of the property
(d) the supply of electricity and water to the common areas of the property for the use or service of its tenants and
(e) the provision of services for the cleaning and guarding of the common areas of the property and such other facilities as are necessary for the lessee's possession and use of the lessee's leased property or part thereof
"first rental" means the first rental of the property by the respective tenant and its duration is determined by the rental document or oral agreement or, in the absence thereof, by the method of payment
"Minister" means the Minister of Justice.

## Application of the law in matters of repossession of houses belonging to the Government
2A. In every case of a residence that is rented as the residence of a person, the owner of which is the Government of the Republic of Cyprus, the provisions of this Law shall apply, to the extent that they concern issues of possession, recovery of possession and eviction of an institutional tenant of a residence to which this Law applies, or concern any procedure, proceeding or measure related to said matters, and any reference to said provisions, to the terms "tenant" and "institutional tenant" in relation to a residence, includes a tenant and an institutional tenant of a residence owned by the Government of the Republic of Cyprus.

*(150(I)/2002)*

## Pending court cases and enforcement of pending court decisions
2B.-(1) In court cases, which on the date of entry into force of the Tenancy (Amendment) Law of 2002, were pending before any court, other than the court established by virtue of this Law, no decision or decree shall be issued for the recovery of possession of a residence, in relation to which the provisions of article 2A are applied, or for the eviction of an institution tenant from her.
(2) It is not permitted to enforce any decisions or decrees to recover possession of a residence in relation to which the provisions of article 2A apply, or to evict an institutional tenant from it, which were issued before the effective date of the Tenancy (Amendment) Law of 2002 by any court other than the court established under this Law.

*(150(I)/2002)*

## Application of the Law for real estate in controlled areas
3.-(1) Whenever the Council of Ministers deems it necessary or appropriate for the purpose of ensuring the availability of houses or shops in return for reasonable rents and the security of their possession, or whenever the public interest otherwise requires, the Council of Ministers may, by decree published in the official gazette of the Republic, declare any area in Cyprus as a controlled area, and as a result, the provisions of this Law will apply to all shops and residences or only to all shops or to all residences, as determined by the Council of Ministers.
(2) The Council of Ministers may, if the circumstances that led to the issuing of a decree pursuant to subsection (1) cease to exist or if the public interest otherwise requires, cancel such decree in whole or in part with regard to the shops and residences as a whole or only with regard to all the shops or as a whole of residences, and if it is annulled by the provisions of this Law, subject to all conditions that may be listed in this decree, it will cease to apply to such area or category of real estate as the case may be.
(3) The Council of Ministers for the implementation of the previous subsections (1) and (2) must every five years at least examine and decide whether, at its discretion, any differentiation is needed in the existing decrees concerning the areas controlled by the Tenancy, taking into account the commercial and tourist development of the area concerned.

## Establishment of Court
4. Rent Control Courts are established, the number of which will not exceed three for the purpose of resolving, with all reasonable speed, the disputes referred to them arising on any matter raised during the implementation of this Law, including any incidental or supplementary matter.
(2) Each Court is composed of a President (hereinafter referred to as "the President") and two other members appointed by the Supreme Judicial Council as provided for in this article.
(3) No person shall be appointed as President of the Court unless he is qualified to be appointed as a District Judge in accordance with section 6(1) of the Courts Acts 1960 to 1982 or any other Act amending or replacing them.
(4) For each case before the Court, the President appoints the two members from which the Court will be composed from lists of members approved by the Supreme Judicial Council for each province.
For this purpose, the Minister submits for each province a list of thirty suitable persons of the highest moral level from which the Supreme Judicial Council selects twenty persons who make up every two years the list for the province from which the President designates the members who will make up the Court for each specific case:
Provided that a person who has been designated as a member of the Court in a specific case, the trial of which has begun, but has not yet been completed, continues to participate as a member of this Court even after the expiration of two years from the compilation by the Supreme Judicial Council of the relevant list, in which his name is included, until the trial of this case is completed:
It is further understood that any person, whose name was included in the list drawn up by the Supreme Judicial Council, may by a document addressed to the President of the Supreme Judicial Council resign, in which case the following procedure is followed to replace the resigned person:
Provided further that both during the compilation of the lists and the definition of the members of which the Court will be composed, care must be taken so that the interests of the owners and tenants are equally represented.
(5) The resolution of any dispute submitted to the Court as provided for in subsection (1) of this article is decided by the President after receiving the opinions of the two members of the Court, who have only an advisory opinion.
(6) The remuneration of the President and his other terms of service are those as defined in article 8 of the Courts Laws 1960 to 1982 or any other Law amending or replacing them in relation to a District Judge and upon completion of the highest step of the scale in relation to a Senior District Judge, and the members such appearance expenses shall be paid as the Ministers of Finance and Justice decided by joint decision.
(7) In case of temporary absence or incapacity of the President, the Supreme Judicial Council appoints on a temporary basis any other person with the qualifications as appointed as President to exercise the powers and perform the duties of President during the temporary absence or incapacity of the President.
(8) No action shall be brought against the President or member of the Court for any act or omission committed by him during the exercise of his duties pursuant to this Law.
(9) There is a Secretariat in addition to the Courts and in each province where it operates, under the direction of a Secretary, the composition of whose staff was to be appointed by the Minister for the performance of the tasks that were intended to be foreseen by a procedural regulation issued under the Supreme Court.
The Secretary and members of the Secretariat's staff are civil servants.
(10) Notwithstanding the provisions of any other Law in force, the sessions of the Court in each province where it convenes shall be held in such building as the Minister of Justice may from time to time designate as a Court for this purpose.
(11) All costs and expenses required for the operation of the Court shall be paid from the public treasury.

## Summary judgment
5. The Court, when hearing any case pursuant to this Law, observing any procedural regulation, is not bound by the applicable law of evidence, and in the event that a witness refuses to answer any question, which in the opinion of the Court tends to incriminate him, he is not required to answer such question. question and is not subject to prosecution because he refuses to answer it.

*(23/1983)*

## Review of decrees or decisions of the Court
6. An order or decision of the Court pursuant to this Law may, upon request, be reviewed, amended or annulled by the Court in the following cases:
(a) In the event that the facts of the case affecting the issue of rent have substantially changed or a substantial change of circumstances has been noted since the issuance of the decree or decision
(b) where the decree or judgment was obtained as a result of any fraud, misrepresentation or material mistake;
(c) in a case where there is available new evidence of a substantial nature that could not have been obtained through the exercise of reasonable diligence when the decree or decision was issued
(d) in a case where the decree or decision was issued in the absence of the party, whose absence was not due to any omission or negligence on his part
(e) in a case where an eviction order has been issued under section 11(1), (f), (g), (h) and the owner within a reasonable time of delivery of possession has not implemented the reasons for the eviction.

## Court applications to determine rents for houses and shops
8.-(1) No increase in the rent of houses or shops may be imposed on an institutional tenant except as provided in this Law.
(2) It is lawful for the legal tenant or the owner of any residence or shop, if he considers himself aggrieved, to apply to the Court for the determination of the legal rent payable in relation to such residence or shop:
Provided that no application is registered before two years have passed from the date the tenant took possession of the property or from the date of the last rent increase or decrease.
It is further understood that it is lawful for the owner and the tenant to enter into negotiations and by written agreement to agree on another increase in the rent, provided that the last such increase will not exceed the maximum rate of rent increase determined from time to time in accordance with the provisions of subsection (4) of this article and that no such increase will takes place before the expiration of two years from the date on which the tenant took possession of the property or from the date of the last rent increase.
(3) In the event that such an application is submitted to the Court, the Court examines it and, after conducting such an investigation if it deems it appropriate and giving each of the parties the opportunity to be heard, and taking into account the limitations and circumstances mentioned in subsections (4) and (5), determines the fair rent from the date of registration of such request and the amount thus determined is considered as the rent which the tenant is obliged to pay to the owner.
For the purposes of this paragraph, Appraisal Officers will serve in addition to the Court who, following the instructions of the Court, will be able to conduct the investigation and file the report before the Court.
(4)(a) The upper limit of the legal rent determined by the Court shall not exceed 14 percent during the first two years from the entry into force of this Law, after the end of which period the rate shall be determined every two years by the Council of Ministers on the recommendation of the Minister by decree published in the official gazette of the Republic.
Provided that, if the first application submitted after the date of commencement of the Tenancy (Amendment) Law of 1995 for a rent increase based on the permitted rate, which does not now exceed 14%, leads to the determination of a rent lower than 40% of the average rents of the small area, then a rent equal to 40% of that average is determined. The percentage of 40% as above, for the first or subsequent applications, increases from 1.1.1997 by 10 percentage points every two years, until it reaches 90% of the average rent of the small area.
(b) The Court's maximum legal rent shall in no event exceed 80% of the rates set forth in subsection (4)(a) for displaced persons and sufferers.
For the purposes of this paragraph, the terms "displaced" and "suffering" acquire the meaning assigned to these terms under Part V of this Law.
(5) Subject to the above paragraphs, in order to determine the fair rent, the market rent and the average rent in the same small area must be calculated, without taking into account the rarity of similar properties in the same small area, while all circumstances (except personal ones) must be taken into account, including the age, character, size, location, condition of the property (and, in the case of a listed building, whether projects have been carried out on it by its owner and at what cost), as well as the facilities provided to it.

## Rent register
9.-(1) The Clerk of the Court prepares and keeps an updated register for cases of recovery of possession or eviction of a tenant and a register for determining rent which will be made available to all interested parties during the working hours of the registry office upon payment of the statutory fees.
(2) The tenant eviction register is prepared and kept up-to-date so that it contains, with reference to any residence or shops in relation to which the tenant's eviction has been requested pursuant to article 11 of this Law, entries-
(a) the names of the parties referred to in the case file and the address of the residences or shops of those necessary for their identification
(b) the judgment or order of the Court.
(3) The register for determining the fair rent shall be prepared and maintained so as to contain, with reference to any residence or shops in relation to which the fair rent has been determined pursuant to this Law, entries-
(a) the details of residences or shops necessary for their identification and the names of the parties referred to in the application
(b) the legal rent as determined by the Court and the date of commencement of its payment.

*(23/1983)*

## Rents in excess of registered rents for residences and shops are illegal
10.-(1) When the rent payable for any residence or shop is registered in a register maintained pursuant to the provisions of article 9, it is not lawful for the owner to receive against the rent of the residence or shop, in relation to the two-year period following the date of commencement of its payment as entered in the register, any amount in excess of the rent so registered or to set as a condition of granting, renewing or continuing the rental of the residence or shop, the payment of any amount in addition to the rent.
(2) Whoever pays or accepts as rent or in any other form any amount in excess of the rent determined by the Court is guilty of a criminal offense and liable to imprisonment not exceeding three months or to a fine not exceeding five hundred pounds or to both of these penalties and the amount so collected amount paid to the Rent Subsidy Fund:
Provided that it is lawful for the owner to accept and for the tenant to pay any amount beyond the rent determined by the Court if, after the expiration of the two-year period referred to in subsection (1) of this article, both parties enter into negotiations and by private contract agree to another increase in the rent, provided that the latter will not exceed the maximum limit of rent increase determined at any time in accordance with the provisions of article 8 of this Law.

*(23/1983)*

## Restriction of evictions
11.-(1) No decision and no decree shall be issued for the recovery of possession of any residence or shop, to which this Law applies, or for the consequent eviction of an institutional tenant, except in the following cases:
(a)(i) In the event that any rent lawfully due is in arrears for twenty-one days or more after service of a demand notice on the tenant and no tender thereof is made prior to the filing of an application for recovery of possession:
It is understood that rent will be considered offered by virtue of this paragraph, if it is sent by registered letter to the person entitled to collect it:
It is further provided that the Court does not order the recovery from the owner of the possession, when the tenant pays within a period of fourteen days from the service of the application on him all the amount due or which may become due from him unless the tenant for the duration of the lease does not systematically pay what is legally due:
(ii) an answer to an application for recovery of possession registered under sub-paragraph (i) shall be accepted for registration by the Clerk of the Court only if the answer is accompanied either by evidence from the Court's accounting office that the amount due in arrears of rent referred to in the application has been deposited with the Court on the date of its registration or by a receipt for the collection of rent issued by the owner or his agent or by evidence of a deposit from a financial institution in favor of the owner or representative thereof:
It is provided that the Secretary's decision to accept or reject the registration of the answer is placed within three (3) working days before the Court for final approval or rejection, and the decision of the Court is not subject to appeal.
(iii) in case of issuing an eviction order following the request of the owner as above, the Court may determine the time of compliance with the eviction order which may not be less than ninety (90) days.
(b) in a case where the tenant or any other occupier of the residence or shop under him has been guilty of conduct constituting a nuisance or a permanent nuisance to persons on the same or neighboring properties or has been found guilty of having acted so that the residence or shop was used or permitted to be used for illegal or immoral purposes or purposes
(c) in a case where the condition of the residence or shop has, in the opinion of the Court, worsened due to destructive acts or willful gross negligence of the tenant or in a case where the tenant wrongfully caused or allowed significant damage to the property to be caused:
It is understood that the Court does not order the eviction of a tenant if the tenant, within two months of the submission of the application for recovery of possession, fully repairs the damages or
(d) in a case where the tenant, despite the express obligation not to sublet, wanted this violation, and the Court considers it reasonable to issue such a decision or such a decree or
(e) in a case where the tenant, by hiring tenants or by subletting or otherwise separating the possession of the whole or any part of the residence or the shop, realizes such a profit, either directly or indirectly, which in relation to the rent paid by the tenant is logically disproportionate and the A court considers it reasonable to issue such a decision or such a decree or
(f) in a case where the property is reasonably required by the owner for occupancy or for the occupancy of the spouse, child or dependent parent or spouse of the latter or, where the owner is a family company, for occupancy of a member thereof and the Court considers it reasonable to make such a decision or order:
Provided that no judgment or decree shall be issued under this paragraph, if the Court is satisfied that, taking into account all the circumstances of the case, greater inconvenience would be caused by the issuance of the decree or judgment than by the refusal to issue it.
For the purposes of this paragraph, the term "circumstances of the case" includes, among other things, whether the tenant is displaced or suffering, as the terms are defined in Part V of this Law, whether there is another comparable and reasonable rental unit available for the tenant, and whether the owner purchased the property after the date on which this Law came into force for the purpose of acquiring possession by virtue of the provisions of this paragraph or
(g) in a case where the store is reasonably required to be occupied by the owner, his wife or his children and where any of them has not been able to secure another similar and at a reasonable rent accommodation for his business or for the purposes of the business and the Court considers it reasonable to issue such a decision or such a decree:
Provided that no judgment or decree shall be issued under this paragraph if the Court is satisfied that, having regard to all the circumstances of the case, greater inconvenience would be caused by the issuance of the decree or judgment than by the refusal to issue it, or
(h) in case the property is reasonably required by the owner-
(j) for the demolition thereof as long as this does not constitute an abuse of right,
(ii) for the demolition and reconstruction of a new property, or
(iii) for materially significant changes that entail its substantial and significant transformation for the purposes of its exploitation or
(iv) for the execution of works in a listed building,
and the Court is convinced that the owner secured the necessary permission for the above, whenever it was necessary, and that the owner cannot reasonably proceed with the actions in sub-paragraphs (j), (ii), (iii) and (iv) without regaining possession of the property, given that he provided no less than four months' notice warning to the tenant to vacate the property or
(i) in the event that the residence or shop is required to be vacated for the execution of a development plan or further development, including the execution of works in a preserved building, pursuant to any Town Planning Law in force at any given time or
(j) in the event that the residence or shop has undergone compulsory expropriation under the Law on Compulsory Expropriation or under any other Law authorizing the compulsory expropriation of property or
(k) where the residence or shop is reasonably required for the purpose of carrying out the lawful duties or powers of a local authority or for any purpose which, in the opinion of the Court, is in the public interest or
(l) in the event that the tenant gives written notice of his intention to vacate the property and, as a result of such notice, the owner has entered into a contract of sale or lease of the property or taken any other measures as a result of which, in the opinion of the Court, the owner would suffer serious damage if he did not obtain the owner of the property;
(m) in a case arising from the provisions of any law or regulations, which are applied by the Government of the Republic of Cyprus for the granting of a rental house belonging to it, or in case it can reasonably be deduced from the circumstances of the granting of a rental house owned by the Government or from the provisions of the rental document, that the rental was made to serve a public purpose which is connected with the tenant's capacity as an employee or an official of the Government or as a person who at that time satisfied criteria defined in law or regulations, and the tenant ceases for any reason after the date of commencement of the Tenancy (Amendment) Law of 2002, to have the said status or to satisfy the said criteria.
(2) In those cases where no other written notice of eviction is required by this article, the landlord must give the tenant a written notice at least one month in advance, without this affecting the cases pending before the court since the publication of the Tenancy (Amendment) Law of 1995.
(3) The eviction proceedings pending before the Court or another court of first instance, on the day of publication in the Official Gazette of the Republic of the Tenancy (Amendment) Law of 1995, are heard on the basis of this Law with the exception of the provisions of the previous subsection (2).
(4) The written warning sent in accordance with the provisions of paragraph (a) of subsection (1) of this article is also considered as an appropriate notice that automatically terminates the rental agreement, without this affecting, on the effective date of the Tenancy (Amendment) Law of 2007, the cases pending before the Court or before any provincial court.
(5) The Court, issuing a decision or decree pursuant to paragraphs (a), (b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i), (j), (k) and (l) of this article, may, subject to the condition that the tenant shall pay all amounts legally due or liable to become due thereunder, suspend the execution of the decision or decree or to postpone the date of possession for such a period not exceeding one year, unless the parties otherwise wished to agree, and subject to such conditions as the Court wished to consider appropriate.

## Compensation to the shop tenant in certain cases
12. In cases where a judgment or decree is issued in accordance with the provisions of paragraphs (g) and (h) of article 11, the Court shall have the discretionary power to order the applicant to pay the tenant compensation, which shall not exceed an amount equal to eighteen months' current rent, if the judgment or decree relates to a shop.

## Compensation to the tenant in cases of commercial favor
13. When due to the exercise of business or work by the tenant in the store there is a commercial favor (air) which the tenant will lose in whole or in part in the event of moving to another store, the Court, when issuing a decision or decree pursuant to any of paragraphs (g) and (h) of subsection (1) of article 11, may not consider the provision of article 12 to be sufficient compensation, to order the payment of additional compensation to the tenant, which does not exceed the loss of commercial favor, taking into account all the circumstances of the tenant, as well as the possibility of an increase in the rental value of the property due to the loss of commercial favor, while the decree or decision is not enforceable, until the above amount of compensation is paid.

## Granting a new lease to the tenant in certain cases
14.-(1) Shop tenant-
(a) against whom a decision or decree was issued pursuant to the provisions of paragraph (h) of subsection (1) of article 11, and
(b) whose property or work has been connected during the last five years with the store, in such a way that he would suffer a loss if he transferred and continued his property or work to another store, may, in the event that the new properties include any store, demand by the service of notice to the owner, within three months of the issuance of the decision or decree, the granting him a new lease of that shop, if any, upon an offer of legal rent and the aforementioned tenant after the termination or expiration of the new lease of the shop, becomes an institutional tenant and in the absence of an agreement between the parties as to the rent, the Court determines every two years the fair rent without being limited subject to any limit:
It is understood that no application is registered before two years have passed from the date of the determination of the legal rent by the Court.
(2) In the event that the owner-
(a) refuse to grant such lease as provided in subsection (1), or
(b) not giving notice within two months of the service of the notice of his desire to do so,
the tenant may apply to the Court for the grant of such tenancy, and if the Court deems that the grant of a new tenancy is reasonable in all the circumstances it may order the grant of such tenancy on payment of a fair rent and for such period and on such terms as the Court may, in the absence of agreement between the parties, judge appropriate and under the condition that the tenant will immediately return the compensation paid to him pursuant to articles 12 and 13 of this Law.
(3) For the purposes of this article, "fair rent" means rent which, in the absence of an agreement between the parties, will be determined by the Court as provided for in subsection (5) of article 8.
(4) The tenant is entitled to demand the granting of a new lease with a counterclaim included in the application for recovery of possession, after the previous paragraphs of this article have been applied accordingly. In this case no notice is required to be sent.

## False representations when making a decision or decree
15. If, after the owner has made a decision or order for possession or eviction, pursuant to this Part, the Court considers that the decision or order was obtained by misrepresentation or by the concealment of material facts, the Court may order the owner to pay to the previous tenant such amount as appears to be sufficient as compensation for damage or loss suffered by the tenant as a result of the decision or decree.

*(23/1983)*

## Power of Court to impose conditions
16. In any application made pursuant to this Part, the Court may, at its discretion, order that, in addition to or in lieu of any other order which the Court wishes to issue, each party shall comply with any conditions, including the payment by one party to the other of any amount agreed upon, which the Court wishes to consider must impose in order to give legal effect to the purposes of this Law.

## Definitions
17. For the purposes of this Part-
"inaccessible area" means any area in Cyprus declared as such by virtue of the tenancy laws of 1975 to 1980 repealed by this Law and includes any other area declared as such by Decision of the Council of Ministers in accordance with the provisions of subsection (3) of article 18;
"distressed area" means any part of a controlled area within the meaning of the Law declared a distressed area by virtue of the tenancy laws of 1975 to 1980 repealed by this Law and includes any other section declared a distressed area by decree of the Council of Ministers issued in accordance with the provisions of subsection (2) of article 18;
"state of emergency" means a state of emergency created as a result of the Turkish invasion which continues to exist until the Council of Ministers, by notification published in the official newspaper of the Republic, sets the date of expiry of such state of affairs;
"displaced person" has the meaning attributed to this term by the Population Register Law, as it is amended or replaced from time to time, and includes displaced persons who attend a higher education school in the Republic recognized by the Ministry of Education and Culture, who rents a residence in the Republic;
"controlled area" means an area in Cyprus declared as such by virtue of the tenancy laws of 1975 to 1980 repealed by this Law and includes any other area declared as such by decree of the Council of Ministers issued in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1) of article 18;
"sufferer" has the meaning attributed to this term by the Law on the Relief of Sufferers, as it is amended or replaced from time to time, and includes a sufferer who attends a higher education school in the Republic recognized by the Ministry of Education and Culture, who rents a residence in the Republic;

## Application of the Law for real estate in controlled, difficult and inaccessible areas
18.-(1) Whenever the Council of Ministers deems it necessary or appropriate for the purpose of ensuring the availability of houses or shops in lieu of high rents and the security of their possession, or whenever the public interest otherwise requires, the Council of Ministers may, by decree published in the official gazette of the Republic, declare any area in Cyprus as a controlled area, in which case the provisions of this Part will apply to any residence or shops within such area.
(2) Whenever the Council of Ministers deems it necessary or appropriate for the purpose of providing relief to tenants whose shops are located within a certain part of a controlled area within the meaning of this Part of an area in which, due to proximity to dangerous points as a result of the Turkish invasion, the usual exercise of their work has been adversely affected and substantially reduced so that it becomes necessary to take measures to relieve them, the Council of Ministers may, by decree published in the official newspaper of the Republic, declare this section as a disadvantaged area, in which case the provisions of this Part will apply to any shops within this area.
(3) The Council of Ministers may, by notice published in the official newspaper of the Republic, declare any area not controlled by the forces of the Republic as an inaccessible area.
(4) The Council of Ministers may, if the circumstances that led to the issuance of a decree pursuant to subsections (1) and (2) or the publication of a notification pursuant to subsection (3) cease to exist or when the public interest otherwise requires, annul such decree or notification if it does not cancel it the provisions of this Part, subject to all conditions that may be listed in this decree, shall cease to apply to such area.

## Application of rents in a distressed area
19.-(1) Subject to the provisions of subsection (2) no rent increase may be imposed on any tenant of a shop in a disadvantaged area beyond the rent determined by the Court or agreed upon or paid before the effective date of this Law.
(2) Upon the entry into force of this Law and thereafter every two years, the Council of Ministers, on the recommendation of the Minister, shall be empowered to, by decree published in the official gazette of the Republic, determine a percentage ratio of increase and decrease of the rents paid by tenants in an area or areas that are underperforming and the rent shall be deemed to be the rent which the tenant is obliged to pay to the landlord.

*(23/1983)*

## Exemption from paying rent in inaccessible areas
20.-(1) No rent shall be paid for any rental of real estate located within an inaccessible area declared as such by virtue of subsection (3) of article 18 of this Law.
(2) The exemption from the obligation to pay rent starts from the date on which the area became inaccessible and is valid as long as the area continues to be so.
(3) The Council of Ministers may, for the purpose of providing relief to the tenants of this area, declare this or part of this area as a controlled or disturbed area pursuant to subsections (1) or (2) of article 18, as soon as the area ceases to be inaccessible.

*(23/1983)*

## Rent reduction under Law 51 of 1974
21. Any reduction in residential rent made pursuant to the provisions of the Residential Property (Temporary Provisions) Law of 1974 shall be deemed to be a reduction made pursuant to the provisions of this Part.

(2) The costs of paying the above allowance are covered by a special fund of the respective State budget which is part of the budget concerning the Ministry of the Interior.

## Hierarchical appeal

## Terms of rental institution
27.-(1) A tenant who, by virtue of the provisions of this Law, maintains possession of any residence or shop, as long as he maintains such possession, observes all the terms and conditions of the last rental contract, even if it has expired and is entitled to the benefits arising from them, as long as they are in accordance with the provisions of this Law, he is entitled to relinquish possession of the residence or shop only after giving such notice as would be appealed under the tenancy agreement and in the event that such notice is not required, only after a written warning, at least one month in advance:
It is understood that in the event that the subtenant becomes an institutional tenant, such institutional tenant owns the residence or the shop subject to the addition of any existing restrictive agreements contained in the rental terms and conditions between the owner and the tenant.
(2) If there is no rental contract or if the contract does not state the contrary, the owner is obliged to repair latent defects, the waterproofing of the roof, the electrical, hydraulic and drainage installation and the physical damages that create a risk to the life of the tenant or the tenant.
(3) Without prejudice to the provisions of the previous paragraphs of this article, the tenant is obliged to protect the property from damages that may be caused by him, by the tenants, by his representatives or invitees and is obliged to repair them without delay.
The tenant is required to allow the landlord to inspect the rental property at reasonable intervals and upon notice.
(4) For the application of the previous subsections of this article, the tenant of the property may notify the owner of the necessary repairs or of the necessary maintenance and the owner is obliged to carry out these without delay.
This paragraph is applied by analogy also in case of failure to fulfill the tenant's obligation based on the previous paragraph.
Failure to fulfill the obligations for repair or maintenance creates a conductive right.
(5) If there is no agreement to the contrary, the tenant is obliged to pay the amount of the common users attributable to the property, including small maintenance costs of common areas and common facilities and excluding serious maintenance, modification or replacement costs.
(6) The lessee of a property is obliged to accept the carrying out by the owner of reasonable repairs, renovations and alterations for the maintenance or improvement of the property.
