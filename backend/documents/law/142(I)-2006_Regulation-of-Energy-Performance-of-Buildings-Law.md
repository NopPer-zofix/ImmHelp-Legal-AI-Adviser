## The Regulation of the Energy Performance of Buildings Law of 2006 (142(I)/2006)
The House of Representatives votes as follows:

## Short title
1. This Law shall be referred to as the Regulation of Energy Performance of Buildings Law of 2006.

*(142(I)/2006)*

## Interpretation
2.-(1) In this Law, unless a different meaning emerges from the text -
"large-scale renovation" means the renovation of a building in which more than 25% of the area of the building envelope is renovated;
"heat pump" means the machine, device or installation that transfers heat from a natural environment, such as air, water or ground, to buildings or industrial applications by reversing the natural flow of heat, so that it flows from a lower to a higher temperature; for reversible heat pumps it may transfer heat from the building to the natural environment;
"minimum building energy performance requirements" means the requirements set out in Article 15;
"competent authority" means the Energy Service of the Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry;
"cost-optimal level" means the level of energy efficiency which results in the lowest cost over the estimated economic life cycle, where -
(a) the lower cost is determined taking into account energy investment costs, maintenance and operation costs (including energy costs and savings, building category, profits from energy produced), as the case may be, and disposal costs, as the case may be;
(b) the estimated economic life cycle is determined by the competent authority and concerns the remaining estimated economic life cycle of a building, if the energy performance requirements have been set for the building as a whole, or the estimated economic life cycle of a building element, if the energy performance requirements have been set for the building elements:
Provided that the cost-optimal level lies within the range of performance levels where the cost-benefit analysis over the entire estimated economic life cycle is positive;
"decree" means a decree issued by the Minister under the provisions of this Law, which is published in the Official Gazette of the Republic;
"minimum building energy performance requirements order" means the order issued by the Minister under section 15;
"building energy efficiency calculation methodology decree" means the decree issued by the Minister pursuant to section 14;
"installer of technical building systems" means a person who is registered by the competent authority in the register of installers of technical building systems and who installs, adjusts and controls technical building systems;
"qualified expert" means a person registered by the competent authority in the register of qualified experts for a specified category or categories of buildings and who, after preparing building energy performance calculations, issues a building energy performance certificate, which includes recommendations;
“heating system inspection report” means the inspection report drawn up by the heating system inspector following an inspection;
“air conditioning system inspection report” means the inspection report drawn up by the air conditioning system inspector following an inspection;
“estimated economic life cycle” means the remaining estimated economic life cycle of a building, as determined by the competent authority, where energy performance requirements have been set for the building as a whole or the estimated economic life cycle of a building component, where energy performance requirements have been set for building elements;
"alternative high efficiency power generation systems" means any of the following systems:
(a) decentralized energy supply systems based on energy from renewable sources;
(b) cogeneration systems;
(c) district heating or cooling systems or block-scale heating/cooling systems, in particular when fully or partially based on energy from renewable sources;
(d) heat pumps;
"renewable energy" means energy from renewable non-fossil sources, namely wind, solar, aerothermal, geothermal, hydrothermal and ocean, hydroelectric, biomass, landfill gas, wastewater treatment plant and biogas energy;
"building energy performance" means the calculated or measured amount of energy needed to meet the energy demand associated with the normal use of the building or building unit, which includes, inter alia, the energy used for heating, cooling, ventilation, hot water production and lighting;
"energy audit" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of the Energy Efficiency Law;
"engineering inspector" [Deleted]
"heating system inspector" means a person who is registered by the competent authority in the register of inspectors and carries out an inspection of heating systems;
“air conditioning systems inspector” means a person who is registered by the competent authority in the register of inspectors and carries out an inspection of air conditioning systems;
“Commission” means the Commission of the European Union;
"European standard" means the standard issued by the European Committee for Standardization (CEN), the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardization (Cenelec) or the European Telecommunications Standardization Institute (ETSI) and made available for public use;
“electrical infrastructure” means the electrical installation, whether the whole installation or any part thereof, the building or the parking area including the electric cables, appliances and related equipment;
"electric vehicle" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of the Promotion and Development of Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Law;
"regulations" means regulations made under section 19;
"Regulation (EU) No. 244/2012" means the delegated Regulation (EU) No. 244/2012 of the Commission of 16 January 2012 to supplement Directive 2010/31/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council, on the energy efficiency of buildings by defining a comparative methodological framework for the calculation of the optimal cost levels of the minimum energy efficiency requirements of buildings and structural elements;
"building envelope" means the built-in elements of a building that separate its interior from the exterior;
“indoor climatic conditions” means the conditions of temperature, humidity, air velocity and quality, and lighting required for smooth living in an indoor space;
"building unit" means a section, floor or apartment within a building, designed or converted to be used separately and for which energy is used to regulate indoor climate conditions;
"building" means the roofed structure with walls for which energy is used to regulate indoor climate conditions;
"near-zero energy building" means the building with very high energy efficiency, determined according to the building energy efficiency calculation methodology and whose near-zero or very low amount of energy required is covered to a very large extent by energy from renewable sources, including that produced on-site or in the vicinity of the building;
"boiler" means the combined boiler and burner unit system, designed to transfer the heat produced by combustion to liquids.
"building energy efficiency calculation methodology" means the methodology specified in the building energy efficiency methodology decree based on the framework specified in Annex IV;
“register of installers of technical building systems” means the register kept under section 9B;
"register of qualified experts" means the register kept under section 7A;
“register of heating inspectors and register of air-conditioning inspectors” means the register kept under section 13A;
"register of energy performance certificates of buildings" means the register kept on the basis of subsection (9) of article 6;
"heat generating unit" means the part of a heating system that produces useful heat using one or more of the following processes:
(a) Combustion of fuel, for example, in a boiler;
(b) exploiting the Joule effect in the heating elements of an electric resistance heating system;
(c) capturing heat from ambient air, exhaust ventilation air, or a water or ground heat source using a heat pump;
"new building" and "new building unit" means the building or building unit that will be erected and for which an application for a planning or building permit has been submitted:
Provided that in the case of new buildings or new building units that do not require the issuance of a planning or building permit, the provisions of article 5 of the Law shall apply.
“Directive 2014/94/EU” means Directive 2014/94/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 22 October 2014 on the development of alternative fuels infrastructure;
“Directive (EU) 2018/844” means Directive 2018/844 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 30 May 2018 amending Directive 2010/31/EU on the energy performance of buildings and Directive 2012/27/EU on energy performance;
"rated power output" means the maximum thermal power expressed in kW which the manufacturer states and guarantees as being provided during continuous operation while respecting the useful efficiency specified by the manufacturer;
"assessment body" means the assessment body, as determined on the basis of the criteria provided for in the decree issued pursuant to paragraph (a) of subsection (3) of article 19;
"energy service provider" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of the Energy Efficiency Law;
"building energy efficiency certificate" means the certificate issued in accordance with the provisions of article 6 and includes the energy efficiency of the building or building unit, calculated according to the building energy efficiency calculation methodology;
"Town Planning Authority" has the meaning assigned to this term by the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Law;
“primary energy” means energy from renewable and non-renewable sources that has not undergone conversion or transformation;
"radical renovation" [Deleted];
"trigger point in the life cycle of the building" means an appropriate time in the life cycle of a building in terms of, for example, the cost-effectiveness or disruption of the smooth operation of the building, to carry out renovations aimed at improving energy efficiency;
"recharging point" has the meaning assigned to that term by the provisions of the Promotion and Development of Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Law;
“building element” means the technical building system or element of the building envelope;
"energy efficiency contract" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of the Energy Efficiency Law;
"Advisory Committee on Methodology for Calculation of Energy Performance of Buildings and Determination of Minimum Energy Performance Requirements for Buildings" [Deleted];
"Advisory Committee for Monitoring the Implementation of Directive 2002/91/EC" [Deleted];
"Advisory Committee for the Promotion of Energy Saving in Buildings and the Promotion of Buildings with Almost Zero Energy Consumption" means the Advisory Committee established by virtue of subsection (1) of article 16;
"cogeneration" means the simultaneous production of thermal energy and electrical and/or mechanical energy in the context of a process;
"building automation and control system" means a system that includes all products, software and mechanical services that can support the energy-efficient, economical, and safe operation of technical building systems through automatic controls and facilitating the manual management of said technical building systems;
"heating system" means the combination of elements required to treat indoor air, by means of which the temperature is increased;
“air conditioning system” means the combination of elements required for the treatment of indoor air, by means of which the temperature is controlled or can be reduced;
"frequently" means the ability to enter the building, whether controlled or not, on a permanent and systematic basis for the purposes of obtaining services, purchasing or receiving products, information and information;
"technical building system" means the technical equipment of a building or building unit for space heating and cooling, ventilation, domestic hot water production, integrated lighting installation, building automation and control, on-site electricity generation or a combination of such systems, including systems using energy from renewable sources;
"district heating" or "district cooling" means the distribution of heat energy in the form of steam, hot water or cold liquids, from a central point of production through a network to multiple buildings or locations, to meet the heating or cooling needs of premises or processes;
"wiring infrastructure" means conduits for the passage of electric cables, in order to enable at a later stage the installation of recharging points for electric vehicles;
"building energy performance calculations" means the calculations carried out to determine the energy performance of a building or building unit, in accordance with the building energy performance calculation methodology;
"Minister" means the Minister for Energy, Trade and Industry;
"rated net power expressed in kilowatts (kW)" [Deleted].
(2) Any other terms that are used in this Law and are not interpreted differently therein, have the meaning attributed to these terms by Regulation (EU) no. 244/2012.
(3) In this Law and the regulatory administrative acts pursuant thereto, a reference to a Regulation, Directive, Decision or other act of the European Union, means the act in question, as it is corrected, amended or replaced from time to time.

## Minimum building energy performance requirements and building energy performance calculations
4.-(1) Every new building or building unit meets the minimum energy efficiency requirements, as defined in the decree on minimum energy efficiency building requirements.
(2) (a) For each building or building unit that undergoes a large-scale renovation, the energy efficiency of the building or its renovated part is upgraded to meet the minimum energy efficiency requirements, as specified in the ordinance on minimum energy efficiency requirements for buildings.
(b) For the purpose of dealing with issues related to strong seismic activity prior to large-scale renovation, the owner of a building or building unit whose building permit was issued before the year 1994 appoints a licensed surveyor who prepares a valuation report of the bearing organization, in accordance with the current Eurocodes regarding the condition of the static structure of the building and its estimated remaining life, which is accompanied from any recommendations regarding its structural enhancement.
(3) Every building element that constitutes part of the shell and is part of an addition to an existing building meets the minimum building energy efficiency requirements, as defined in the ordinance on minimum building energy efficiency requirements.
(4) The competent authority prepares a software program, available free of charge, based on which the building energy efficiency calculations are made and the results are presented, as specified in the building energy efficiency calculation methodology decree.
(5) The provisions of subsections (1), (2), (3) and (6) do not apply to buildings that fall under the categories of buildings specified in Annex II and to buildings that have been declared as preserved buildings, in accordance with the provisions of the Preserved Buildings Law, or have been declared as ancient monuments, in accordance with the provisions of the Antiquities Law, where the observance of the minimum energy efficiency requirements alters their character or appearance in an unacceptable manner, provided that the owner submits to the Director of the Department of Urban Planning and Housing or the Director of the Department of Antiquities or the Competent Local Authority, where applicable, all of the energy upgrading interventions and/or modifications of the building and sufficient documentation of the exceptions.
(6) Any building element that is part of the building envelope and has a significant impact on the energy efficiency of the envelope, when retrofitted or replaced, the energy efficiency of the building element meets the minimum energy efficiency requirements as specified in the building energy efficiency requirements ordinance.
(7) For the purposes of determining the cost-optimal levels of the minimum energy efficiency requirements of buildings or building units, the comparative methodological framework of Annex I is applied, in accordance with the calculation of the optimal cost levels of the minimum energy efficiency requirements referred to in Annex I of Regulation (EU) no. 244/2012.
(8) For the purposes of implementing subsections (1) to (3), a qualified expert prepares building energy efficiency calculations and submits them to the competent authority, if requested.
(9) Building energy efficiency calculations are prepared according to the building energy efficiency calculation methodology.
(10) The competent authority, after a justified request, examines the compatibility of other software with the building energy efficiency calculation methodology:
Provided that the compatibility check procedure is determined by the competent authority and any costs resulting from the procedure are borne by the applicant.

## Buildings with almost zero energy consumption
5A-(1) For the purposes of implementing the provisions of this Law, until 31 December 2020 all new buildings are buildings with almost zero energy consumption while, from 1 January 2019, all new buildings housing public authorities or owned by them must be buildings with almost zero energy consumption.
(2) The competent authority may decide not to apply the requirements of subsection (1) in special and justified cases, in which the analysis of the cost-benefit relationship for the economic life cycle of the specific building has a negative result.
(3) The competent authority draws up a national plan to increase the number of buildings with almost zero energy consumption which may include, among others:
(a) Targets differentiated by building category and intermediate targets for improving the energy performance of new buildings;
(b) details regarding the practical application of the definition of near-zero energy buildings, including the numerical index of primary energy use in kWh/m2 per year;
(c) information on policies and financial or other measures contributing to increasing the number of nearly zero-energy buildings, including details of requirements and measures for the use of energy from renewable sources in new buildings and buildings undergoing large-scale renovation.
(4) The national plan to increase the number of buildings with almost zero energy consumption may be revised at regular intervals to reflect technical progress in the construction sector.

## Long-term renovation strategy
5B.-(1) The competent authority shall establish a long-term strategy for the renovation of the national building potential, public and private, intended for residential or other uses and its transformation into a high-energy-efficient and carbon-free building potential by the year 2050, hereinafter referred to as the "long-term renovation strategy", facilitating the cost-effective conversion of existing buildings into buildings with almost zero consumption energy, which includes the following:
(a) An overview of the national building potential based, where appropriate, on statistical sampling and the expected percentage of renovated buildings in the year 2020;
(b) identifying cost-effective approaches to renovations according to building type and climate zone, taking into account possible appropriate trigger points in the life cycle of the building, as appropriate;
(c) policies and actions to stimulate cost-effective renovation of buildings, including incremental renovation, and to support targeted cost-effective measures and renovations, such as the introduction of an optional building renovation passport system;
(d) an overview of policies and actions concerning the worst-performing parts of the national building stock, conflicting incentive dilemmas and market failures, and a description of national actions that contribute to alleviating energy poverty;
(e) policies and actions concerning all public buildings,
(f) an overview of national initiatives to promote smart technologies and well-connected buildings and communities, as well as improving skills and education in the construction and energy efficiency sectors, and
(g) documented assessment of expected energy savings and overall benefit, including in relation to health, safety and air quality.
(2) The long-term renovation strategy presents a roadmap with measures and measurable national progress indicators, with a view to the long-term 2050 objective of reducing greenhouse gas emissions in the Union by 80-95% compared to the year 1990, in order to ensure a highly energy-efficient and carbon-free national building stock and to facilitate the cost-effective conversion of existing of buildings in buildings with almost zero energy consumption, and the roadmap includes indicative milestones for the years 2030, 2040 and 2050 and identifies the way in which these milestones contribute to the achievement of the Union's energy efficiency targets in accordance with the provisions of Directive 2012/27/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 October 2012 on energy efficiency, amending the directives 2009/125/EC and 2010/30/EC and the repeal of directives 2004/8/EC and 2006/32/EC.
(3) The competent authority, in order to support the mobilization of investments for the renovations necessary to achieve the objectives of subsection (1), facilitates access to the appropriate mechanisms that allow-
(a) the grouping of projects, such as through investment platforms or groups and through consortia of small and medium-sized enterprises, in order to enable the access of investors and the offer of combined solutions to potential clients;
(b) reducing the perceived risks of energy efficiency activities for investors and the private sector;
(c) using public funding to attract private investment and address specific market failures;
(d) the orientation of investments towards an energy-efficient building potential, in accordance with Eurostat guidelines, and
(e) accessible and transparent advisory tools, such as one-stop services for consumers and energy advisory services, regarding appropriate energy-efficient renovations and appropriate financing instruments.
(4) In order to support the development of the long-term renewal strategy, the competent authority shall carry out a public consultation on the said strategy, before submitting it to the Commission, attach a summary of the results of the public consultation as an annex to the long-term national renewal strategy and determine the practical details regarding consultations with all stakeholders without exception during the implementation of the long-term renewal strategy.
(5) The competent authority shall record in an annex to the long-term renovation strategy the details of the implementation of the most recent long-term renovation strategy, including the planned policies and actions.
(6) The long-term renovation strategy can be used to address the issue of fire safety and the risks associated with strong seismic activity or fires affecting both energy renovations and the lifetime of buildings.
(7) The first long-term renovation strategy is submitted to the European Commission by 10h March 2020 and then submitted under the final consolidated energy and climate plan referred to in the provisions of Article 32 of Regulation (EU) 2018/1999 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 December 2018 on the governance of the Energy Union and Climate Action, amending Regulations (EC) No. 663/2009 and (EC) no. 715/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council, of directives 94/22/EC, 98/70/EC, 2009/31/EC, 2009/73/EC, 2010/31/EU, 2012/27/EU and 2013/30/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council, of directives 2009/119/EC and (EU) 2015/652 of the Council and for the repeal of Regulation (EU) no. 525/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council.

## Financial incentives and other instruments for the energy performance of buildings
5C.-(1) The competent authority shall duly ensure consideration of the provision of appropriate financial incentives and other means with a catalytic effect on the energy efficiency of buildings and the transition to buildings with almost zero energy consumption.(2) Financial measures for energy efficiency improvements during building renovation are required to be linked to targeted or achieved energy savings, as determined by one or more of the following criteria:
(a) The energy efficiency of the equipment or material used for the renovation, where the equipment or material used for the renovation must be installed by an installer with an appropriate level of certification or qualification,
(b) standard values for calculating energy savings in buildings;
(c) the improvement achieved due to the renovation by comparing the energy performance certificates issued for the building or building unit before and after the renovation,
(d) the energy audit results,
(e) the results of another relevant transparent and proportionate method demonstrating the improvement of energy efficiency.

## Building energy performance certificate
6.-(1) The owner of the building or building unit shall ensure the issuance of a building energy efficiency certificate-
(a) for buildings or building units under construction;
(b) for buildings or building units offered for sale and/or sold;
(c) for buildings or building units available for lease and/or leased to a new tenant, and
(d) for buildings in which the total useful area of more than two hundred and fifty square meters is used by a public authority and which are frequently visited by the public.
(2) During the construction, sale or lease of a building or building unit, a building energy performance certificate or a copy thereof is shown to the prospective tenant or buyer and delivered to the buyer or tenant.
(3) The building energy performance certificate includes the building's energy performance and reference values, such as the minimum building energy performance requirements, to enable the building or building unit owners or tenants to compare and evaluate the building or building unit's energy performance.
(4)(a) The building energy performance certificate shall also include recommendations for the most cost-optimal or economically advantageous improvement in the energy performance of the building or building unit, unless there is no reasonable possibility of such improvement compared to the applicable energy performance requirements;
(b) the recommendations referred to above cover–
(i) the measures taken in relation to the large-scale renovation of the building envelope or its technical systems; and
(ii) the measures for individual building elements regardless of the large-scale renovation of the building envelope or its technical systems.
(5) these recommendations are technically feasible for the specific building and can lead to an estimate regarding the range of payback periods or the cost-benefit ratio for its entire economic life cycle.
(6)(a) The building energy performance certificate shall provide an indication of how the owner or tenant may obtain more detailed information, including cost-effectiveness, about the recommendations contained in the energy performance certificate;
(b) the cost-benefit assessment is based on a number of standard assumptions, such as the assessment of energy savings and baseline energy prices and preliminary cost estimation. In addition, it includes information on the steps to be taken to implement the recommendations;
(c) the owner or tenant may be provided with other information on related matters such as energy audits, heating system inspections and air conditioning system inspections or financial or other incentives and financing possibilities.
(7) The process of issuing the building energy performance certificate is carried out in an independent manner and is issued by qualified experts.
(8) The building energy efficiency certificate is valid for a maximum of ten years.
(9) The competent authority keeps a register of energy performance certificates for buildings.
(10) Databases for energy performance certificates allow the collection of data on the measured or calculated energy consumption of specific buildings, including at least public buildings for which an energy performance certificate has been issued, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1).
(11) Upon request to the competent authority, at least aggregated and anonymous data may be made available for statistical and research purposes, in accordance with the Union and national data protection requirements, while the same data may also be made available to the owner of the building.

## Certification of detached houses
6A.- The issuance of the building energy efficiency certificate for detached houses may be based on the evaluation of another representative building of a similar design and size with a similar level of energy efficiency, in accordance with the methodology for calculating the energy efficiency of the building and as long as the qualified expert who issues the building energy efficiency certificate guarantees said correspondence.

## Certification of building units
7. The issuance of a building energy efficiency certificate for building units may be based, in accordance with the building energy efficiency calculation methodology –
(a) in the common certification of the whole building, or
(b) in the evaluation of another representative building unit of the same building complex, which has the same energy characteristics.

## Posting of a building's energy efficiency certificate in a prominent place, visible to the public
9.-(1) In buildings with a total useful surface of more than two hundred and fifty square meters for which a building energy efficiency certificate has been issued in accordance with paragraph (d) of subsection (1) of article 6, the building energy efficiency certificate is posted in a prominent place, visible to the public.

(4) For the purposes of applying subsection (1) and since the public authorities are housed in existing buildings, the energy efficiency calculations and the building energy efficiency certificate issued may be based on the actual annual energy consumed to meet the various needs associated with the normal use of the specific building.

## Technical building systems
9A.-(1) New technical building systems that are installed in existing buildings or building units, technical building systems that are replaced in buildings or building units, as well as existing technical building systems that are upgraded must meet requirements regarding overall energy efficiency, their correct installation, correct dimensioning, regulation and control, in order to optimize the energy use of technical building systems:
Provided that, the requirements regarding technical systems are applied to the extent that this is technically, operationally and economically feasible.
(2) All new buildings are equipped with self-regulating systems for the autonomous regulation of the temperature in each room or where justified, in a designated heated zone of the building unit, while in existing buildings the installation of such self-regulating devices is required when the heat generating unit is replaced, provided that their cost is lower than ten percent (10%) of the total cost of the replaced heat generating units and provided that no substantial modifications to the technical systems are required.
(3) Installation of technical building systems is done by installers of technical building systems.
(4) During the study and design of the technical systems described in subsection (1), provision is made for safe access, for purposes of installation and/or regulation and/or control and/or testing and/or maintenance and/or inspection of the equipment of these systems through permanent suitable means of access.
(5) When a technical building system is installed, replaced or upgraded, the overall energy efficiency of the changing part and, where appropriate, of the overall system is required to be assessed and in such case the results are documented and transmitted by the installer of the technical building system to the owner of the building, so that they remain available and can be used to verify compliance with the minimum requirements determined by the provisions of subsection (1) and to issue certificates energy performance, subject to article 6 and the provisions of the Regulation of the Energy Performance of Buildings (Energy Certification of Buildings) Regulations.

## High efficiency alternative energy production systems
9C.-(1) For each new building, the technical, environmental and economic feasibility of installing the available alternative high-efficiency energy production systems is studied and taken into account before the start of construction.
(2) The installation study of alternative high-efficiency energy production systems may be carried out for a building or for groups of similar buildings or for common types of buildings in the same area.
(3) The qualified expert makes sure to keep the study referred to in subsection (1) and make it available to the competent authority, when it requests it.
(4) The competent authority shall take measures to promote alternative high-efficiency energy production systems in buildings undergoing large-scale renovation, to the extent that this is technically, operationally and economically feasible.

## Electrification
9D.-(1) In new non-residential buildings, as well as in non-residential buildings undergoing large-scale renovation with more than ten car parking spaces, at least one recharging point must be installed, within the meaning of Directive 2014/94/EU, as well as cabling infrastructure, i.e. electrical cable conduits for at least one parking space in five, in order to enable the installation of points at a later stage recharging for electric vehicles when-
(a) the car park is within the building and, in the case of large-scale renovations, the renovation measures include the renovation of the car parks or the electrical infrastructure of the building; or
(b) the car park is located on an adjacent site and, in the case of large-scale renovations, the renovation measures include the renovation of the car park or the electrical infrastructure of the car park.
(2) Until 1h January 2025 all non-residential buildings with more than twenty (20) parking spaces must install at least one recharging point.
(3) In buildings used as residences, with more than two car parking spaces, whether new buildings or buildings undergoing large-scale renovation, the wiring infrastructure, i.e. electrical cable conduits for each parking space, must be installed in order to enable the installation of recharging points for electric vehicles at a later stage, when-
(4) In the case of buildings part of which is not used as a residence and part of which is used as a residence, the provisions of subsections (1) and (2) apply to the part of the building that is not used as a residence and the provisions of subsection (3) to the part of the building that is used as a residence.

A comparative methodology framework for setting cost-optimal levels of energy performance requirements for buildings and building components.
1. The benchmarking methodology framework allows the competent authority to determine the energy performance of buildings and building components and the economic aspects of energy performance-related measures and to link them together, with the aim of determining the cost-optimal level.

It should be based on the relevant European standards linked to Directive 2010/31/EU.

- to define the energy performance measures to be assessed for the reference buildings; these may be measures for individual buildings as a whole, for individual structural elements or for a combination of structural elements,

- to calculate the cost (e.g. the net present value) of the energy efficiency measures (as mentioned in the second case) during the expected economic life cycle applied to the reference buildings (as mentioned in the first case), by applying the principles of the comparative methodology framework.

Appendix IV
Common general framework for calculating the energy performance of buildings
[section 2(1) and 14(1)]
A. 1. The energy efficiency of a building is determined based on the calculated or actual energy use and reflects the usual energy consumed by the building for space heating, space cooling, hot water for domestic use, ventilation, integrated lighting installation and other technical systems of the building.
Building energy efficiency is expressed as a numerical index of primary energy use in kWh/(m².y), with the aim of both energy efficiency certification and compliance with minimum energy efficiency requirements, and the methodology applied to determine building energy efficiency is transparent and open to innovation.
The competent authority describes the methodology for calculating building energy performance according to the national annexes of the general standards, i.e. ISO 52000-1, 52003-1, 52010-1, 52016-1, and 52018-1, which have been prepared by the European Committee for Standardization (CEN), in the context of the M/480 order, and this provision does not constitutes a legal codification of said standards.
2. The energy needs for space heating, space cooling, domestic hot water, lighting, ventilation and other technical systems of the building are calculated in a way to ensure optimal levels of hygiene, indoor air quality and comfort.
The calculation of primary energy is based on primary energy factors or weighting factors by energy carrier, which may be based on national, regional or local annual, and possibly also seasonal or monthly, weighted averages, or on more specific information available for individual urban systems.
Primary energy factors or weighting factors are determined by the competent authority and, when applying these to calculate energy performance, it must be ensured that the optimum energy performance of the building envelope is used.
When determining the primary energy coefficients for the purpose of calculating the energy efficiency of buildings, the energy from renewable sources offered by the energy operator and the energy from renewable sources produced and used on site may be taken into account, provided that this is applied indiscriminately.
3. The methodology is determined taking into account at least the following factors:
(a) the following actual thermal characteristics of the building (including its internal partitions):
(i) heat capacity;
(ii) insulation;
(iii) passive heating;
(iv) cooling elements; and
(v) thermal bridging;
(b) heating installation and hot water supply, including their insulation characteristics;
(c) air conditioning facilities;
(d) natural and mechanical ventilation, which may include airtightness;
(e) integrated lighting installation (mainly in the non-residential sector);
(f) design, location and orientation of the building, including external climatic conditions;
(g) passive solar systems and solar protection;
(h) indoor climate conditions including intended indoor climate conditions.
(i) internal loads.
4. The positive effect of the following is taken into account:
(a) local sun exposure conditions, active solar systems and other heating and electricity systems based on renewable energy sources;
(b) electricity produced by cogeneration;
(c) district or block scale district heating and cooling systems;
(d) natural lighting.
5. For the purpose of this calculation, buildings are classified into the following categories:
(a) detached houses of various types;
(b) apartment complexes;
(c) offices;
(d) educational buildings;
(e) hospitals;
(f) hotels and restaurants;
(g) sports facilities;
