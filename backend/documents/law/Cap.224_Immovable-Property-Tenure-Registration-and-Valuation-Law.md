## The Immovable Property (Possession, Registration and Valuation) Law (CHAP. 224)
BECAUSE with his Decision, No. 82.881 dated 20.6.2017, the Council of Ministers decided to authorize the Minister of Finance to promote the transfer of the responsibilities of the Land Surveying Service to the structures of the Department of Cadastre and Land Surveying, as a separate and specialized Branch.
AND BECAUSE the Council of Ministers, by the same decision, decided to authorize the Minister of the Interior to promote the necessary amendments to the relevant legislation, so that all the powers and responsibilities granted to the Director of the Revival Service and which derive from it are transferred to the Director of the Department of Cadastre and Land Surveying,
The House of Representatives votes as follows:

## Short title
1. This Law shall be referred to as the Immovable Property (Possession, Registration and Valuation) Law.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Interpretation
2. In this Law-
"licensed credit institution" has the meaning assigned to this term by article 2 of the Credit Institutions Operations Law.
"immovable property" includes-
(a) land;
(b) buildings and other structures, structures or appurtenances which are firmly attached to any land or to any building or other structure or structure;
(c) trees, vines and any other thing planted or growing on any land and any fruits thereof before the alienation;
(d) springs, wells, water and rights to water whether held with or independently of any land;
(e) privileges, liberties, easements and any other rights and advantages belonging, or deemed to belong, to any land or any building or other structure or building
(f) undivided ideal share of ownership set forth above;
(g) land created after inundation of the sea;
(h) only for the purposes of registration of immovable property under this Law or for the purposes of the provisions of Part IV of this Law or for the purposes of the Transfer and Mortgage of Immovables Law, maritime space, which is declared-
(i) as a marina pursuant to the provisions of the Marine Regulation Law, or
(ii) as a place for mooring and servicing of pleasure boats pursuant to the provisions of the Law on the Management of Places of Mooring for Pleasure Boats,
which is to be filled to create a dry land, as this area appears in a land plan prepared by the Director;
"restructuring" has the meaning assigned to this term by article 2 of the Land and Spatial Department (Fees and Rights) Law.
"value", in relation to immovable property, means the amount which the immovable property could be expected to fetch if sold in the open market by a willing seller to a willing buyer;
"assessed value" in relation to immovable property means the amount resulting from a general assessment or reassessment or revision of an assessment which is as near as possible to the value;
"public road" means any street, square, footpath, open space or area over which the public has a right of way and includes any land set aside by, or with the consent of, the competent authority as a public road;
"Director" means the Director of the Land Surveying Department and includes any officer appointed by the Director for all or any of the purposes of this Act either generally or for any particular purpose;
"the Court", in relation to any matter relating to immovable property, means the District Court of the province in which such property is situated;
"registered", together with the grammatical variations and related expressions of this term, means registered in the Land Registry maintained pursuant to the provisions of this Law, whether it is computerized or handwritten, and the date of registration is considered the date on which the entry precedes in time in any of the two Registries;
"Provincial Land Office", for matters relating to immovable property, means the Provincial Land Office of the Province in which the immovable property is situated or any other office or place which the Director may, by notice in the Official Gazette of the Republic, designate for all or some of the provisions of this Law and may include any branch of the Provincial Land Office operating in that province:
It is understood that only such operations are carried out in an annex as authorized by the Director.
"credit acquisition company" has the meaning assigned to this term by article 2 of the Credit Facility Trading and Related Matters Law.
"hostile possession", together with the grammatical variations and cognate expressions of that term, means possession by a person not entitled to it, when the express or implied consent or permission of the person so entitled to such possession has not been given or obtained;
"religious body" includes a religious establishment or religious institution of any denomination and any throne, church, chapel, monastery, mosque, tekke, place of worship or synagogue;
"Guardian" has the meaning assigned to that term in section 2 of the Turkish Cypriot Property (Administration and Other Matters) (Temporary Provisions) Law 1991;
"Real Estate Registry" means the Registry maintained pursuant to the provisions of article 51 of this Law and includes a computerized Registry;
"government settlement" has the meaning assigned to that term by the Provision of Housing Assistance to Displaced Persons, Ill Persons and Other Persons Act.
"owner" means the person entitled to be registered as the owner of any immovable property whether or not so registered;
"credit institution" has the meaning assigned to this term by the Credit Institutions Act, as amended or replaced from time to time;
"transaction", together with the grammatical variations and cognate expressions of that term, in relation to immovable property, means the voluntary transfer or mortgage of immovable property and any other voluntary encumbrance or act affecting immovable property which is required to be carried out in a District Land Office by any law for the time being in force;
"self-housing housing estate" means the area in which the Republic has set aside plots for the purposes of housing beneficiaries of housing assistance based on the Provision of Housing Assistance to Displaced Persons, Ill Persons and Other Persons Law.
"registration fee" means the fee payable in connection with the registration of a title to immovable property under any law for the time being in force.

## Abolition of immovable property categories under Ottoman Laws
3.-(1) The categories of immovable property hitherto known by virtue of the Ottoman Land Code as "Mulk", "Arazi Memluke", "Arazi Miriye", "Arazi Metruke", or "Arazi Mevat" are abolished and from now on every immovable property is owned, occupied and realized in compliance with the provisions of this Law or any other law that is from time to time in force and in accordance with these provisions.
(2) Any immovable property hitherto known as "Mulk" or "Arazi Memluke" and which belongs by ownership to a private individual as such on the date of entry into force of this Law shall continue to be owned, occupied and vested as private property.
(3) Any immovable property known as "Arazi Mirie" and which is owned by a private individual as such on the date of entry into force of this Law shall be owned, occupied and vested as private property.
(4) Any immovable property which, on the date of entry into force of this Law, is held, managed and vested as a Waqf property in accordance with the provisions of the Cyprus Evqaf (Management of Mohammedan Religious Property) Decree and Law of 1928 and 1934, continues to be held, managed and vested in this manner as if the This Law had not been established in compliance with the provisions of Articles 36, 37 and 38 of this Law.
(5) Any immovable property hitherto known as "Arazi Metruke" which is legally owned or communally owned by a city, village or parish on the date this Law comes into force continues to be owned or communally owned by that city, village or parish.
(6) Any immovable property hitherto known as "Arazi Mevat" and any immovable property other than that specified in subsections (2) to (5) and any free or unoccupied immovable property that is not owned by a private individual or that is not legally held on the date this Law comes into force shall be owned, occupied and vested by the Republic as property of the Republic.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## No right, etc., on immovable property except by virtue of this Law
4.-(1) Notwithstanding the provisions contained in paragraph (c) of subsection (1) of article 29 of the Law on Courts or any other law amending or replacing it, and subject to the provisions of this article, the law relating to trusts (trusts), the law relating to waqfs (vakfs) and the provisions of any other law from time to time in force, no right of ownership (estate), interest, right, privilege, liberty, servitude or any other advantage in, upon or over any real property shall not exist or be created, acquired or transferred except by virtue of the provisions of this Law.
(2) Subsection (1) shall not apply to any right of ownership (estate), interest, right, privilege, liberty, servitude or any other advantage in, upon or over any real property created, acquired or conveyed by deed and for consideration and existing on the 4th day of March in the year 1953:
Provided that either-
(a) such estate, interest, right, privilege, liberty, servitude or other advantage is registered or registered, or may be registered or registered, pursuant to another provision of this Law; or
(b) (i) the document referred to above is deposited in the District Land Office; and
(ii) the real property aforesaid was not transferred by virtue of sale to a bona fide purchaser for consideration after March 3, 1953, and prior to the filing of the document as provided in paragraph (i) otherwise or to a purchaser who after such transfer received consideration from the owner of such estate, interest, right, privilege, liberty, servitude or other advantage, as provided for in the aforementioned document.
(3) Upon the filing of a document as provided in paragraph (b)(i) of the proviso to subsection (2), the same, if the immovable property aforesaid is registered, shall be entered, in the books of the District Land Office and, when possible, in the certificate of registration relating to such immovable property, without inquiry as to the right of ownership (estate), interest, right, privilege, liberty, easement or advantage existing, created, acquired or transferred with it.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Extent of privately owned land
5.-(1) Private ownership of land extends, subject to the provisions of this Law or any other law from time to time in force, on the surface and the crust of the earth, under the surface and in the space above the surface, reasonably necessary for its fruition, but does not extend to minerals:
Provided that the private ownership of any land, which is land which is located in the areas specified for the purposes of this article in the spatial map signed by the Director and deposited in the Provincial Land Office before the commencement of this Law, extends over the crust of the earth below the surface without any limitation as mentioned above.
(2) For the purposes of this article-
"minerals" includes any material of economic value which is part of, or naturally derived from, the earth's crust including mineral oil, bitumen, bitumen and natural gas but not decaying minerals or peat, trees, timber and similar forest products.
(3) Nothing in the provisions contained in this article or in any spatial map deposited in the District Land Office under the proviso to subsection (1) shall diminish or extend any rights conferred by virtue of a mining lease granted by or on behalf of the Government under any law for the time being in force relating to mining and in force on the date of commencement of this Law; but the vesting of the rights shall continue of them, while this lease lasts, to the same extent as if this Law had not been enacted.

## Removed
6. Article 6 is repealed.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Lakes, etc. that are not under private ownership are within the Republic
7. Every lake, river, stream and natural aqueduct which is not under private ownership on the date of commencement of this Law and the basins, beds or channels thereof, and every land from which the sea or the water of any such lake, river, stream or aqueduct has receded, as well as any land which has come from the inundation of the sea, excluding any such land which is under private ownership on the date mentioned above, vests in the Republic:
Provided that none of the provisions contained in this article shall be construed as affecting any rights over any lake, river, stream or natural aqueduct which-
(a) have been practiced continuously for a full period of thirty years before the date aforesaid; or
(b) have been conferred by a firman or other valid instrument of title drawn up before the 4th June, 1878, which has had effect from the time of its drawing up; or
(c) are exercised pursuant to the provisions of any applicable law.

## Public roads and beach
8. Every public road and part of the beach which is not under private ownership on the date of entry into force of this Law shall be transferred to the Republic for use by the public.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## No title in hostile possession against the Republic or registered master
9. No title to immovable property shall be acquired by any person in hostile possession against the Republic or registered master.

## Title in hostile possession in certain cases
10. Subject to the provisions of article 9, proof of undisputed and uninterrupted hostile possession by a person, or by those from whom he receives the claim, of immovable property for the full period of thirty years, entitles that person to be considered as the owner of such property and as it is registered in his name:
Provided that none of the provisions contained in this article shall affect the period of usufruct in respect of any immovable property which began to be occupied by hostiles before the commencement of this Act, and all matters relating to usufruct during such period shall continue to be governed by the provisions of the enactments repealed by this Act relating to usufruct, as if this Act had not been enacted:
It is further provided that regardless of the existence of any incapacity which brings about by virtue of these laws the extension of the period of use, this period in any case shall not exceed a total of thirty years even when any such incapacity may continue to exist at the expiration of the thirty years.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## No adverse possession title to certain real property
10A. Notwithstanding the provisions of Article 10, no title shall be acquired in immovable property under hostile occupation-
(a) In immovable property located within an inaccessible area due to the Turkish Invasion against a master belonging to the Greek Community or a foreigner.
(b) In immovable property situated within the free area against a master belonging to the Turkish Community.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Rights of way, etc. over the immovable property of another
11.-(1) No right of way or any privilege, liberty, easement, or any other right or advantage shall be acquired over the immovable property of another except-
(a) by virtue of a concession by its owner duly registered in the books of the Provincial Land Registry; or
(b) where it has been exercised by any person or persons from whom he receives the claim for a full period of thirty continuous years;
It is understood that the provisions of this paragraph do not apply to any immovable property which is the property of the Republic or property which was included in the Republic; or
(c) where this was recognized by a decision of a competent Court; or
(d) where it was conferred by a firman or other valid instrument drawn up before the 4th June, 1878, which had effect from the time of its drawing up; or
(e) where this has been acquired under the provisions of Article 11A; or
(f) where it was created and acquired under the provisions of the Compulsory Expropriation Act 1962 or any law amending or replacing it; or
(g) where this has been reserved in writing by the owner of any immovable property by the transfer of such property;
Provided that the previous paragraph also applies in the event that any provision related to the use or development of any immovable property is included or with a restriction on the use or development thereof; or
(h) where this is created by a decision of the Director to ensure passage in an enclosed block, originating from division and distribution, in the context of the implementation of the provisions of subsections (1), (2) and (3) of article 29.
(2) No person shall exercise any right of way or any privilege, liberty, easement or any other right or advantage over the immovable property of another except when he-
(a) acquired as provided in subsection (1); or
(b) is exercised under the provisions of any law in force from time to time;
(c) is exercised pursuant to written permission from its owner.

## Obligation to provide passage
11A.-(1) Irrespective of the provisions of this Law, if immovable property due to any reason is enclosed in such a way as to be deprived of the necessary access to a public road, or if the existing access is insufficient for its proper use, development or exploitation, the owner of the immovable property is entitled to demand an access to neighboring immovable properties upon payment of reasonable compensation.
For the purposes of this subsection "passage" includes the right to convey water, sewage or any other liquid by means of grooves, or pipes or any other suitable means.
(2) The direction of the passage and the extent of the right to use it, as well as the compensation that must be paid, are determined by the Director after prior notification to all interested parties, and if, after this notification, any of the interested parties fails to attend during the on-site investigation defined in the notification, the Director may proceed with any required action in his absence.
(3) Neighbors' obligation to provide passage does not exist if the communication of the immovable property to the public road was stopped by a voluntary act or omission of its owner.
(4) If, due to the sale of a part of the immovable property, the communication of the part that was sold or remained to the public road was cut off, the owner of the part, through which the communication was until now, is obliged to provide passage. The sale of a part is equated with the sale of one of several immovable properties belonging to the same owner.
(5) A toll granted under this section shall be deemed to be a right, easement or benefit acquired under the provisions of section 11, and the provisions of this Act shall apply to any such toll.
(6) The Council of Ministers issues Regulations regulating every matter that needs regulation for the best implementation of this article and especially the procedure followed for its purposes:
Provided that Regulations made on the basis of this subsection are submitted to the House of Representatives, which within 15 days of this submission decides on them. In case of approval or amendment of the Regulations filed in this way, they shall enter into force as approved by the Parliament.
(7) The provisions of this article shall not apply to any type of state land without a special decision of the Council of Ministers and under such conditions as may be provided for in the decision.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Rights, etc., acquired or exercised in relation to the immovable property of another
12.-(1) When any right, privilege, liberty, servitude, or other advantage has been acquired as provided in subsection (1) of section 11 in respect of any immovable property, the same shall be deemed to attach to such property and to be included in any transaction carried on in relation to such property.
(2) When any such right, privilege, liberty, servitude or any other advantage has been abandoned by notice in writing to the District Land Office, or has not been exercised for a full period of thirty continuous years, it shall be deemed to have lapsed.
(3) When this right is a right of way, if due to the opening of a public road or other passage or if there is no longer a need for it, the owner of the working or dominating property is entitled to demand its abolition, and the Director investigates the case, decides whether this right should be abolished or not, notifies his decision to all interested parties and if his decision is that the right should repealed, shall proceed to repeal it after the lapse of thirty days from the date of the said notification:
Provided that, if any claim is made for the payment of any compensation by the owner of the working property for the abolition of the above right, the Director after taking into account the facts of each case, including the time of use of the right, shall determine the compensation that may have to be paid and shall notify the same to all concerned and shall not proceed with the abolition of the right before he is satisfied that the compensation assessed by him has been paid.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Calculation of the period of thirty years
13. The period of thirty years specified in articles 10, 11 and 12 shall be counted and deemed to be the period immediately preceding the date on which a claim may be brought under these articles.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Determination or modification of the right-of-way position, etc.
14.-(1) At the request of the owner of the dominant property or the owner of the working property, the Director may, after thirty days' notice to all interested parties, determine or modify the location or direction of any relevant right-of-way, passage, groove, ditch, stream, conduit, pipe, wire or other similar but in such a way that such determination or modification does not affect either of these properties less favorably than before.
(2) The Director may proceed with the determination or modification provided for in subsection (1) even if the owner of any of the two affected properties to whom the notification provided for in the above subsection was given fails to appear on the day and time specified in this notification.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Aqueducts and irrigation ditches
15.-(1) When any parcels of land belonging to several owners are irrigated by an aqueduct or ditch, any owner may apply to the Director for a change in the position or direction of such aqueduct or ditch in his parcel and the Director may, after giving thirty days' notice to any owner whose land will be affected by such change, change the location or direction thereof, but only in such manner so that the interests of any such other owner or the applicant are not materially affected or prejudiced thereby. If, after this notification, the person to whom the notification was given fails to appear, the Director may proceed with actions in his absence.
(2) When more than one person is entitled to water flowing through an aqueduct or channel, in order to prevent any waste of water-
(a) all such persons shall clean and keep such aqueduct or channel in proper repair and condition, and each such person shall be liable for the consequent expense in proportion to his share of the water; and
(b) each of these persons shall close the main opening of his own aqueduct or furrow as soon as his land is irrigated or as soon as the time during which he is entitled to use the water expires.
(3) If any person omits or neglects to comply with the provisions of sub-section (2) any other person or persons having an interest in the matter may take all such steps as are reasonably necessary to prevent waste and may also recover the amount for which the person in default may be liable, by civil action.
(4) The provisions of this article do not apply to irrigation departments established pursuant to the Irrigation Departments (Villages) Law.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Wells
15A. When more than one person is entitled to water drawn from a well, all such persons are obliged to clean, properly repair and keep in good condition that well, and each of these persons is responsible for the resulting expenses in proportion to his share.
For the purposes of this article the word "well" has the meaning given by article 2 of the Law on Wells.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Area of irrigation right
16. When any person acquires or holds any irrigation right, such right shall extend to a right in or to any artificial or other ditch, aqueduct, aqueduct, well or chain of wells made for the distribution of the water to which such right relates and a space not exceeding five feet from each of the two ends of such furrow, aqueduct, aqueduct, well or chain of wells which may be required to clean, repair, or protect the same shall be deemed to be a part thereof, and the owner of the land on either side of the furrow, aqueduct, aqueduct, well, or chain of wells shall not interfere with and cultivate or plant such space.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Plantations along public roads
17.-(1) No person shall plant any tree, bush or cane on any immovable property adjoining a public road at a distance of less than five feet from the boundary of such property along the public road without first securing a license from such person as may be appointed by the Cabinet for the purpose (hereinafter in this article referred to as "the authority").
(2) The Government may plant any tree, bush or cane on any real property adjoining a public road within five feet of the boundary of such property along the public road notwithstanding the fact that such property belongs to another person and any tree, bush or cane so planted shall be the property of the Republic.
All trees, bushes, or reeds planted in this manner before the coming into force of this Law by or under the authority of the Government shall be considered as legally planted under the provisions of this Law and constituting the property of the Republic.
(3) When the land on which a tree, bush or reed has been planted pursuant to the provisions of subsection (2) is later required for building or irrigation purposes and this tree, bush or reed obstructs the reasonable use of the land for this purpose, the authority, at the request of the owner of the land, must move it at the expense of the Government.
(4) A tree, shrub or cane planted after the entry into force of this Law in violation of subsection (1) may be moved at the expense of the person who acted in violation of this subsection.
(5) Where the authority is satisfied that any tree, bush or cane planted within the limits specified in sub-section (1) before the commencement of this Act causes damage or undue obstruction to the reasonable use of any public road or to the safety of any person traveling along any such road, he may, after giving thirty days' notice to the owner thereof, remove it at the expense of the Government.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Power of the Council of Ministers to exchange property of the Republic, etc.
18.-(1) The Council of Ministers may, under such conditions, limitations, conditions and criteria, which they wanted to be determined by Regulations-
(a) Grant, lease, exchange or otherwise alienate any property of the Republic or immovable property vested in the Republic under the provisions of this Law, other than a public road or the beach;
(b) exchange or alienate part of any public road, if it is satisfied that another adequate public road has been provided in its place, or that such exchange or alienation will improve that public road;
(c) to lease part of the beach for the purposes of ports, marinas, berthing and service areas for pleasure boats, piers, wharves, wharves, fish farms and for any other purpose of public benefit.
(1A) Irrespective of the provisions of this Law or any other Law or the Regulations issued pursuant thereto, a marine area which has been registered as immovable property pursuant to paragraph (h) of article 2 of this Law and has been declared as a place for mooring and servicing pleasure boats pursuant to the provisions of the Management of Places for Pleasure Boats Law, may not be alienated, except to be leased for development purposes, and to the said property the free access of the citizens is ensured.
(2) Within three months of the implementation by the Council of Ministers of any acts pursuant to subsection (1) of this article, a report shall be submitted to the House of Representatives detailing the cases in which these concessions were made.
(3) Regardless of the provisions of subsection (1), the Council of Ministers shall not grant or alienate immovable property of the Republic or immovable property that has come to the Republic under the provisions of any law for residential purposes, except only for the following cases:
(a) housing of persons who are considered beneficiaries based on the provisions of the Provision of Housing Assistance to Displaced Persons, Patients and Other Legal Persons and the heirs of the beneficiaries who died on or after April 7, 2006 in the case of a residential unit in a Government Settlement and after July 20, 1974 in the case of a residential unit in a Settlement Self-housing or other relatives who lived or co-habited in the same housing unit as the deceased in question and in accordance with such terms, limitations, conditions and criteria for this purpose, with Regulations issued pursuant to this article and which may have retroactive effect:

(b) housing displaced persons and/or families benefiting from a Government Plan which is drawn up and implemented for the purpose of rebuilding apartment buildings within Government Settlements, replacing apartment buildings that had been erected within these Government Settlements and which are declared by the relevant authority to be demolished and/or housing families benefiting from the implementation Government Plan based on income criteria:
Provided that, for the purposes of this subsection, the term "Government Plan" means a housing policy plan announced by the government of the Republic.

## Community property provisions
19. Where by law or custom immovable property (which in this Act shall be referred to as "the community property") is held or vested in community by any town, village or parish, the following provisions shall apply, namely-
(a) The inhabitants of such town, village or parish are jointly and severally entitled to the exclusion of any person who is not a resident thereof, to possess or enjoy the common property subject to any conditions under which the common property is held or vested by law or custom;
(b) no person owns or acquires at any time an individual or exclusive right over the community property or any part thereof -
(c) where the community property or any part thereof is required for any of the following purposes, namely-
(i) the creation of a village or parish;
(ii) land improvement;
(iii) soil conservation;
(iv) mining exploitation
(n) public service project;
the Council of Ministers may, by notification in the Official Gazette of the Republic, declare that this property or part of it will cease to be community property:
Provided that in any such case property of the Republic of equal benefit as the community property, if available, shall be assigned to replace it or, if no property of the Republic is available, an amount equal to the value of the community property, as determined by the Director, shall be granted and made available for the benefit of such city, village or parish.
(d) when by law or custom the community property is occupied or appropriated by any town, village or parish for a specific use, the Council of Ministers may, on the application of at least two-thirds of the residents of that town, village or parish, who have completed the eighteenth year of their age, and after being satisfied that such use is no longer in the interest of that town, village or parish, terminate such use and either substitute it for another use or dispose of the property in such manner as he saw fit:
Provided that any such usufruct or the proceeds of any such disposition shall be or be disposed of for the benefit of such town, village or parish.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Cultivating land without the consent of the Republic or the registered owner
20. When a person cultivates land belonging to the Republic or to a person registered for that purpose without the written consent of the Republic or that person, as the case may be, any product thereof or any profit thereof shall become the property of the Republic or that person, as the case may be, without payment of any compensation.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Structures, etc., on real property held in undivided ideal shares
21. When immovable property is held in undivided ideal shares, all co-owners are entitled to their corresponding share in proportion, in-
(a) any building or other structure or structure erected on, or firmly attached to, the property;
(b) any tree or vine planted or well opened therein.
(c) any permanent improvement effected thereon;
regardless of whether it was erected, attached, planted, opened, induced by conjuncture or any other person.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Imposition of fees on immovable property held in undivided ideal portions
21A.-(1) Regardless of the provisions of this Law, for the purposes of imposing fees pursuant to the provisions of the Law on Municipalities, the Law on Communities and the Law on Sewerage Systems, as they are amended or replaced from time to time, in the event that immovable property is held in undivided ideal shares, it is considered to belong to its owners in proportion to their physical shares of ownership over it.
(2) For the purposes of subsection (1) -
(a) any co-owner may submit a written application to the Director for the determination of the general appraised value of the share attributable to him.

## Ownership of condominiums owned by units and limited condominiums

(a) The share in the jointly owned property belonging to the unit without express reference to that share,
(b) any limited common property granted exclusively to that unit for exclusive use without express reference to it;

## Share in jointly owned property
38Th. For the calculation of the share ratio, fractions smaller than one centimeter are not taken into account:

(2) No decision to divide a jointly owned building pursuant to subsection (1) is valid, unless it is approved by the competent authority.

A unit owner who omits or neglects to comply with his obligations under this article, is obliged, in addition to any other obligation or liability imposed on him by or by virtue of this Part or the Regulations, to compensate the other unit owners, if due to his negligence or omission the disruption of the free, uninterrupted and full use of these units or the jointly owned property is prolonged.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Total destruction of a jointly owned building
38IE.-(1) The jointly owned building is considered totally destroyed, when the owners of one hundred percent (100%) of the units of the jointly owned property so decide, in a general meeting convened for this purpose.
(2) In the event that a decision is taken, pursuant to subsection (1), according to which the jointly owned building is considered to be totally damaged, the owners of the units will decide, on the measures to be taken, in a general meeting that will be convened specifically for this purpose. The decision must be taken unanimously.
(3) If the owners of the units do not agree under this article about-
(a) Whether the jointly owned building will be considered totally destroyed by virtue of subsection (1); or
(b) for the measures to be taken under subsection (2),
an application may be made to the Court for a decision on the above matters and the Court has jurisdiction to make a decision on the matters in dispute.
(4) The Court will make a decision that the condominium is totally damaged if it is satisfied that, having regard to the rights and interests of the owners of the units as a whole, it is fair to consider the condominium to be totally damaged.
(5) If the Court makes an order that the jointly owned building is totally damaged or if it makes an order in respect of measures to be taken under subsection (2), the Court may by order impose such conditions and give such directions, including directions for the payment of sums of money, as it thinks fit for the equitable apportionment of the consequences of the order among all the owners of the units.
(6) If the Court issues a decision that the jointly owned building is not totally damaged it may give any instructions it deems appropriate, including instructions for the rebuilding or restoration, in whole or in part, of the jointly owned building. In the exercise of the powers conferred on it by this subsection, the Court may make such orders as it thinks necessary or expedient for carrying out its directions, including orders by which-
(a) It is ordered that the insurance money in respect of damage to the jointly owned building received by the Management Committee be made available;
(b) sums of money are ordered to be paid by the owners of the units or one or some of them;
(c) impose such conditions as the Court thinks fit.
(7) For the purposes of this article, the application may be submitted to the Court by the Administrative Committee or by any main unit or any person who has a registered encumbrance or right.
(8) In the event of an application being submitted to the Court pursuant to this article, any licensed insurer who has taken out insurance in relation to the jointly owned building has the right to appear before the Court.
(9) The Court may amend any decree it has issued pursuant to this article.
(10) On an application under this section, the Court may make such order for the payment of costs as it thinks fit.

## Settlement of border disputes
58.-(1) When any dispute arises as to the boundary of any registered land, the Director shall, in the first instance, settle such dispute, after notice having been given to the parties at least fourteen days previously, informing them of the time at which the disputed boundary shall be surveyed, and no Court shall entertain any suit or other proceeding in respect of such dispute, unless it is settled in the first instance stage as provided for in this article.

(3) As soon as the Director has decided on a boundary dispute, he shall give notice to the parties involved in the dispute of his decision and place such landmarks as he thinks fit to indicate the boundary line as decided by him and make such measurements and notes as may be required to ascertain the position of the landmarks.

Provided further that, in the case of a contract of lease of Government immovable property between the Government, as owner, lessor, and a displaced person, as lessee, for purposes of housing or self-housing, who was alive on or after 7h April 2006 and the lease relates to a residential unit built in a government settlement, and which was in existence after 20 July 1974 and the lease relates to a residential unit built in a housing estate, partly on state-owned land and partly on Turkish Cypriot-owned land, the prescribed rent shall not be collected or deemed to be due:

(a) A copy of this contract bearing the signatures of the lessor and the lessee and the legal certification of their authenticity;

(d) any other document or element that the Director reasonably wanted to require.

It is understood that the provisions of this paragraph do not apply in the cases of a lease contract of state real estate between the government, as lessor owner, or a contract of lease of Turkish Cypriot real property between the Guardian, as lessor, and a displaced person, as lessee, for the purposes of housing or self-housing;

(f) if the lessor is under prohibition:
Provided that none of the provisions contained in this paragraph shall affect the powers of any person authorized, under or under the provisions of any law for the time being in force, to lease immovable property registered in the name of a person subject to prohibition;
(g) if the immovable property that is leased is subject to an encumbrance, unless the written consent of the person for whose benefit this encumbrance operates is obtained, and if this is a mortgage and of any guarantor:
It is understood that in the cases of renting out Turkish Cypriot real property, in accordance with the first reservation of subsection (1) of this article, the consent will be given by the Guardian.
(h) if the lease agreement does not provide for certain start dates (immediately or at any later time from the date of signing the relevant agreement or subject to the occurrence of a certain event) and the end of the lease period, even if this termination is subject to the occurrence of a certain event, provided that provision may be made in it for the renewal of the lease for a further period or periods that are clearly defined, but without the possibility of renewals ad infinitum

(j) if, on the date of submission of the application for registration of the lease, the period of this lease that has not yet expired together with any further periods clearly provided for in the relevant contract or period of renewal or extension of the contract does not exceed fifteen years:
Provided that a lease of immovable property, as referred to in the first proviso of subsection (1) of this article, may be registered, even if the period that did not expire does not exceed 15 years.
(5) Every contract for the lease of state immovable property between the Government, as owner, lessor, or contract for the lease of Turkish Cypriot immovable property between the Guardian, as lessor, and a displaced person, as tenant for the purposes of housing or self-housing, shall be considered, with regard to its submission for the purposes of registration, in accordance with the provisions of this Part, as not subject to paper marking pursuant to the Law on Paper Stamps.

## Legal consequences of registration
65C.-(1) By registering a lease in accordance with article 65B, the lessee acquires a right in rem over the immovable property leased in relation to which the registration was made, over any other relevant immovable property of the lessor such as those referred to in articles 22 and 23 and over everything which during the period of the lease may be planted, constructed, built or attached on the leased real estate.
(2) The right in rem referred to in subsection (1) is subject to the conditions contained in the lease agreement, and subject to these, it may be transferred or sub-leased as provided for in article 65D and be subject to inheritance, encumbered or compulsorily sold:
Provided that the reference in the subsection to the terms contained in the lease agreement does not in any way affect the encumbrance, under or by virtue of the provisions of any laws for the time being in force, otherwise or by mortgage, or forced sale of the real right acquired by the registration of the lease agreement.

*(CHAPTER 224)*

## Registration of transfer or sublease of real right
65D.-(1) Where a right in rem acquired under a lease registered under section 65B has, subject to the terms of the said contract, been transferred or sublet for a period exceeding fifteen years, such transfer or sublease shall not be deemed to create any right in rem unless it is registered in accordance with subsection (3).
(2) Registration of the transfer or sublease of a real right referred to in subsection (1) is not carried out-
(a) if under the terms of the lease such transfer or sublease is prohibited;
(b) if under the terms of the lease such transfer or sublease is permitted only with the written consent of the lessor, and he either refuses to give such consent or is of unknown abode:
It is provided that in any such case the lessee may apply to the Court, and the latter, after executing any order which it may deem necessary to issue for the service or publication of a notice to the lessor, may order that the transfer or sublease be registered without the aforesaid consent;
(c) if, mutatis mutandis, the conditions referred to in paragraphs (a) and (c) to (i) of subsection (4) of article 65B are met;
(d) if the prescribed fees and charges are not paid for giving the notice required by subsection (4).
(3) Subject to the provisions of subsection (2), the registration of the transfer or sublease referred to in subsection (1), as well as the registration of any transfer as a result of inheritance or, as the case may be, the forced sale of a real right acquired pursuant to this Part shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of article 65B which, mutatis mutandis, shall also apply to the registration of any of such transfer or sublease as apply to the registration of the original lease.
(4) Upon making a registration as described in subsection (3), the Director shall notify the owner of the immovable property concerned.

(g) the lease agreement does not provide for certain start dates, immediately or within twenty (20) years from the date of signing the relevant agreement or subject to the occurrence of a certain event, and termination of the period of the lease, even if this termination is subject to the occurrence of a certain event, meaning that it may be included in this provision for the renewal of the lease for a further period or periods which are clearly defined, but without the possibility of infinite renewals;

4. (b) will not in any way affect the common property, its smooth operation or fruition; and
(c) will not affect in any way the walls that support the jointly owned building and its external walls, will not in any way endanger the security of the jointly owned building and will not affect its external appearance.

Every person who becomes the owner of a unit at a later stage has the same obligation to contribute towards this amount. This amount will be used as a fixed reserve against the common expenses of the jointly owned property, including the insurance of the jointly owned building according to the Law.
6. The Management Committee will submit to the owners a quarterly statement in which the expenses of the previous three months will be mentioned, their nature and the amount any member of the Management Committee, who is a owner, has contributed towards these expenses. These initial payments from the gentlemen will be used to pay expenses incurred in the first and subsequent years. If such initial payments prove insufficient to cover the expenses of the quarter, the principals shall accordingly increase the above amount, upon written notice of the Management Committee to each principal.
7. No owner or possessor-
(a) He shall not use any unit-
(i) For any unlawful purpose;
(ii) (state the non-permitted uses depending on the nature of the premises, i.e. residence or work);

(iv) part of the common property in such a way as to affect the appearance or aesthetics of the common building or the uninterrupted and undisturbed use of the owners, lessees, tenants, licensees, owners or those who use the other units of the common building;

A quorum exists if persons who own at least fifty percent (50%) of the jointly owned property are present, in person or by proxy, as shown in article 38Ο of the Law and in Table A' attached to these Regulations.
21. If a quorum is not present half an hour after the time appointed for the general meeting, the meeting is adjourned to the same day of the following week, at the same place and time. Although at such general meeting there is no quorum half an hour after the time appointed for the general meeting, the persons present and entitled to vote shall constitute a quorum.
22. At the beginning of the general assembly, the President and Secretary of the assembly must be elected.
23. Resolutions of the general meeting shall be taken by a show of hands, unless any master present in person or by proxy requires a vote to be taken. If no vote is required, a statement by the President that a decision was taken by a show of hands shall be conclusive evidence of that fact, without proof of the number or percentage of votes cast for or against the decision. The requirement to hold a vote can be revoked.
24. If a vote is required, it shall be conducted in any manner deemed appropriate by the President and the result of the vote shall be the decision of the assembly.
25. In the event of a tie, by ballot or show of hands, the President of the assembly shall have a second or casting vote.
26. In the event that the decision is taken by a show of hands, each owner will have one vote; if the decision is taken by voting, each owner will have a number of votes corresponding to the share in the jointly owned property belonging to his unit, as provided for in article 38I of the Law and as shown in Table A' attached to these Regulations.
27. Votes may be cast in person or by proxy, regardless of whether the decision is taken by a show of hands or by ballot.
28. The appointment of a proxy must be made in a document signed by the person appointing the proxy or by his representative. The proxy may be general or for a specific meeting and is not required to be a principal.
29. If the unit owner is a legal entity, he may participate in the meeting and vote with a proxy appointed in accordance with the provisions governing the legal entity.
30. When the unit belongs to more than one owner, the owners may, by a document signed by all the owners or their representatives, appoint one of them or any other person as their proxy for all general meetings or for a specific general meeting.
31. Unless a unanimous resolution is required by or under the Act, no owner shall be entitled to vote at any general meeting unless all dues due in respect of his unit have been paid.
32. If the owner is a trustee, he will exercise the right to vote in relation to the relevant unit excluding the beneficiaries of the trust and these beneficiaries do not have the right to vote.
33. Decisions in the general assembly are taken by a majority of the votes of the persons present and entitled to vote, unless a greater or qualified majority is determined by the Law or these Regulations. Where a qualified majority is specified by the Law or these Regulations in relation to any matter, no decision in relation to that matter shall be valid unless it is made by the majority specified.
34. A decision that imposes on any principal obligations or payments of any kind or extent not provided for by the Law or these Regulations or that alters his rights is not valid with respect to that principal, unless he consents to it.
35. The secretary of the general assembly must keep minutes of the proceedings in the appropriate manner.
PART VI - MANAGEMENT COMMITTEE
36. During the first general meeting, the owners determine the number of persons who will make up the Management Committee, which cannot exceed five, and elect the person or persons who make up the Management Committee.
37. If there is more than one member of the Management Committee, a treasurer is elected. If the members do not exceed two, they must be unit masters.
38. If a temporary Management Committee has been appointed pursuant to article 38KG of the Law, its term will expire on the date of the first general meeting.
39. A general assembly may change the number of members of the Management Committee appointed pursuant to these Regulations or supplement it by electing new members, if for any reason the number of its members has decreased:
Provided that, if the number of members of the Management Committee is reduced below the number set according to these Regulations, the remaining members of the Management Committee must convene an extraordinary general meeting in ten days to elect new members.
40. The term of office of the Management Committee, including the term of office of the members elected pursuant to Regulation 38, lasts until the election of the new Management Committee at the next regular general meeting:
It is understood that the owners, by their decision taken in a general meeting, can at any time terminate the term of office of any member of the Management Committee and replace him with another person.
41. The members of the Management Committee may be re-elected.
42. The members of the Administrative Committee, if there are more than two, elect one of them as President. If the members of the Management Committee exceed two, decisions are taken by majority vote. If there are as many votes in favor of a particular proposal as there are against, the decision is considered rejected.
43. The Management Committee exercises all the powers and performs all the duties specified in the Law or these Regulations and any other powers or duties delegated or imposed upon it by the owners in general meeting.
44. The Management Committee may employ persons or representatives and pay them reasonable remuneration, as it deems necessary.
45. The Management Committee keeps statements of income and expenses and submits them for approval with all relevant receipts and data to the regular general meeting.
46. Every master may at a reasonable time inspect the statements of income and expenditure and the relevant receipts and evidence.
47. The financial year in relation to a jointly owned building begins on the first day of January and ends on the thirty-first day of December each year:
Provided that the first financial year begins on the date of registration of the jointly owned building in the Land Registry and ends on the thirtieth day of December of the same year:
It is further provided that, if the date of registration of the co-owned building in the Land Registry is after June 30, the first financial year ends on the thirty-first of December of the year immediately following the year of registration.
