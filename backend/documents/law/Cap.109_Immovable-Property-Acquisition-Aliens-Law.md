## The Real Estate Acquisition (Foreigners) Law (CHAP. 109)

## Short title
1. This Law will be referred to as the Real Estate Acquisition (Foreigners) Law.

*(CHAPTER 109)*

## Interpretation
2. In this Law-
"alien" means not a citizen of the Republic and includes a company controlled by foreigners, a foreign company and a trust for the benefit of a foreigner but does not include-
(a) foreign Cypriot;
(b) foreign spouse of a citizen of the Republic, who is not divorced from her spouse pursuant to a decision of a competent Court;
(c) a citizen of a member state who has the citizenship of the European Union, in accordance with the provisions of article 20 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and a citizen who has the citizenship of a state party to the EEC. · and
(d) a legal person, established in accordance with the legislation of a member state and which has its registered office, its central administration or its main establishment in a member state party to the EEC;
(e) [Repealed];
(f) [Repealed];
"alien Cypriot" means a person, not a citizen of the Republic, who was born in Cyprus at the time when his parents had their habitual residence in Cyprus or whose father was born in Cyprus during the time when the parents of the said father had their habitual residence in Cyprus and includes a foreign spouse of a foreign Cypriot who is not separated from the spouse by virtue of a decision of a competent Court;
“secondary residence” [Deleted];
"foreign-controlled company" means, in relation to the acquisition of immovable property, any organization-
(a) in which half or more than half of the directors or persons holding the position of director, by whatever title they may be called, are foreigners or
(b) in which half or more than half of the votes belong to foreigners or to persons exercising, directly or indirectly, the right to vote on behalf of foreigners or
(c) in which half or more than half of the shares are owned by foreigners or
(d) in which a foreigner has, at the time of acquisition of immovable property by the organization, any powers conferred on him by the organization's articles of incorporation or other constitutive instrument to ensure that the business of the organization is carried on as he wishes;
Provided that when the powers referred to in this paragraph are granted to two or more persons acting jointly, the organization shall not be considered a corporation controlled by foreigners unless half, or more than half, of such persons are foreigners or
(e) in which any controlling interest is substantially owned half or more than half by foreigners or by an organization falling within any of the remaining paragraphs of this definition.
"alien trust" means a trust of any nature of which the beneficiary or one of the existing beneficiaries is a foreigner and includes any express or implied contract or agreement, written or oral, under which property not owned by ownership of a foreigner shall be held by him or for his benefit or otherwise be at his disposal or under a lease not falls under the provisions of paragraph (a) of subsection (6) of article 3;
"Member State" means a Member State of the European Union but does not include the Republic;
"state party to the E.O.H." means a state party to the Agreement on the European Economic Area, signed in Oporto on 2 May 1992 and ratified by the Agreement on the Participation of the Czech Republic, the Republic of Estonia, the Republic of Cyprus, the Republic of Latvia, the Republic of Lithuania, the Republic of Malta, the Republic of Hungary, the Republic of of Poland, the Republic of Slovenia and the Slovak Republic in the European Economic Area and Final Act (Sanction) Act 2004;
“Sovereign Base Areas” means the Akrotiri Base Area and the Decelia Base Area which are under British Dominion as defined in Article 1 of the Treaty Establishing the Republic of Cyprus signed at Nicosia on 16 August 1960;
“foreign corporation” means an organization incorporated in a country other than the Republic or the Sovereign Base Areas, whether or not it is controlled by foreigners.

## Restrictions on the acquisition of real estate by a foreigner in certain cases
3.-(1) The acquisition by a foreigner of immovable property otherwise or due to death without prior permission of the Council of Ministers is prohibited.
(1A) When the acquisition of immovable property exceeds the area absolutely necessary for the construction of a property for residence or business accommodation and in any case exceeds the area of two staircases, the permit granted by the Council of Ministers shall also be subject to such terms, limitations, conditions and criteria as may be determined by Regulations issued by the Council of Ministers and approved by the House of Representatives.
(2) Upon the submission of an application by any person, foreign or not, for the granting of a license for the acquisition of immovable property by a specific foreigner or by any foreigner-
(a) the Council of Ministers takes charge of this application, decides on it and communicates its decision in writing to the person who submitted the application with all possible speed.
(b) any permit granted may provide that no further permit is required for the acquisition by another foreigner, with such conditions as were intended to be imposed on the granted permit, of the immovable property for the acquisition of which it is granted, and in such a case and despite the provisions of this article no such further permit is required.
(3) A valid contract or agreement, written or oral, made in accordance with the provisions of the Law on Contracts or any other law in force from time to time, which provides for the acquisition of immovable property by a foreigner does not grant to the foreigner or for his benefit any right to acquire the immovable property, except after the granting of the permission of the Council of Ministers provided for in subsection (1) for the acquisition of this immovable property.
(4) None of the provisions contained in this Law affect the right of a foreigner to take the actions referred to in Article 2 of the Land Sale (Special Execution) Law.
(5) Any registration of immovable property in violation of this article is invalid.
(6) For the purposes of this article, the term "acquisition of immovable property" also includes-
(a) the lease of immovable property for a period which exceeds thirty-three years or may, together with any further periods by which a unilateral elective right to extend or renew the lease is granted, exceed thirty-three years:
Provided that for the purposes of subsection (1A) the above mentioned period must exceed ten years.
(b) The acquisition of shares in a company which was incorporated as a legal entity in the Republic or in the Sovereign Base Areas and which has, in each case, immovable property or in the Sovereign Base Areas when, taking into account any other shares of the same company that may be owned by foreigners, the acquisition of this share by a foreigner would make the company in question controlled by foreigners
(c) the creation of a trust for the benefit of a foreigner, which concerns, in whole or in part, immovable property, a lease of immovable property that falls under the provisions of paragraph (a) or a share of a company whose acquisition falls under the provisions of paragraph (b).

*(CHAPTER 109)*

## Power to issue Regulations
4.-(1) The Council of Ministers may issue Regulations that are published in the Official Gazette of the Republic-
(a) determining the minimum size of lots into which an alien may subdivide his immovable property for the purpose of sale
(b) generally for the better realization of the purposes of this Law.
(2) Any registration of immovable property made in contravention of any Regulations issued under subsection (1) shall be null and void.
(3) Regulations issued pursuant to this article may enable the Council of Ministers to exclude, in the manner in which the Regulations intended to determine, any foreigner or class of foreigners from the operation of these Regulations.

*(CHAPTER 109)*

## Exceptions.
5. The provisions of this Law are subject to the exceptions provided for in paragraph 3 of Article 39 and in Articles 46, 55 and 58 of the Treaty Establishing the European Community.

1. The provisions of the Acquisition of Real Estate (Aliens) (Amendment) Act 1990 (50 of 1990) are deemed to have come into force on 5 October 1989 (3 of 50 of 1990).
2. Transitional Provisions of Laws 52 of 1969 and 55 of 1972.
The Acquisition of Immovable Property (Aliens) (Amendment) Act 52 of 1969, and the Acquisition of Immovable Property (Aliens) (Amendment) Act 55 of 1972, contained certain transitional provisions. Since these are not part of the basic law, they cannot be included in the consolidated text of the law as an article. However, the mentioned provisions may be useful from a practical point of view, so it was considered appropriate to quote them in these comments.
(A) The Acquisition of Immovable Property (Aliens) (Amendment) Act 52 of 1969, the date of which came into force on 11th July 1969, contained the following transitional provisions in section 6 thereof:
6.-(1) Every decree issued on the basis of article 3, as it was in force before its repeal by this Law, and is in force on the day of the entry into force of this Law is considered as a decree issued on the basis of the new article 3 of the basic law substituted by this Law and as applicable both to the lease of immovable property and to the acquisition of shares of a company that falls under, in each case, within the definition referred to in subsection (4) of the aforesaid new section 3 and shall continue in force until revoked or amended by a new ordinance.
(2) Any consent given to the acquisition of immovable property pursuant to an ordinance such as that referred to in subsection (1) shall be deemed to be a permit granted under the new section 3 of the principal law as substituted by this Law and shall continue to be valid even after the expiration of the validity of this ordinance.
(3) None of the provisions contained in this law shall apply to the lease of immovable property or the acquisition of shares in a company that falls, in any case, under the definition of subsection 4 of the new article 3 replaced by this Law, if such lease or acquisition was agreed pursuant to a contract entered into before the effective date of this Law, provided that this contract will be brought to the Minister of Internal Affairs, within two months from the date of entry into force of this Law, by any of the contracting parties to note the date of its entry into force, and this note of the date constitutes indisputable proof of the entry into force of the contract as stated above.
(B) The Acquisition of Immovable Property (Aliens) (Amendment) Act 55 of 1972, the date of which came into force on 7th July 1972, contained the following transitional provisions in section 4.
4.-(1) Subsections (1) and (2) of section 6 of the Acquisition of Immovable Property (Aliens) (Amendment) Law of 1969 are hereby repealed, and the number 3 in brackets at the beginning of the only remaining third subsection is deleted:
Provided that any consent given before the entry into force of the said Law and any permit granted before the entry into force of this Law for the acquisition, in each case, of immovable property by a foreigner pursuant to the then provisions of the basic law in force, shall be considered as a permit granted pursuant to article 3 which was amended by this Law and is still valid.
(2) None of the provisions contained in this law affect the rights of a foreigner existing on the date of the commencement of the validity of this trust for his benefit, which relates in whole or in part to immovable property, provided that the document of the deed which created the said trust shall be brought to the Minister of the Interior, within two months from the above date, in order to note the date of its bringing, and this note of date will constitute indisputable proof of the delivery of the document in the manner mentioned above.
(3) None of the provisions included in this law shall apply to a contract entered into before the effective date of this Law for the acquisition of immovable property that has not yet been completed, in cases where the receipt of the prior permission of the Council of Ministers for this acquisition is not required in accordance with the provisions of the basic law but is required in accordance with the provisions of this Law, provided that this contract will be brought to the Minister of the Interior, within two months from the date of the coming into force of this Law, by any of the contracting parties to note thereon the date of its entry, and such note of the date shall constitute indisputable evidence of the entry of the contract in the manner aforesaid.
(4) For the purposes of this article, the term "acquisition of immovable property" also includes the lease of immovable property and the acquisition of a company share, which are referred to in paragraphs (a) and (b) of subsection (6) of article 3 of the basic law (as amended by this Law), and the term "immovable property" shall be interpreted accordingly.
This Law [S.S.: i.e. L.54(I)/2003] comes into force on the date of the Republic's accession to the European Union.
