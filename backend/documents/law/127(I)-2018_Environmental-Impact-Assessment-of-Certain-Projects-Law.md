## The Environmental Impact Assessment of Certain Projects Law of 2018 (127(I)/2018)
For purposes of harmonization with the acts of the European Union entitled -
"Directive 2011/92/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2011 on the assessment of the effects of certain public and private works plans on the environment", and
"Directive 2014/52/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16 April 2014 amending Directive 2011/92/EU on the assessment of the effects of certain public and private works plans on the environment",
The House of Representatives votes as follows:

## Short title
1. This Law shall be referred to as the Environmental Impact Assessment of Certain Projects Law of 2018.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Interpretation
2.-(1) In this Law, unless a different meaning emerges from the text-
"permit or approval or authorisation" means the decision taken by any government agency or the Cabinet which gives the right to the developer to carry out the work;
"applicant" means the person applying to secure approval to be entered in the Register;
"Reasoned Finding" means the decision taken by the Environmental Authority pursuant to the provisions of section 24;
"development" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of subsection (1) of article 2 of the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Law or the Road Regulations and Buildings Law;
"local authority" means a borough council or community council;
"geological heritage" means the natural geological or geomorphological features that have aesthetic, touristic, fundamental or scientific and educational value and provide a unique insight into the geological processes that influence the formation or evolution of the Earth, which may include one or a set of geosites;
"Opinion" means the decision of the Environmental Authority pursuant to the provisions of Article 29;
"public hearing" means a hearing held in accordance with the provisions of section 39;
"public consultation" means the consultation conducted in accordance with the provisions of subsection (7) of section 26 and aimed at recording the views of the interested public on the project under consideration;
"public work" means a work carried out by a government agency;
"Director" means the Director of the Department of Environment of the Ministry of Agriculture, Rural Development and Environment and may include his representative;
"facility" has the meaning assigned to that term by the provisions of subsection (1) of article 2 of the Water Pollution Control Law;
"special conservation zone" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of article 2 of the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Law;
"special ecological assessment" means the process of due assessment of the effects on the environment carried out under the provisions of article 16 of the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Law and the provisions of subsection (5) of article 7 of the Protection and Management of Wild Birds and Game Law;
"concerned authorities" means the authorities that may be concerned by a project or have a reasonable interest in the environmental effects that may arise from a project or possess specialized knowledge because of their special competence in environmental matters or because of local and/or regional competence and include local government authorities; depending on the nature of the proposed project and its expected main serious effects on the environment, these authorities include those referred to in the Third Appendix, without its contents being considered as exhaustive;
"Committee" means the committee established under the provisions of section 10;
"interested public" means a public that is affected or may be affected or whose interests are at stake by the various decision-making procedures regarding the effects of projects on the environment carried out in accordance with the provisions of this Law; non-governmental organizations whose main purpose of establishment is defined as the promotion of environmental protection are considered to have interests that are affected;
"project" means a project that falls under any category of projects of the First or Second Schedule or that will be determined under the provisions of article 5 and concerns the implementation of construction works or other installations or technical constructions or other interventions in the environment or the landscape, including interventions related to the exploitation of mineral resources;
"special protection zone" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of article 2 of the Protection and Management of Wild Birds and Game Law;
"public" means one or more natural or legal persons, as well as their associations, organizations or groups;
"notification" means disseminating information and informing public authorities and the public to take any decision or take any action in accordance with the provisions of this Law and includes electronic and printed information;
"project owner" means a person who applies for a permit for the implementation of a private project or the government agency that has the responsibility and takes the initiative to promote the execution of a project;
"government agency" means a ministry or an agency under a ministry or an independent official of the Republic of Cyprus and includes a department or branch of a ministry;
"Environmental Impact Assessment Study" or "Study" means a document or series of documents prepared in accordance with the provisions of section 26;
"Register" means, pursuant to the provisions of article 35, the Register of Registered Researchers of Environmental Impact Assessment Studies.
"body governed by public law" means a body established by law for purposes of public interest;
"building permit" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of the Roads and Building Regulations Law and which gives the right to the developer to implement it;
"Environmental Authority" means the Director of the Department of Environment of the Ministry of Agriculture, Rural Development and Environment;
"Environmental Approval" means the approval granted by the Environmental Authority under the provisions of section 20;
"Nature 2000 Network area" includes sites of community importance, special conservation zones and special protection zones;
"Town Planning Permit" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of article 2 of the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Law;
"Town Planning Authority" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of article 4 of the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Law;
"Coordinator" means the researcher who is registered in the Register and designated responsible for the coordination and preparation of Environmental Impact Assessment Studies;
"Department" means the Department of the Environment of the Ministry of Agriculture, Rural Development and the Environment;
"place of community importance" has the meaning assigned to that term by the provisions of article 2 of the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Law;
"Minister" means the Minister for Agriculture, Rural Development and the Environment.
(2) For the purposes of this Law-
(a) the term "impacts on the environment" means the direct, indirect and cumulative significant effects of a project;
(i) on population and human health;
(ii) biodiversity and especially protected species and habitats in accordance with the provisions of the Nature and Wildlife Protection and Management Law and the Wild Birds and Game Protection and Management Law,
(iii) in the landscape;
(iv) in groundwater and surface water, the atmosphere, soil, sea and climate;
(v) in material goods,
(vi) cultural heritage including antiquities, as defined in the provisions of the Antiquities Law,
(vii) on geological heritage,
and the interactions that the factors included in subparagraphs (i) to (vii) above have with each other.
(b) "environmental impact assessment" means the process consisting of—
(i) the preparation of a Study by the project owner, pursuant to the provisions of subsection (1) of article 18 or article 19 and which is prepared in accordance with the provisions of articles 25 and 26,
(ii) carrying out consultations or updates or notifications, depending on the case as provided for under the provisions of articles 8, 27, 38 and 39 and, as the case may be, article 21,
(iii) the evaluation by the Environmental Authority of the information included in a Study in accordance with the provisions of article 26 and of any additional information provided, where required, by the project owner, in accordance with the provisions of subsections (2) and (5) of article 28, as well as any other relevant information received through consultations or updates or notifications, as the case may be, as provided under the provisions of articles 8, 21, 27, 38 and 39,
(iv) the Opinion of the Environmental Authority issued pursuant to the provisions of article 29 regarding the significant effects of the project on the environment, taking into account the results of the assessment under sub-paragraph (iii) and, where appropriate, its own supplementary assessment, and
(v) the integration of the Opinion of the Environmental Authority in the decisions referred to in the provisions of article 31.

## Purpose of this Law
3. The purpose of this Law is the essential contribution to ensuring a high level of protection of the environment, public health and quality of life in maintaining the integrity of the areas of the Nature 2000 Network and its components and the efficient use of resources, through the assessment of the environmental impacts of public and private sector projects that may have significant impacts on the environment, before they are licensed or approved for implementation.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Scope
4.-(1) Subject to the provisions of subsections (2), (3), (6) and (7), this Law applies to any project that falls under the category of projects of the First or Second Annexes, including public projects, regardless of whether or not their execution requires the granting of a Planning or other permit or approval or authorization pursuant to the provisions of any law.
(2)(a) The Council of Ministers, at the request of the Minister of Defense and taking into account the observations of the Environmental Authority, may decide, considering each case separately, to exclude from the application of the provisions of this Law any project or part of a project, which is included in the First or Second Annex, which is intended to serve exclusively the purposes of the defense of the Republic, if it considers that the application of the provisions of this Law in relation to the above project will adversely affects the purpose for which it is intended.
(b) The provisions of paragraph (a) apply provided that the Minister and the Minister of Defence, in cooperation, take care to ensure the fulfillment of the purposes of this Law.
(3) The Minister, and the Minister of the Interior, by agreement, provided that they ensure the fulfillment of the purposes of this Law, may approve the exemption of a project or a part of a project from the application of the provisions of this Law in whole or in part, with the sole purpose of dealing with emergency incidents of civil protection, for which there is a need to take immediate special temporary measures and it is necessary to carry out the project without delay, for the purpose of protecting people, preventing damage to property or the environment or for the protection of public health or safety, when they are satisfied that the purposes of the project could otherwise be adversely affected.
(4) During the planning or execution of any project or part of a project which is exempted under the provisions of subsection (2), the Director or his representative, a representative of the Ministry of Defense and a representative of the General Staff of the National Guard, examine the possible effects of the project on the environment and formulate and implement appropriate measures to reduce or eliminate said effects, especially if they affect-
(a) habitats of protected wild fauna and flora, including sites of community importance, special conservation areas and special protection areas and species included in the Nature 2000 Network in accordance with the provisions of the Nature and Wildlife Protection and Management Act and/or the Wild Birds and Game Protection and Management Act;
(b) coastal areas and areas of special environmental value;
(c) protected natural areas; and
(d) landscapes, sites or structures of historical, cultural, architectural or archaeological importance.
(5) After the implementation of a project to deal with an incident, which was exempted in accordance with the provisions of subsection (3), the Director may, in cooperation with other government agencies and/or other bodies, proceed to ascertain any serious effects on the environment from the measures taken and determine management measures to deal with them and to restore the environment, to the extent that this is required and possible and coordinate their implementation.
(6)(a) In the case of a project that will be executed or operated in accordance with the provisions of a law specific to the project in question, the Environmental Authority may exempt the project from the obligation of public consultation, provided that it is satisfied that the purposes of this Law are met.
(b) The Director informs the European Commission every two years from the entry into force of this Law on the cases of application of the exception referred to in the provisions of paragraph (a).
(7)(a) In exceptional cases and since the application of the provisions of this Law would negatively affect the purpose of a public project, by a decision of the Council of Ministers taken on the proposal of the Minister, the provisions of this Law shall not be applied to this project provided that the fulfillment of the purposes of the provisions of this Law is ensured, as referred to in the provisions of subsection (8):
(b) The relevant decision can only be taken when the Council of Ministers is satisfied that the execution and/or operation of the project will not cause significant impacts on the environment of another state, and provided that-
(i) the execution and/or operation of the project is subject to the application of the measures determined by the Environmental Authority upon completion of the specialized form of environmental impact assessment, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (8), and
(ii) the information obtained in the context of the specialized form of environmental impact assessment, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (8) and the information about the decision to grant an exemption and the reasons for its granting, are made available to the interested public.
(c) The decision of the Council of Ministers to exempt a public project from the application of the provisions of this Law issued pursuant to the provisions of paragraph (a), is published with the reasons for the exemption in the Official Gazette of the Republic and posted on the website of the Department.
(d) The decision on the exemption of a public project which is taken pursuant to the provisions of paragraph (a) is notified by the Director before the start of the construction of the project, to the European Commission, citing the reasons that justify the exemption provided and the information made available to the public in accordance with the provisions of subsection (8).
(8) In the case of projects that are exempted from the application of the provisions of this Law, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (7), the Environmental Authority –
(a) carry out a specialized environmental impact assessment on the basis of all necessary information, taking into account the criteria set out in the Fourth Schedule in order to be satisfied that the project will not have a significant negative impact on the environment;
(b) determine measures to address any impacts; and
(c) informs the public regarding the result of the above assessment, with a relevant posting on the Department's website.
(9)(a) In the case of projects that are exempted from the application of the provisions of this Law in accordance with the provisions of subsections (2), (3) and (7), all the provisions of the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Law and the Protection and Management of Wild Birds and Game Law shall be observed.
(b) Projects exempted from the application of the provisions of this Law are not exempted from the obligation to carry out a special ecological assessment in accordance with the provisions of Article 16 of the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Law and the provisions of subsection (5) of Article 7 of the Protection and Management of Wild Birds and Game Law.

## Application of the First and Second Schedules
5.-(1)(a) In the event that there is doubt as to whether a project that may have significant negative effects on the environment is included in the First or Second Annex, it may be decided by the Director that this project is classified in the closest or most relevant category of project and falls under the specific First or Second Annex.
(b) For this purpose, the Director may, if he deems it necessary, seek the opinions of the authorities concerned, non-governmental organizations and the interested public.
(2) In cases where the Minister of the Interior has issued or is issuing a Development Decree pursuant to the provisions of Article 22 of the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Law by which a Planning Permit for development or any category of development subject to the provisions of this Law is granted, without a relevant application having been submitted, the competent authorities, during the process of granting a building permit, are required to act as a Town Planning Authority with regard to the obligations specified in provisions of this Law, all the provisions of which also apply to these developments.
(3) All projects of the Second Annex proposed to be carried out in the sensitive areas referred to in the provisions of subsection (4) of article 4 are subject, regardless of their size, to the obligation to submit the information referred to in the provisions of article 23 and to their examination and issuance of a Reasoned Finding in accordance with the provisions of article 24.
(4) Changes to a project included in the Second Schedule which, in the judgment of the Director, could have significant negative effects on the environment, such as if the project is to acquire a significantly increased capacity and scope, or if significant parameters such as its geographical extension, its technology, management practices or the manner of its use will change, transfer it to a project of the First Schedule, especially if the environmental conditions themselves create a risk of causing significant effects on it or if they are likely to to have cumulative effects of the project together with another project.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Phased approvals
6.-(1) In the cases of the integrated developments of large and complex uses referred to in point 30 of the First Annex, the environmental impact assessment process must be completed before the urban planning approval of the General Spatial Study, at least with regard to the significant negative effects of such development on the environment.
(2) In the above cases, the Environmental Authority must be satisfied that the Study submitted contains sufficient information and analysis to understand the effects of the development on the environment to the extent that they allow it to assess the significant negative effects as a whole and issue an Opinion before obtaining the approval referred to in the provisions of subsection (1).
(3) The components of all developments referred to in the provisions of subsection (1) and/or the effects on the environment remain for assessment at a later stage, during the licensing process of the individual projects, in accordance with the provisions of this Law.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Time limits
7.-(1) The time limits specified in various provisions of this Law may be extended by the Director by setting new specific time limits, in exceptional and justified cases depending on factors such as the volume of documents or the complexity of the proposed project or the non-granting of the requested information or the established difficulty of the project owner to provide all the required information in a timely manner.
(2) The Director shall inform the project owner in writing of the reasons for the extension of the deadline and the expected date of completion.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Transmission of information and consultations with concerned authorities
8.-(1) The Environmental Authority transmits, by e-mail or by post, to the authorities concerned, the information submitted to it in accordance with article 23 and for the Study being submitted.
(2) The Environmental Authority transmits, together with the information provided for in subsection (1), an accompanying note in which it requests the authorities concerned to submit by electronic mail or by post, within twenty (20) working days from the date of their receipt, their documented opinions and observations regarding the possible effects on the environment of the proposed project.
(3)(a) In the event that the complexity of the proposed project requires a specialized analysis, the authorities concerned may request an extension of the above time for submission of their documented opinions and observations.
(b) If the above extension is deemed justified, the Environmental Authority approves it, specifying both the duration of its validity and the data regarding the collection and evaluation of which it is granted.
(4) The Environmental Authority may invite the interested authorities referred to in subsection (1) to a meeting of the Committee provided for in the provisions of article 10 or of the group of experts provided for in the provisions of article 14, as appropriate.
(5) For the purposes of this article, "electronic mail" has the meaning assigned to this term by the provisions of article 4 of the Electronic Communications and Postal Services Regulation Law.

## Obligation to provide information
9.-(1)(a) Any government agency or organization under public law or local administration authority that possesses information or studies, particularly when these are prepared within the framework of the laws referred to in the provisions of subsection (2) of article 34, which could reasonably be considered relevant or necessary for the purposes of preparing or evaluating a Study or the information submitted in accordance with the provisions of article 23, with special reference to paragraph (a) of the subsection (2) of article 2, must, if requested, grant them to the project owner, who pays any prescribed fees and rights.
(b) In the event that the above authorities consider that the provisions of article 37 apply, they shall act in accordance with the provisions of said article.
(2) The Environmental Authority may inform in writing any state agency or public law organization or local administration authority regarding the usefulness and necessity of any studies or information that may be in their possession for the purposes of preparing or evaluating a Study or the information submitted in accordance with the provisions of article 23.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Establishment of a Committee
10.-(1)(a) A committee named "Environmental Impact Assessment Committee" is established (hereinafter "Committee"), consisting of the following-
(i) The Director or his representative, who acts as chairman of the Committee;
(ii) the Director of the Department of Urban Planning and Housing or his representative;
(iii) the Director of the Labor Inspection Department or his representative;
(iv) the Director of the Forestry Department or his representative;
(v) the Director of the Water Development Department or his representative;
(vi) the Head of the Game and Wildlife Service or his representative;
(vii) the President of the Scientific and Technical Chamber of Cyprus or his representative;
(viii) the President of the Cyprus Federation of Environmental Organizations or his representative; and
(ix) a representative from a non-governmental organization not represented by the Cyprus Federation of Environmental Organisations;
(b) The Departments or Service referred to in the provisions of paragraph (a) (i) to (vi) are prohibited from acting as members of the Committee when it examines projects for which it is the project owner and a Study is referred for examination to the Environmental Authority.
(2) Depending on the type of project, the characteristics of the environment that may be affected and the expected effects of the project, the Director may invite as experts, representatives from services with specialized knowledge, as well as from a specialized non-governmental or other body or organization with special knowledge of the project and the nature of its effects.
(3) All the members referred to in the provisions of subsections (1) and (2) must be qualified from a technical and scientific point of view for the matters examined and the projects evaluated, including the effects on the environment. Depending on the project and the nature of its effects, each authority and body mentioned above is obliged to be represented by a suitably qualified representative or representatives and may send a different representative or representatives for each matter under consideration.
(4)(a) The Director keeps a list of the qualifications and experiences of the Committee members which is freely accessible on the Department's website.
(b) The representative or representatives sent by each authority and body in accordance with the provisions of subsection (3) must be registered in the said list.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Powers of the Commission
11.-(1) The Commission examines the effects that the execution and/or operation of the project may have on the environment and the measures to reduce or deal with them.
(2) The Commission may request, through its President, to invite to any meeting a representative of a state agency or organization of public or private law or a scientific body or non-governmental organization, or organized groups of citizens, the interested public as well as any person, if it judges that their opinions or specialized knowledge on a specific subject may be considered useful or necessary for its work.
(3) At each meeting of the Committee, during which a project is examined and evaluated-
(a) the local authority within whose administrative boundaries it is proposed to be carried out and any other local authority likely to be affected by the project are invited to attend and express their views on the possible effects on the environment; and
(b) if it is a public project or a project to be carried out by a public law organization or a local administration authority, a representative of the state agency or the organization or authority that will carry it out is invited, for the purposes of presenting the project and providing clarifications:
It is understood that, at a meeting of the Committee during which a project is examined and evaluated, the interested public is also invited to attend and express opinions in the event that they themselves request their representation in it.

## Committee meetings and procedure
12.-(1) The Chairman of the Committee directs its work, draws up the agenda for each meeting and ensures that it is communicated by printed or electronic invitation to each member of the Committee at least seven (7) days before the meeting and is posted on the website of the Department.
(2) The Committee has a quorum when the Chairman or his representative and at least four (4) members attend the meeting.
(3)(a) Since the purpose of the Committee is to advise the Environmental Authority based on the specialized knowledge of its members, in the event that a member of the Committee is unable to attend the meeting, he may express his views in print or electronically.
(b) Opinions submitted in paper or electronic form are recorded in the minutes of the meeting and are communicated to all members of the Committee.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Secretary of the Commission
13. The Director appoints an employee of the Department as secretary of the Committee, who keeps summary minutes.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Panel of experts/evaluators
14.-(1) Irrespective of the provisions of article 10, the Director may, independently or after the recommendation of the Committee, in exceptional cases or for the purposes of carrying out a special ecological assessment in accordance with the provisions of article 33, assign to a group of experts or to an expert, the evaluation of one or more specialized sections of a Study.
(2) The experts or the specialist carry out an evaluation of the Study or the parts thereof assigned to them and submit a recommendation to the Environmental Authority.
(3) The Director informs all the members of the Committee in print or electronically of any decision to assign the evaluation of a specialized part of the Study, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1).
(4) At the stage of the evaluation of one or more specialized sections of a Study by a group of experts, in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1), summary minutes are kept by an employee of the Department designated by the Director. these minutes are made available to the public in printed form and in electronic form in a separate section of the Department's website.

## Conflict of interest
15.-(1) Persons who have a personal or financial interest either in the project under consideration or in the project owner shall not participate as members of the Committee.
(2)(a) The persons referred to in the provisions of subsection (1) make a declaration of their personal and financial interests and a declaration of conscientious and impartial execution of duties, upon assuming their duties at the Commission.
(b) Without prejudice to the provisions of paragraph (a), a person who has a personal, direct or indirect interest, in relation to the matter considered by the Committee, must declare it to the Secretary of the Committee at the beginning of the meeting or as soon as the existence of the person's interest becomes clear from the course of the discussion.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Final authority of the Environmental Authority
16. The Environmental Authority retains intact the obligation and the right to accept or not any proposal for decision-making submitted by the Committee, a group of experts or an expert as well as the possibility of approving or rejecting a Study or additions to the Studies of the projects under consideration.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Additional conditions for the granting of a Planning Permit or for the approval of project execution
17.-(1) Regardless of the provisions of the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Law or any other law or administrative practice, in a case where –
(a) the Town Planning Authority examines an application for the granting of a Town Planning Permit; or
(b) the Council of Ministers or any other government agency is considering the issue of granting approval and/or authorization for the execution of a project,
and the permit or approval or authorization relates to a work falling within any of the categories of works in the First or Second Schedule, the said authorities shall have due regard as a material factor in deciding whether to grant the permit or grant the approval or authorization, as the case may be-
(i) the Reasoned Finding of the Environmental Authority prepared pursuant to the provisions of article 24,
(ii) the Opinion of the Environmental Authority prepared pursuant to the provisions of article 29,
(iii) any representations submitted by another State, in accordance with the provisions of Article 21, and
(iv) any submissions made by persons or authorities who in accordance with this Law have the right to submit such submissions.
(2) After taking their decision, these authorities act in accordance with the provisions of article 31.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Projects subject to a Planning Permit
18.-(1) Irrespective of the provisions of the Law on Town Planning and Spatial Planning, when an application is submitted to the Town Planning Authority to secure a Town Planning Permit which concerns the execution of a project that falls under any of the categories of projects in the First or Second Annexes, the project owner submits as an integral part of his application-
(a) Study, if the work falls within a category of work in the First Schedule; or
(b) the information referred to in the provisions of article 23, if the project falls under a category of projects of the Second Annex.
(2) When submitting the application, the developer must submit as many copies of the Study or the information submitted in accordance with the provisions of article 23 as requested by the Environmental Authority.
(3) After submitting the application in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1), the Urban Planning Authority sends to the Director copies of the Study or the information provided for in the provisions of article 23, together with the application and any additional information or data it has at its disposal that in its opinion is useful or necessary for the purposes of assessing the effects that the execution and/or operation of the project for which the application was submitted may have on the environment.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Public works
19. Notwithstanding the provisions of any other law or administrative practice, before approval or authorization is granted by the Council of Ministers or by any other government agency for the execution of a public project falling within any of the categories of projects in the First or Second Schedule, it shall be prepared and submitted to the Environment Authority, in respect of the project in question –
(a) Study, if the work falls within any of the categories of work in the First Schedule; or
(b) the information provided for in the provisions of article 23, if the project falls under any of the categories of projects in the Second Annex.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Projects not subject to a Planning Permit-Environmental Approval
20.-(1) Any person who intends to carry out a project which falls under the category of projects of the First or Second Annexes, but whose execution does not constitute development in accordance with the provisions of the Urban Planning and Spatial Planning Law, must, before starting any work on the execution of the project, secure an Environmental Approval from the Environmental Authority, in accordance with the provisions of this article.
(2)(a) In order to secure the Environmental Approval, a relevant application is submitted to the Director, which is accompanied by-
(i) Study, if the work falls within a category of work in the First Schedule; or
(ii) the information provided for in the provisions of article 23, if the project falls under a category of projects of the Second Annex.
(b) When submitting the application, the project owner must submit as many copies of the Study or the information submitted in accordance with the provisions of article 23 as requested by the Environmental Authority.
(3) The Environmental Authority may, as a result of the examination of the above application and in compliance with the provisions of subsection (2) of article 16 of the Nature and Wildlife Protection and Management Law –
(a) refuse to grant Environmental Approval, on the grounds that the execution and/or operation of the project will cause significant adverse effects on the environment which cannot be eliminated or reduced by taking any measures; or
(b) grant the Environmental Approval without the imposition of any conditions; or
(c) to grant the Environmental Approval, but to impose specific conditions regarding the execution of the project, which aim to eliminate or reduce the effects that the execution and/or operation of the project may have on the environment.
(4) The decision of the Environmental Authority and, where applicable, the Environmental Approval are posted on the website of the Department.

*(127(I)/2018)*

## Transboundary effects
21.-(1) In the event that it becomes known to the Director that the execution of a specific project in the Republic may have significant effects on the environment of another state that is a member of the European Union or, in the event that the other state whose environment may suffer significant effects from the execution of such a project requests to be informed about this project, the Director shall send to that state, as soon as possible and in any case no later than the date of informing the public in the Republic about the execution of the project, the following information –
(a) a description of the project, together with any available information regarding the significant effects that the project may have on the environment of that State; and
(b) full particulars as to the nature of the decision which may be taken,
and provides this state with a reasonable deadline to notify the Director whether it will participate in the environmental impact assessment process, as defined in this Law.
(2) If the state to which information was sent in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1) notifies its desire to participate in the process of assessing the effects on the environment from the execution of the project, the Director shall send it, as soon as possible, the following, if the provisions of subsection (1) have not already been sent –
(a) the application in relation to which the effects on the environment are being considered;
(b) a statement that the project is subject to an environmental impact assessment process and, as the case may be, that the provisions of this article apply;
(c) the date the Study was submitted and the name of the person or government agency that submitted it;
(d) a copy of the Study submitted in respect of the project;
(e) any information collected in accordance with the provisions of sections 25 and 28 and the main reports and advice provided to the Environment Authority at the time the interested public is informed in accordance with the provisions of section 27;
(f) a statement as to the nature of the possible decisions or the draft decision, where available;
(g) information regarding the environmental impact assessment process provided for in the provisions of this Law, including the deadlines and the manner of providing all available information; and
(h) information regarding the publication of a notification for the preparation of a Study and the submission of presentations in accordance with the provisions of article 27.
(3) When the Director acts in accordance with the provisions of subsections (1) and (2) he also ensures that-
(a) the information and data it sends to be made available within a reasonable time, to the authorities of the other state which are responsible for environmental matters, to the public of the other state whose area may suffer significant effects from the execution of the project and to the public through the website of the Department, and the Official Gazette of the Republic; and

## Joint assessment, Reasoned Finding and/or issuance of an Opinion
33.-(1) In the case of projects for which there is an obligation to carry out an assessment of their effects on the environment from the provisions of this Law, as well as a special ecological assessment in accordance with the provisions of article 16 of the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Law and article 7 of the Protection and Management of Wild Birds and Game Law, the provisions of this article shall apply.

(3) The special ecological assessment, autonomous or as part of the entire environmental impact assessment process, is carried out after a relevant study has been prepared by the project owner, taking into account the conservation objectives of a place of community importance, the relevant special conservation zone or special protection zone. the sole purpose is to ensure the Environmental Authority that the project does not damage the integrity of the said place of community importance, the said zones or cause an alteration of their character and to impose relevant conditions to mitigate any other effects that may affect them.
(4) In the case of projects that are not included in the First or Second Annex but may affect a place of community importance or the zones referred to in the provisions of subsection (4), the provisions of article 16 of the Nature and Wildlife Protection and Management Law shall apply.
(5) For projects of the Second Annex that may affect a place of community importance and/or the zones referred to in subsection (4), they are submitted to the Environmental Authority, together with the information referred to in the provisions of article 23 and special information that includes, at least, the following-
(a) a brief description of the site, including its main ecological characteristics, based on the cartographic, descriptive, statistical and other data available for the Nature 2000 Network areas, the protection objectives and the provisions of the management plan;
(b) assessment of the potential effects on the protected area or object, using available information and data, including those described in the provisions of paragraph (a) and other available environmental information supplemented, if necessary, by field information from the site and ecological surveys; and
(c) determining whether there is a risk that the effects identified are significant, considering that, in case of uncertainty, the effects should be assumed to be significant.
(6) The Reasoned Finding of the Environmental Authority may reach the following conclusions or results with regard to the impact of a place of community importance, a special conservation zone or a special protection zone –
(a) The nature of the project is such that it precludes the existence of significant impacts and the project may proceed, either without any changes or with such conditions as may be proposed, in order to ensure that the integrity of the site of community importance, special conservation area or special protection area is not impaired. or
(b) the effects are distinct and significant, or uncertain to the extent that the integrity of the site of community importance, the special conservation zone or the special protection zone is likely to be endangered and, for this reason, a special ecological assessment must be prepared as referred to in the provisions of subsection (3); or
(c) the potential significant effects of the project will be negative and irreversible on the species and habitats of the site of community importance, special conservation area or special protection zone and, for this reason, the project must be rejected.
(7) For projects of the First Annex, a special ecological assessment is submitted in accordance with the provisions of article 16 of the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Law, which allows the assessment of the possible effects for the purposes of the procedure carried out in accordance with the provisions of article 28, as part of the Study, respecting the provisions of subsections (2) and (3) and containing the information of the Sixth Annex, to the extent that these they are relevant to the project, the area and the potential impacts.
(8) During the assessment process, the group of experts, which is determined in accordance with the provisions of article 14 for the purposes of the special ecological assessment, examines and evaluates the elements resulting from the special ecological assessment, in order for the Environmental Authority to receive the required documentation that the project will not damage the integrity of the site of community importance, the special conservation zone or special protection zone and will not cause an alteration of their character, and recommends relevant conditions to mitigate any other impacts that may affect the site of community importance, the special conservation zone or the special protection zone.
(9) The Opinion of the Environmental Authority is-
(a) negative if the special ecological assessment process concludes that the effects of the project will be negative and irreversible for the species and habitats of the site of community importance, the special conservation zone or the special protection zone.
(b) positive and after taking into account all the binding findings resulting from the special ecological assessment process it has carried out.

## Public hearing
39.-(1)(a) For each project of the First Schedule for which a Study is submitted, the Director decides whether the holding of a public hearing would be useful or appropriate, taking into account the characteristics of the project, whether it is a project similar to which has not been done before in its form or dimensions, the scope of public interest in the project as well as its spatial and environmental effects and dimensions.

(c) In the event that he decides to hold a public hearing in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1), the Director shall ensure the following:
(i) Regulates every necessary detail for the conduct of the public hearing, determines the days and places of its holding and identifies the main issues that are considered essential in terms of the evaluated project and for which the presentation of opinions is sought.
(ii) makes relevant publications and announcements, at least fifteen (15) days before the hearing, on the website of the Department, in two widely circulated newspapers in the Republic, with postings in the offices of the local administration authority concerned and, in cases of large projects with possible wider implications, with announcements on television and radio stations.

## Offenses and penalties
46.-(1) Any person who,
(a) violates any conditions and/or measures set by the Environmental Authority in an Environmental Approval or Reasoned Finding or Opinion,
(b) commits any act or omission or refusal to comply contrary to the provisions of this Law or the decrees or regulations or decisions or instructions or notices issued under this Law;
(c) knowingly makes a false or misleading statement;
(d) willfully or negligently fails to act as contemplated in this Law or provides false or misleading information or inaccurate information or omits information or conceals information in relation to any matter under this Law, including information and Studies prepared in accordance with the provisions of articles 23 and 26, respectively, and their relevant annexes, and/or
(e) obstructs an inspector in the performance of the duties and powers conferred on him under this Act;
is guilty of an offense and, upon conviction, is subject to a fine not exceeding five thousand euros (€5,000). In the event of a second or subsequent conviction, said offenses are punishable by a prison sentence not exceeding six (6) months or a fine not exceeding ten thousand euros (€10,000) or both.
(2)(a) If, for any of the above offenses for which a charge has been brought against a person, it is alleged that the commission thereof causes or is likely to cause pollution of the environment or danger to public health, an interim injunction may be sought, prohibiting the continuation or repetition of the alleged unlawful act or omission until the final disposition of the case in respect of which the charge has been brought.
(b) The decree in question is issued by the President of the District Court at the request of the Attorney General of the Republic.
(c) The conditions for issuing such a decree are governed, mutatis mutandis, by the provisions of article 32 of the Courts Law, article 9 of the Civil Procedure Law and the relevant Civil Procedure Procedural Regulations.
(d) The temporary decree can also be issued after a unilateral (ex parte) application, in application, observing the proportions, the provisions of article 9 of the Civil Procedure Law and the Civil Procedure Procedural Regulations. In this case, for the purposes of filing an objection or for the opposing party to demonstrate a reason for the issued decree to cease to remain in force, the relevant deadline that can be set by the Court does not exceed fourteen (14) days.
(3) In addition to any penalty imposed under the provisions of subsection (1), the court shall have power to order that the project in respect of which the criminal offense was committed or any part of the project be demolished or moved or suitably configured so as to comply with any conditions accompanying the Environmental Approval, or the Reasoned Finding or Opinion, as contained in the Planning Permit, or the approval of the Council of Ministers or the state agency and to order the restoration of the environment at the expense of the person responsible for the project and to determine the time within which the condemned must comply.
(4) Any person against whom an order has been made under the provisions of subsections (2) and/or (3) fails to comply with the order shall be guilty of an offense whether or not the provisions of subsection (5) have been applied and shall, on conviction, be liable to imprisonment for a term not exceeding twelve (12) months or to a fine not exceeding fifty thousand Euros (€50,000) or both of these penalties.
(5) In the event that a person, against whom a decree has been issued pursuant to the provisions of subsections (2) and/or (3), fails to comply with the decree, then the measures may be taken by the Department and the relevant costs shall be collected from the person in question as a civil debt owed to the Republic.
(6) If any legal entity is responsible for the commission of any of the above offenses and it is proven that the offense was committed with the consent, collusion or tolerance-
(a) of any of the members of the board of directors or management or the committee managing the affairs of that legal person, or
(b) the general manager or director or managing director of the legal entity,
then the criminal prosecution for the offense can be directed against the legal person and all or any of the above natural persons.

FOURTH APPENDIX
(Articles 4, 22, 23 and 24)
AMOUNT DETERMINATION CRITERIA FOR A PROJECT INCLUDED IN SECOND APPENDIX REQUIRED TO PREPARE ASSESSMENT STUDY IMPACTS ON THE ENVIRONMENT
1. Features of the project:
The characteristics of the project must be considered, particularly in terms of
(a) the size and design of the whole project;
(b) stacking with other existing and/or approved projects;
(c) the use of natural resources and in particular soil, land, water and biodiversity,
(d) the generation of waste,
(e) pollution and nuisance;
(f) the risk of major accidents and/or disasters related to the project in question, including the risks caused by climate change, according to scientific knowledge, and
(g) risks to human health (for example due to water contamination or air pollution).
2. Location of the project:
The environmental sensitivity of the geographical areas likely to be affected by the projects is examined, particularly in terms of
(a) the existing and approved land use;
(b) the relative richness, availability, quality and regenerative capacity of the natural resources of the area and its subsoil (including soil, land, water and biodiversity), and
(c) the capacity of the natural environment to absorb impacts, with particular emphasis on the following areas:
(i) Wetlands, riparian areas, estuaries.
(ii) Coastal areas and the marine environment.
(iii) Mountain and forest areas.
(iv) Protected natural areas and natural parks.
(v) Conserved or protected areas under -
- the Town Planning and Spatial Planning Laws of 1972 to 2016,
- the Forestry Law of 2012,
- the Protection and Management of Nature and Wildlife Acts 2003 to 2015, including places of community importance and special conservation areas, and
- the Protection and Management of Wild Birds and Game Laws of 2003 to 2015, including special protection zones.
(vi) Areas in which there has already been a failure to meet environmental quality standards set by European Union and national legislation related to the project or in which such failure is deemed to have occurred.
(vii) Densely populated areas.
(viii) Landscapes and sites of historical, cultural or archaeological significance.
3. Type and characteristics of potential impacts:
The possible significant effects of a project on the environment must be examined in relation to the criteria set out in points 1 and 2, in terms of the effects of the project on the factors set out in subsection (3) of article 26 taking into account-
(a) the magnitude and spatial extent of the impacts (for example the geographical area and size of the population likely to be affected);
(b) the nature of the effects;
(c) the transboundary nature of the effects;
(d) the intensity and complexity of the impacts;
(e) the likelihood of the effects;
(f) the expected onset, duration, frequency and reversibility of the effects;
(g) the accumulation of impacts with the impacts of other existing and/or approved projects, and
(h) the ability to effectively reduce impacts.

A description of the relevant aspects of the current state of the environment (baseline scenario) and an outline of its likely evolution if the project is not implemented to the extent that, with reasonable effort, it is possible to estimate physical changes from the baseline scenario, based on the availability of environmental information and scientific knowledge.
4. Description of the factors specified in subsection (4) of article 26 that may be significantly affected by the project: population, human health, biodiversity (for example flora and fauna), land (for example land occupation), soil (for example organic matter, erosion, compaction and sealing), water (for example hydromorphological changes, quantity and quality), air, climate (for example gas emissions of greenhouse effect, any impact related to adaptation to climate change), material assets, cultural heritage (including architectural and archaeological aspects) and the natural landscape.
5. Description of the possible significant effects that the project may cause on the environment, including from-
(a) the construction and existence of the project, including, as the case may be, demolition work;
(b) the use of natural resources, in particular land, soil, water and biodiversity, in accordance with the sustainable availability of these resources;
(c) the emission of pollutants, noise, vibrations, light, heat, radiation, causing nuisance and disposal and recovery of waste,
(d) risks to human health, cultural heritage or the environment (for example due to accidents or disasters);
(e) the accumulation of impacts with other existing and/or approved projects, taking into account any environmental problems concerning areas of particular environmental importance that may be affected or the use of natural resources;
(f) the climate impacts of the project (for example nature and magnitude of greenhouse gas emissions) and the vulnerability of the project to climate change, and
(g) the technologies and materials to be used.
The description of the potential significant impacts on the factors referred to in subsection (3) of article 26 must cover the direct and any indirect, secondary, cumulative, cross-border, short-term, medium-term and long-term, permanent and temporary, positive and negative impacts of the project, cumulative with other existing and/or approved projects. This description takes into account the environmental protection objectives set at the level of the European Union or by the Republic and which are related to the project or the parameters of the environment that will be affected.
6. A description of the forecasting methods or data used to identify and assess the significant environmental impacts, including details of the difficulties, such as technical weaknesses or lack of knowledge encountered in gathering the required information, as well as a presentation of the main uncertainties that exist. Where possible, quantify the uncertainty of the forecasts.
7. Description of the measures proposed to prevent, prevent, reduce and, if possible, compensate for any significant negative effects on the environment identified and, accordingly, any proposed monitoring arrangements, such as the preparation of an ex post analysis of the project. The description in question should explain the extent of prevention, reduction, prevention or compensation of significant adverse effects on the environment and cover both the construction stage and the operation stage and any subsequent abandonment and/or demolition of the project.
8. Description of the expected significant adverse effects of the project on the environment, resulting from the project's vulnerability to major accident and/or disaster risks associated with the project in question. For this purpose, relevant information available and obtained through risk assessments in the application of the Occupational Safety and Health (Management of Major Accident Hazards Involving Hazardous Substances) Regulations 2015 and the Ionizing Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Acts 2002 to 2011 may be used for this purpose, provided that they are met the terms of this Law. Accordingly, this description must include measures to prevent and deal with or mitigate the significant negative effects of these events on the environment and detailed information on the preparedness and proposed response to such emergencies.
9. Non-technical summary of the information prepared in accordance with points 1 to 8.
10. Reference list detailing the sources used for the descriptions and estimates included in the Study.
11. Details for the Study Group.

(Article 33)
INFORMATION INCLUDED IN THE IMPACT ASSESSMENT STUDY IN THE ENVIRONMENT AND/OR IN A SPECIAL ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT FOR PROJECTS WHICH AREAS OF THE NATURE 2000 NETWORK AFFECTED OR MAY BE AFFECTED
Scholars ensure the inclusion in the Study or stand-alone text, as appropriate, of the following and attach a checklist at the beginning noting as many parameters as have been covered therein.
1. Information about the project:(Note: These may be shared with the rest of the Study, with related references and references.)

(b) Total area to be occupied by the project.
(c) Size and other specifications of the project.
(d) Characteristics of existing, proposed, or other approved projects likely to cause interactive or cumulative impacts with the proposed project and likely to affect the area.
(e) Planned or foreseen nature conservation initiatives (measures, actions) that may affect the status of the area in the future.
(f) Mapping (location) of the relationship (e.g. distance) between the proposed project and the Nature 2000 Network area.
2. Information about the area of the Nature 2000 Network:
(a) Reasons for the designation of the area as Nature 2000 (species and habitats).
(b) Conservation and protection objectives of the area and factors contributing to its conservation value.
(c) Existing conservation status of the area.
(d) Current state of the area (habitats, species, landscape, cohesion of the area).
(e) Key characteristics of Schedule I habitats and Schedule II species of the Nature and Wildlife Conservation and Management Acts 2003 to 2015 and Schedule I birds of the Wildlife and Game Protection and Management Acts 2003 to 2015.
(f) Ecology (function and structure) of species and habitats and of the ecosystem in general.
(g) Ecological requirements and aspects of the area considered vulnerable to change, particularly land use change.
(h) Basic elements of ecosystem structure and function that create and maintain the integrity of the area (landscape elements).
(i) Anthropogenic and seasonal effects (biotic and abiotic factors) on key habitats of Schedule I and species of Schedule II of the Conservation and Management of Nature and Wildlife Acts 2003 to 2015 and of birds of Schedule I of the Protection and Management of Wild Birds and Game Acts 2003 to 2015.
(j) Other management, conservation and protection issues related to the area, including possible future physical changes, taking into account the anthropogenic factor.
The relevant information, compatibility, measures, analyzes and conservation objectives included in the case-by-case Management Plans of the prepared Nature 2000 Network areas are taken into account.
3. Information on the conservation objectives of the protected area in relation to the project:
(a) Project relationship with area management.
(b) Effects of project implementation on the area's conservation objectives (habitats and species) and landscape elements.
(c) Likely to cause delays in achieving the conservation objectives of the area.
(d) Likelihood of affecting progress towards achieving the area's conservation objectives.
(e) Potential for disturbance of the abiotic and biotic factors that maintain the favorable status of the area.
(f) Other objectives: Information as to whether the plan or project has the potential to-
(i) cause changes in vital aspects (such as rates of land cover and vegetation, diversion of streams and rivers, nutrient balance, water balance), which determine ecosystem structure and function;
(ii) change the dynamics of relationships (between, for example, soil and water or plants and animals) that determine ecosystem structure and/or function;
(iii) interfere with predicted or expected natural changes in the area, such as affecting water dynamics or chemical composition;
(iv) cause a reduction in the extent of the area's habitats and the coherence of the Nature 2000 Network areas;
(v) cause a decline in the population of species of flora and fauna;
(vi) alter the balance between species of flora and fauna;
(vii) reduce the area's biodiversity;
(viii) cause disturbances that could affect population size or the density or balance between species, and
(ix) cause habitat fragmentation.
4. Other information:
(a) Alternatives considered.
(b) If there are compelling reasons of significant public interest for the implementation of the project.
(c) Compensatory and mitigation measures proposed.
(d) Data for the Study Group.

EIGHTH APPENDIX (Articles 35 and 36) REGISTER OF ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT ASSESSMENT STUDIES
1. Registry Content:
(a) The Register contains the details of natural persons whose registration is approved by the Register Committee pursuant to the provisions of this Law, in compliance with the provisions of the act of the European Union under the title "Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016 on the protection of natural persons against the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data and on the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC" and the Law on the Protection of Natural Persons Against the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Movement of Such Data.
(b) The Register shall contain the registration number, the date on which the registration was approved, and any other detail the Director deems necessary and useful.
2. Registry Committee:
(a) For the purposes of this Law, a Registry Committee is established which is responsible for evaluating and approving applications for Coordinator registration in the Registry, examining complaints and/or issues of violation of Coordinator obligations and submitting recommendations for deletion or temporary suspension of Coordinator registration.
(b) The Registry Committee has five members and is composed of the following members who are appointed by the Minister by Notification, which is published in the Official Gazette of the Republic:
(i) The Director or his representative, as chairman;
(ii) one (1) official of the Environment Department of the Ministry of Agriculture, Rural Development and Environment;
(iii) one (1) academic, who is not registered in the Registry, with relevant knowledge and/or qualifications to be determined by the Council of Chancellors;
(iv) one (1) representative from the Scientific and Technical Chamber of Cyprus (ETEK), who is not registered in the Register and possesses relevant knowledge and/or qualifications; and
(v) one (1) representative from a Non-Governmental Organization, who is not registered in the Registry and possesses relevant knowledge.
(c) The term of office of the members of the Registry Committee is five years, without the right of re-appointment, with the exception of the Director who serves ex officio.
(d) In the event of the resignation of a member of the Registry Committee before the end of his term, the Minister shall publish the new composition of the Committee in his Notification.
(e) The Commission may invite any special expert as a consultant in a case in which it considers that this serves its purposes.
3. Minimum required qualifications of a Coordinator for registration:
To register a Coordinator in the Registry, the following qualifications are required as a minimum: (I) CATEGORY A
(a) Academic qualifications: University degree in subjects related to the environment, such as Environmental Studies, Environmental Science, Environmental Engineering, Environmental Science and Technology, Environmental Management, Environmental Impact Assessment, or in another relevant subject at the discretion of the Registration Committee:
It is understood that the term "university degree" also includes a master's degree or title.
(b) Experience:
(i)Three years of professional experience in one or more fields related to environmental management services, such as environmental protection, environmental monitoring and controls, inspections, pollution control, emissions management, waste management, environmental resource management, environmental research; and
(ii)experience as a member of a writing team of at least five (5) relevant studies from three (3) different categories of projects set out in the First Annex for which an Environmental Impact Assessment Study was prepared for projects or plans/programmes or specialized areas of analysis.
(c) Vocational training:
At least thirty (30) hours of professional training within the last five (5) years which was provided by a recognized institution of higher education, as defined in the provisions of the Quality Assurance and Certification of Higher Education and the Establishment and Operation of a Body for Related Law Matters or by a body of the public sector or the wider public sector and which is directly related to the environmental impact assessment:
Provided that, the professional training provided for in this paragraph is not necessary for the registration of a Coordinator in the Register for a period of two (2) years from the date of the first publication of the Register after the entry into force of the Environmental Impact Assessment of Certain Projects (Amendment) Law of 2023.
(II) CATEGORY B
(a) Academic qualifications:
University degree in at least one of the following subjects or a combination of these subjects: Ecology, Wildlife Management, Sustainable Development, Protected Area Management, Natural Resource Management, Energy Management, Pollution Control, Waste Management, Civil Engineering, Chemical Engineering, Urban Planning, Architecture, Landscape Architecture, Topography, Mechanical Engineering, Biology, Biochemistry, Geography, Geology, Agriculture, Forestry, Forestry, Forestry and Biodiversity Management, Meteorology, Physics, Natural Science, Chemistry, Environmental Economics, Environmental Law, Environmental Governance, Mining and Mineral Resources Engineering, Oceanography, Hydrology, Marine Biology or another relevant subject at the discretion of the Registry Committee:
It is understood that, for the purposes of this paragraph, the term "university degree" also includes a master's degree or title.
(i)Five years of professional experience in one or more fields related to environmental management services, such as environmental protection, environmental monitoring and control, inspections, pollution control, emissions management, waste management, environmental resource management, environmental research; and
(ii)experience as a Member of the writing team of at least ten (10) relevant studies of various formats for which an Environmental Impact Assessment Study was prepared for projects or plans/programs or specialized areas of analysis.
At least thirty (30) hours of professional training within the last five (5) years, which was provided by a recognized institution of higher education, as defined in the provisions of the Quality Assurance and Certification of Higher Education and the Establishment and Operation of a Body for Related Law Matters or by a body of the public sector or the wider public sector and which is directly related to environmental impact assessment:
Provided that, the professional training provided for in this paragraph is not necessary for registration of a Coordinator in the register for a period of two (2) years from the date of the first publication of the Register after the entry into force of the Environmental Impact Assessment of Certain Projects (Amendment) Law of 2023.
(III) CATEGORY C
In the event that the Study is prepared by a government agency, an official of the agency who holds the qualifications provided for in Categories A or B, who is allowed to be registered in the Registry and who is approved by the Registry Committee, is indicated. 4. Submission of applications:
(a) A person who wishes to be registered in the Register submits an application to the Director on a relevant form prepared by the Department, accompanied by certificates proving the qualifications and certifications, where applicable, including certifications from the Cyprus Council for the Recognition of Degrees (KYSATS) or from the Scientific and Technical Chamber of Cyprus (ETEK) in the case of an engineer who is registered with a valid license, in the relevant discipline of engineering.
(b) The application form includes a declaration, signed by the applicant, about his qualifications and experience, which is accompanied by a detailed curriculum vitae and supporting information, including academic degrees, certificates of professional education and special training, information about his current employer, certifications of previous work experience, reports, publications, references, with which it is possible to verify the possession of those required for registration in the Register of Qualifications.
(c) After the first publication of the Register after the entry into force of the Environmental Impact Assessment of Certain Projects (Amendment) Law of 2023, applications may be submitted by new interested parties at any time, and their examination and the corresponding differentiation of the Register is carried out every six months.
(d) After three (3) months have passed since the publication of the Environmental Impact Assessment of Certain Projects (Amendment) Law of 2023 in the Official Gazette of the Republic, the Director posts on the Department's website an announcement about the initiation and application submission process.
(e) The application is accompanied by the corresponding examination fee, which is determined by the Minister by Decree issued pursuant to the provisions of Article 50.
(f) A Coordinator may request deletion from the Registry by submitting a written request to this effect to the Registry Committee.
(g) A person registered in a corresponding register of a member state of the European Union or the European Economic Area or another third country, when submitting his first Study, presents the relevant valid permit, approval or registration, as the case may be, from the legally competent authority of the said member state or third country:
Provided that, for the submission of further Studies, the registration procedure in the Registry is followed. 5. Examination of applications and objections:
(a) The Registry Committee evaluates the registration applications and compiles a list of applicants who may be registered in the Registry as Coordinators.
(b) The Registry Committee may, by a majority, judge a person as suitable for registration in the Registry, if it is convinced that he possesses the appropriate qualifications, knowledge and experience in the relevant fields based on the minimum required qualifications provided for in point 3.
(c) The registration is approved for a period of five (5) years and, in case the conditions under which it was granted do not change, it is renewed for a further period of five (5) years, provided that the Coordinator submits a relevant request at least six (6) months before the end of the period for which his registration was approved.
(d) The decision of the Registry Committee to register or reject an application for registration in the Registry is communicated in writing by the Director to the applicant together with the reasons for the decision.
(e) In case of rejection of the application for registration in the Registry, an applicant who disagrees with the decision of the Registry Committee may submit a reasoned request to the Registry Committee for a review within thirty (30) days from the date of notification of the decision, indicating the specific issues which he considers to have been wrongly evaluated by the Registry Committee.
(f) The Registry Committee examines the request for reconsideration and communicates its reasoned decision to the applicant within thirty (30) days from the date of its receipt.
(g) In the event that the applicant disagrees with the decision of the Registry Committee on the request for review, he may appeal to the Minister within fifteen (15) days from the date of notification of the decision on the request for review, indicating the specific issues that he considers to have been wrongly evaluated by the Registry Committee.
(h) The Minister examines the appeal and communicates his reasoned decision to the applicant within thirty (30) days from the date of receipt of the appeal.
6. Deletion and suspension of registration from the Registry:
(a) The Registry Committee, based on the evidence submitted before it, decides to suspend the registration of a Coordinator in the Registry for a period of time not exceeding one (1) year or to remove him from it in the event that he is found guilty by a court of any offense in violation of the provisions of this Law or any Regulations or Decrees issued pursuant to it.
(b) The Registry Committee, based on the information submitted before it, examines and may decide to suspend the Coordinator's registration in the Registry for a period of time not exceeding one (1) year, and/or to remove him from it in the event that it finds that the Coordinator has committed one or more of the following acts more than one (1) time:
(i) Provided incorrect and misleading assessment/information or omitted information in a Study;
(ii) submitted low-level work in a Study, i.e. did not follow the guidelines of the Environmental Authority or other competent authorities for the preparation of studies; or
(iii) demonstrably used or copied a Study or part of a Study without the approval of its author:
Provided that, the Director ensures that within the framework of the procedure provided for in this paragraph, the Registry Committee takes into account the relevant recommendation from the Environmental Impact Assessment Committee.
(c)(i) In the event that the registration of the same person in the Registry is suspended more than two (2) times, the Registry Committee examines and may decide on his permanent non-registration in the Registry;
(ii) in case of deletion of the same person from the Register more than two (2) times, the Register Committee examines and may decide on the permanent deletion without the right to re-register him.
(d) The Department keeps a record of the violations provided for in paragraph (b) for each Coordinator and submits the data to the Registry Committee every six months.
(e) The Registry Committee handles complaints submitted to it in compliance with the provisions of the act of the European Union entitled "Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of April 27, 2016 on the protection of natural persons against the processing of personal data and on the free circulation of such data and on the repeal of Directive 95/46/EC", and on the Protection of Natural Persons Against the Processing of Personal Data and the Free Circulation of such Data Law.
7. Suspension and Deletion Procedures:
(a) The Director informs the interested person in writing of the initiation of the examination procedure for suspension of registration or deletion from the Registry by sending a written detailed report of the facts and of the deadline within which he may submit a written documented response, i.e. within twenty-one (21) days from the date of notification to him of the written detailed report.
(b) The interested person may request a hearing from the Registry Committee and the Registry Committee shall provide such person with an opportunity to present his views.
(c) In the event that the Registry Committee decides to delete or suspend a Coordinator's registration from the Registry, the Director shall proceed with the deletion or temporary suspension, as the case may be.
(d) The Director publishes in the Official Gazette of the Republic and on the website of the Department a notice about the Coordinators who were deleted or whose registration was temporarily suspended.
(e) The developer submits to the Department of the Environment a new Environmental Impact Assessment Study by a Coordinator registered in the Register for the project prepared Study by a Coordinator whose registration in the Register was deleted or temporarily suspended.
8. Registry Accessibility:
The Director keeps the Register updated and freely accessible on the Department's website.
9. Responsibilities of Coordinator:
(a) The project owner designates a person who is registered in the Registry as Project Coordinator.
(b) The Coordinator-
(i) selects the scholars who participate in the preparation of the Study and this composition ensures that the Study is drawn up in accordance with the provisions of this Law, the relevant guidelines of the Environmental Authority and the conclusion of its conclusions;
(ii) is the study team's liaison with the project owner and project design team, the Environment Authority, the various government or other agencies and authorities and the public, and is responsible for preparing the Study;
(iii) prepares the final text of the Study in accordance with the requirements of this Law and the instructions of the Environmental Authority and other authorities, and is responsible for the findings and interpretations from the specialized analyzes carried out by the study team and from other studies and the documents made available to it and the comments and observations of the authorities concerned and the public; and(iv) bear responsibility for the content and final conclusion and recommendation of the Study to the Environmental Authority taking into account the assessment and opinions of the study team.
10. Declaration:
(a) The Coordinator and the other researchers and the supervised collaborators in the preparation of the Study sign a responsible declaration, in which it is stated that the specific Study or part of it was prepared exclusively by them and that they take responsibility for its content, to the extent that it concerns them, and a summary list of the parts of the study prepared by each member of the study group is included.
(b) The statements are included in the Study and their absence entails its non-examination by the Environmental Authority and the consolidation of the process.
NINTH APPENDIX
(Articles 35 and 36)
CODE OF AUTHORITIES
Because the environmental impact assessment services I provide must be of the highest professional standards and because in my related activities I must act in an independent, impartial and objective manner, and the provision of a service to the client with whom we have agreed to provide it is done under an equal bond relationship, I hereby declare that, in the provision of these services-
1. I recognize the importance of the society in which I live, and I contribute positively to it;
2.place the integrity of the environment, public health and safety above any private interest;
3.I am characterized by integrity, honesty, honesty and transparency in the way I conduct my professional activities and in the way I deal with my clients, government agencies and the public;
4.refuse to render professional services when I am asked to be biased in analysis or to omit or distort facts in order to reach a predetermined conclusion or result;
5.I take all appropriate measures and actions to ensure the validity of all data collected, analyzes performed or studies prepared by me or under my direction or control and I am accountable for the objectivity of my findings and recommendations;
6. discourage the distortion or misuse of work that I have performed or that was done under my direction;
7. I do not undertake the offer of services if I have any financial, professional or personal interests that may affect the objectivity of my work;
8.continually improve my professional knowledge and skills and keep abreast of progress and new developments in environmental impact assessment and related areas of competence and integrate this knowledge into my professional activities;
9. I understand that the violation of the above entails any suspension of my registration or deletion from the Register and/or brings about other consequences pursuant to the current legislation.
