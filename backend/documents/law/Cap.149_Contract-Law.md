## The Law on Contracts (CHAP. 149)

## Short title
1. This Law will be referred to as the Law on Contracts.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## General rule of interpretation of the Law
2.-(1) This Act shall be interpreted in accordance with the principles of legal interpretation prevailing in England, and the expressions used therein shall be presumed to have the meaning assigned to them by English law, and shall be interpreted accordingly to the extent that such interpretation is not contrary to the content of the text and provided that no other meaning is expressly intended.
(2) In this Law, unless the text indicates otherwise, the following words and expressions are used in the following sense:
(a) a person who declares to another his will for an act or omission, with the aim of securing the other's consent to that act or omission, is considered to be making a proposal~
(b) the proposal is deemed to have been accepted, when the person to whom it is addressed declares his consent to it. The proposal when accepted becomes a promise~
(c) the person who proposes is called the “debtor”, and the person who accepts the proposal is called the “creditor”~
(d) when, at the debtor's demand, the creditor or any third party has performed or is performing or promises to perform an act or omission, such act or omission is called "consideration" for the promise~
(e) every promise and every set of promises, which constitute consideration for each other, is an agreement~
(f) mutual promises are the promises, which constitute the consideration or part of the consideration between them~
(g) agreement legally unenforceable, is void~
(h) legally enforceable agreement, is a contract~
(i) an agreement legally enforceable at the option of one or more parties, but not at the option of the other or others, is a voidable contract~
(j) a contract which ceases to be legally enforceable becomes void when it ceases to be enforceable~
(3) “Representatives of the deceased” means the successors of the estate of the deceased according to law.
"dowry" means property given by one of the spouses or future spouses or by a third person for the sake of one of the spouses or one of the future spouses to the other spouse or, as the case may be, to the other future spouse, in exchange for the marriage.
"dowry agreement" means an agreement for the provision of dowry.

## Notification, acceptance and withdrawal of proposal
3. The notification of the proposal, the acceptance of the proposal, and the revocation of the proposal and the acceptance respectively, are considered to have been made by any act or omission of the person who proposes, accepts or withdraws, by which he intends to communicate the said proposal, acceptance or revocation which induces notification thereof.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## When is notification made?
4.-(1) The notification of the proposal takes place when it becomes known to the person to whom it is addressed.
(2) Notification of acceptance is made-
(a) against the person who proposed, when it is placed in the way of transmission to him, so that it leaves the authority of the person who accepts~
(b) against the person who accepts, when it becomes known to the person who proposed~
(3) Notification of revocation shall be made-
(a) against the person revoking, when it is placed in the path of transmission to the person to whom it is addressed so that it leaves the authority of the revoking person~
(b) against the person to whom it is addressed, when it becomes known to him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Revocation of offer and acceptance
5. The proposal may be revoked at any time before notification of its acceptance is made to the person who proposed, but not later.
Acceptance may be revoked at any time before notification is made to the accepting person, but not later.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Method of revocation
6. The proposal is revoked-
(a) by the service of a statement of revocation to the other party by the person who proposed~
(b) on the expiry of the period fixed in the offer for acceptance or, in the event that no such period is fixed, after the lapse of a reasonable time from the making of the offer, without notification of the acceptance~
(c) by the non-fulfilment by the person who accepts, of a deferral clause of the acceptance~ or
(d) by the death or insanity of the person proposing, if the death or insanity comes to the knowledge of the person accepting before acceptance.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Acceptance must be absolute
7. To convert the offer into a promise, the acceptance must-
(a) be absolute and unconditional
(b) manifested in the usual and reasonable manner, unless the proposal also specifies the manner of acceptance. If the proposal specifies the manner of acceptance, and the acceptance is not made in the manner specified, the person making the proposal may, within a reasonable time from the communication of the acceptance to him, insist that his proposal be accepted in the manner specified and not otherwise~ but if he does not persist, he accepts the acceptance.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Acceptance upon fulfillment of conditions or receipt of consideration
8. Fulfillment of the terms of the proposal or acceptance of any consideration for a mutual promise offered with the proposal shall constitute acceptance of the proposal.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Promises, express and implied
9. The promise is considered express, insofar as the proposal or acceptance is made in writing or orally. A promise is considered tacit, insofar as the proposal or acceptance is made in a way other than written or oral.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## What agreements constitute contracts
10.-(1) Contracts are all agreements which are drawn up with the free consent of parties capable of contracting, for legal consideration and for a legal purpose, which are not expressly characterized by this Law as invalid~ subject to the provisions of this Law, contracts may be drawn up in writing, or orally, or partly written and partly oral, or may be inferred from the conduct of the parties.
(2) None of the provisions contained in this Law affect any Law in force in the Republic and which is not expressly repealed by this Law, by which any contract is required to be drawn up in writing or in the presence of witnesses, or the validity of any Law relating to the registration of documents.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Ability to cooperate
11. Subject to the provisions of subsection (2), anyone capable of contracting is-
(a) has intact brakes~ and
(b) is not deprived of the capacity to contract, by virtue of any Law.
(2) The law for the time being in force in England, and relating to contracts entered into with a minor, shall apply to contracts entered into with a person who has not attained the age of eighteen years:
It is provided that a married person is not considered incapable of contracting for the sole reason that he has not reached the age of eighteen.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Designation of the party that has the brakes intact
12. A person is considered to have intact the brakes for the purpose of drawing up a contract, if at the time of its drawing up, he can perceive it and form a reasonable judgment about its consequences on his interests.
A person who does not usually have the brakes intact, but has the brakes intact at intervals, may draw up a contract during the time when the brakes are intact.
A person who usually has the brakes intact but intermittently does not have the brakes intact, cannot draw up a contract during the time he does not have the brakes intact.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of “consent”
13. Two majorities are considered to consent, when they agree about the same thing in the same sense.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of “free consent”
14. Consent is considered free, when it is not induced by-
(a) coercion, as defined in Article 15~ or
(b) mental pressure, as defined in article 16~ or
(c) fraud, as defined in Article 17~ or
(d) false representation as defined in Article 18~ or
(e) misrepresentation, subject to the provisions of articles 20, 21 and 22.
Consent is deemed to have been induced in the above manner if it would not have been given in the absence of said coercion, duress, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of “coercion”
15.-(1) "Coercion" is the commission or threat of commission of an act prohibited by the Penal Code or its amendment, or the illegal retention, or the threat of retention, of property, to the detriment of any person, which is done with the intention of forcing another to enter into an agreement.
(2) It is immaterial whether the Penal Code or any amendment thereof is in force or not in the place where the coercion is exercised.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of "Mental Stress"
16.-(1) The contract is considered to have been concluded as a result of "mental pressure" when the relations existing between the parties are such that one of them is in a position to dominate the will of the other and take advantage of this position to secure an unfair advantage over the other.
(2) In particular and without prejudice to the above principle, any person who-
(a) has real or apparent authority over the other or is in a relationship of trust with the other. or
(b) enters into a contract with a person whose mental capacity is temporarily or permanently impaired due to age, illness or mental or physical impairment.
(3) When a person who is in a position to dominate the will of another enters into a contract with him, and the transaction appears by itself or from the evidence adduced to be unduly onerous, the burden of proof that the contract was not entered into as a result of mental pressure is borne by the person who is in a position to dominate the will of the other.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of "fraud"
17.-(1) "Fraud" includes any of the following acts committed by one of the contracting parties, or with his consent, or by his representative, with the purpose of deceiving another contracting party or his representative, or forcing him to enter into a contract-
(a) the representation of an untrue fact as true, by a person who does not believe it to be true~
(b) the active concealment of a fact by a person who knows or believes the fact
(c) a promise made with no intention of fulfilling it~
(d) any other act of intent to deceive
(e) any act or omission specifically defined by law as fraud.
(2) Mere silence as to facts, which may affect the will of a person to enter into a contract, does not constitute fraud, unless the circumstances are such that, having regard to them, the person who is silent is under an obligation to declare them, or unless his silence amounts in itself to a declaration.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of “false representation”
18. “False representation” includes-
(a) making a positive statement in a manner not justified by the information of the person making the statement, of a fact which is untrue even though the person making the statement believes it to be true~
(b) any breach of duty which, without intent to defraud, confers a benefit upon the offender or any person claiming through him, by misleading another to his detriment or to the detriment of anyone claiming through him~
(c) causing, even without cause, a mistake as to the substance of the object of the agreement to a party thereof.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Cancellation of agreements entered into without free consent
19.-(1) If consent to an agreement was given as a result of coercion, fraud or misrepresentation, the agreement is a contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was so given.
(2) The contracting party, whose consent was given as a result of fraud or misrepresentation, may, if he deems it appropriate, insist on fulfilling the contract and be restored to the position he would have been in if the representations made had been true.
(3) If such consent was given in consequence of a false representation or silence, which constitutes fraud within the meaning of section 17, the contract is, nevertheless, not voidable, if the party whose consent was so given, was in a position to discover the truth by exercising ordinary diligence.
(4) Fraud or misrepresentation which did not cause the consent to enter into a contract of the party on whom the fraud was perpetrated, or to whom the misrepresentation was made, does not render the contract voidable.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Power to rescind a contract entered into as a result of mental duress
20.-(1) If consent to an agreement was given as a result of mental pressure, the agreement is a contract voidable at the option of the party whose consent was thus given.
(2) This contract may be canceled either completely or, if the party entitled to cancellation obtained any benefit by virtue of it, under such conditions as the Court deems it just to impose.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## An agreement becomes void if both parties are mistaken as to a fact. Consequences of legal error
21.-(1) If both parties to the agreement are mistaken as to a fact material to the agreement, the agreement is void.
Misrepresentation as to the value of the subject matter of the agreement is not considered a mistake of fact.
(2) The contract is not voidable because it was entered into as a result of a mistake regarding any law in force in the Republic~ but a mistake about a law not in force in the Republic brings about the same consequences as a mistake about a fact.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## A contract concluded as a result of a mistake by one of the contracting parties about a real event
22. The contract is not voidable for the sole reason that it was entered into by one of the contracting parties who was mistaken about a real fact.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Legal and illegal benefits and purposes
23. The consideration or purpose of the agreement is lawful unless-
(a) is prohibited by law~ or
(b) is of such a nature that, if permitted, it would contravene the provisions of any law~ or
(c) constitutes fraud~ or
(d) causes or involves damage to the person or property of another~ or
(e) the Court finds that it is contrary to morality or public policy.
In each of these cases the consideration or purpose of the agreement is considered illegal. Any agreement whose purpose or consideration is illegal is void.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## An agreement becomes void if the purpose and consideration is partially illegal
24. If any part of a single consideration for one or more purposes or any one consideration or any part of more than one consideration for a single purpose, is illegal, the agreement is void.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## An agreement without consideration is void unless in writing
25.-(1) An agreement concluded without consideration is void, unless-
(a) be made in writing and signed by the party who bears the burden thereof and is entered into by reason of natural love and affection between the parties, who are closely related to each other~ or unless
(b) is a promise to indemnify, in whole or in part, a person who has already done something voluntarily for the debtor or has done something which the debtor could legally be compelled to do~ or unless
(c) is a promise, given in writing and signed by the party who bears the burden thereof, to pay a debt in whole or in part, the payment of which the creditor could enforce in the absence of any law in force from time to time relating to limitation.
In any such case the agreement constitutes a contract.
(2) The provisions of this article do not affect the validity, in terms of the relationship between donor and donee, of a donation that has already been made.
(3) An agreement entered into with the debtor's free consent is not invalid for the sole reason that the consideration is insufficient~ however, the insufficiency of the consideration may be taken into account by the Court when judging the issue of whether the debtor's consent was freely given.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## An agreement restricting the freedom to marry is void
26. Any agreement restricting the freedom to marry is void.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Dowry document and contracts of a similar nature void
26A. Every dowry document and every contract of a similar nature, the validity of which is governed by Cypriot law, and by which an obligation is assumed as a cause of marriage to provide property to the future spouses or to each of them separately are invalid:
It is understood that this article does not apply to marriage contracts between Mohammedans undertaken according to the practice held in the Turkish Family Courts.

## An agreement in restraint of trade is void. Caveats
27.-(1) Any agreement to the extent that it is restrictive of the freedom to exercise a legal profession, trade or any kind of business, is void.
(2)(a) The person selling the commercial interest of a business may agree with the purchaser to refrain from carrying on a similar business, within certain local limits, for the period during which the purchaser or his licensee in the commercial favor, carries on a similar business within such limits, provided that such limits shall be deemed by the Court to be reasonable, having regard to the nature of the business~
(b) on the dissolution or in prospect of dissolution of a general or limited partnership, the partners may agree that some or all of them shall not carry on a business similar to that carried on by the partnership within such local limits as are mentioned in subsection (a)~
(c) the partners may agree that, during the existence of the general or limited partnership, some or all of them will not carry on a business other than that carried on by the partnership.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agreements limiting the freedom to take legal action are void
28.-(1) Any agreement, to the extent that it completely limits the freedom of any of the parties to enforce its rights under or in relation to any contract, through the usual judicial measures, or to the extent that it limits the deadline for the enforcement of said rights, is void.
(2) This section does not make illegal a contract by which two or more agree that any dispute which may arise between them in respect of any matter or class of matters shall be referred to arbitration and that only the amount awarded in arbitration may be recovered in respect of the dispute so referred.
After this contract has been drawn up, judicial measures may be taken for specific performance thereof, but if judicial measures other than those relating to specific performance of the contract or recovery of the amount awarded in this way are taken, by one of the contracting parties against the other in relation to any matter agreed to be referred to arbitration, the existence of this contract constitutes an obstacle to the judicial measures taken.
(3) This section does not make illegal a contract in writing by which two or more agree to refer to arbitration any matter which has already arisen between them, nor does it affect any provision of law for the time being in force relating to referrals to arbitration.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agreements void for vagueness
29. Agreements, the meaning of which is not clear or cannot be made clear, are void.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agreements under the type of bet are void
30. Agreements under the form of a bet are void, and no legal action shall be taken to recover anything alleged to have been won by a bet or entrusted to another in anticipation of the result of any game or other uncertain event upon which the bet rests.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of “conditional contract”
31. "Conditional contract" is a contract to act or refrain from acting, if an event consequential to the contract occurs or does not occur.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Executory contract subject to the occurrence of an event
32. A contract to act or refrain from acting subject to the occurrence of a future and uncertain event is not legally enforceable until the occurrence of the event.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Contract enforceable under the condition of non-occurrence of the event..
33. A contract to act or refrain from an act, under the condition of the non-occurrence of a future and uncertain event becomes enforceable, when the occurrence of the event becomes impossible and not before.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Heresy dependent on future conduct of person in life
34. If the future event, on which the contract depends, is the conduct of a person at an indefinite time, the event is deemed to have become impossible when that person does something, which renders that conduct impossible at any specified time, or otherwise if further sects are fulfilled.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Heresy dependent on the occurrence of a certain event within a certain time..
35.-(1) A contract to act or refrain from an act under the condition of the occurrence of a certain uncertain event within a certain time becomes void if, after the certain time, the event did not occur, or if before the certain time, the event became impossible.
(2) A contract to act or refrain from an act under the condition of the non-occurrence of a certain uncertain event within a certain time becomes legally enforceable, when the event does not occur after the certain time or before the expiration of the certain time, if it becomes certain that the event will not occur.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## An agreement under an impossible condition is void
36. An agreement to do or refrain from doing under the condition of the occurrence of an impossible event is void, regardless of whether at the time of the agreement the parties knew or not that the condition was impossible.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Obligation of the parties
37.-(1) The contracting parties have an obligation to fulfill or offer to fulfill their promises, unless they are exempted or exempted from this performance, by virtue of the provisions of this Law or any other Law.
(2) Unless a contrary intention is inferred from the contract, in the event of the debtor's death before the performance of the contract, the promise binds his representatives.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of renouncing an offer to be fulfilled
38.-(1) If the debtor offered to perform, and the creditor did not accept the offer, the debtor is not liable for non-performance, nor does he lose his rights from the contract.
(2) Every tender to be fulfilled must meet the following conditions-
(a) be unconditional
(b) be made at a suitable time and place and under such circumstances as to afford a reasonable opportunity to the person to whom it is addressed to ascertain whether the person offering to perform is able and willing at the time and place in question to perform whatever he owes under his promise~
(c) if the offer concerns the delivery of a thing to the creditor, he must be given a reasonable opportunity to examine whether the thing offered is what the debtor must deliver by virtue of his promise.
(3) An offer to one of several co-contractors carries the same legal consequences as if it were made to all.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of a party refusing to fulfill the promise in its entirety
39. If one of the contracting parties refuses to fulfill, or renders himself unable to fulfill, his promise in its entirety, the creditor may terminate the contract, unless he has expressed verbally or by conduct, his consent to continue the contract.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Obliged to fulfill a promise
40. If it appears from the nature of the contract that the intention of the parties was that any promise therein should be personally performed by the debtor, the promise must be performed by the debtor. In other cases, the debtor or the representatives of a deceased debtor may, in order to fulfill the promise, use a competent person for this.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of acceptance of performance by a third party
41. If the creditor accepts fulfillment of the promise by a third party, he cannot then turn to enforcement against the debtor.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Transfer of joint obligations
42. Unless a contrary intention is inferred from the contract, if two or more have made a joint promise, the obligation to be fulfilled rests with all of them while they are alive, and after the death of any one of them, the representatives of the deceased jointly with the survivor or survivors, and after the death of the last survivor the representatives of all the deceased jointly.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Each of the co-debtors may be compelled to perform
43.-(1) If two or more made a joint promise, the creditor may, in the absence of an express agreement to the contrary, force one or more of the co-debtors to fulfill the promise in its entirety.
(2) Unless a contrary intention is inferred from the contract, each of the co-debtors may compel any of the others to contribute an equal reason to the fulfillment of the promise.
(3) If any of the co-debtors fails to contribute, the damage caused by the omission is borne in equal shares by the others.
(4) The provisions of this section do not prevent the guarantor from seeking from the guarantor any payments made by the guarantor on behalf of the guarantor, nor entitle the guarantor to seek anything from the guarantor for payments made by the guarantor.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of discharge of one of the co-debtors
44. If two or more have made a joint promise, the release of one of the creditors does not release the others, nor does it release the released one from his liability towards the other co-debtors.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Transfer of common rights
45. Unless a contrary intention is inferred from the contract, if a person has made a promise to two or more jointly, the right to demand performance of the promise, in respect of the relations between him and the parties, has all the co-promisors while they are alive, and after the death of any one of them, the representatives of the deceased, jointly with the survivor or survivors, and after the death of the last survivor the representatives of all of the deceased jointly.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Fulfillment time when no disturbance is required and no fulfillment time is specified
46. If by virtue of the contract, the debtor is obliged to fulfill the promise without disturbing the creditor and no time of fulfillment is specified in the contract, the obligation must be fulfilled within a reasonable time.
"What is a reasonable time" is a question of fact in each specific case.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Time and place of performance when the time is set but no nuisance is required
47. If the promise was set to be fulfilled on a certain day and the debtor undertook to fulfill it without disturbing the creditor, the fulfillment may be done at any time during the normal working hours of that day and at the place where it must be fulfilled.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The nuisance to be fulfilled on a certain day must be carried out at the appropriate time and place
48. If the promise was set to be fulfilled on a certain day and the debtor did not undertake to fulfill it without disturbing the creditor, the creditor is obliged to disturb the debtor for performance at the proper place and during the usual working hours.
"What is an appropriate time and place" is, in each specific case, a question of fact.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Place of fulfillment when no nuisance is required and no place of fulfillment is specified
49. If the promise was set to be fulfilled without bothering the creditor and no place of fulfillment was specified, the debtor is obliged to bother the creditor to set a reasonable place for the fulfillment of the promise and fulfill it in that place.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Fulfillment in the manner or at the time specified or approved by the lender
50. The fulfillment of the promise may be done in any way or at any time specified or approved by the lender.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## In the case of mutual promises neither is bound to perform unless the other is ready and willing to perform
51. If the contract consists of mutual promises to be fulfilled at the same time, neither of the debtors is bound to fulfill the promise incumbent upon him unless the other is ready and willing to fulfill the promise incumbent upon him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Order of fulfillment of mutual promises
52. In case the contract expressly defines the order in which the mutual promises will be fulfilled, they must be fulfilled in the order specified~ in the absence of such a term in the contract, the promises must be fulfilled in the order imposed by the nature of the transaction.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability of the contracting party who prevents the execution of the contract
53. If the contract contains mutual promises and one of the contracting parties prevents the other from fulfilling his promise, the contract becomes voidable at the option of the hindered contracting party and the latter is entitled to compensation from the other for any damage he wanted to suffer due to the non-fulfillment of the contract.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of non-performance of a promise which should have been performed first under a contract consisting of mutual promises
54. If one of the mutual promises of which the contract consists cannot be fulfilled or its fulfillment cannot be claimed before the fulfillment of the other, the person who is burdened with this other promise, if he fails to fulfill it, cannot claim fulfillment of the promise with which the other of the contracting parties is burdened, but is obliged to compensate him for any damage that he wanted to suffer, due to non-fulfillment of the contract.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of non-performance within a certain time, in a contract where time is of the essence
55.-(1) If one of the contracting parties undertook to perform a certain action within a certain time or before that time, or certain actions within certain times or before those times, and fails to take any such action at or before the certain time, the contract or the part of the contract that has not yet been fulfilled becomes voidable at the option of the creditor, if the intention of the contracting parties was to make time an essential condition of contract.
(2) If the intention of the contracting parties was not to make time an essential condition of the contract, the contract does not become voidable due to the debtor's failure to act within the specified time or before it~ however, the lender has the right to compensation from the debtor for any damage that he wanted to suffer due to this failure.
(3) In the event of a voidable contract due to the debtor's failure to fulfill his promise at the agreed upon time, if the creditor accepts fulfillment of the promise at a time other than that agreed upon, then he cannot claim compensation for damage suffered due to non-fulfillment at the agreed time, unless, at the time of acceptance, he notifies the debtor of his intention to claim compensation.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agreement to perform an impossible act
56.-(1) An agreement to perform an act that is impossible in itself is void.
(2) A contract to perform an act, which after the conclusion of the contract becomes impossible, or illegal due to an event which the debtor could not prevent, becomes void as soon as the act becomes impossible or illegal.
(3) If the promise concerns an impossible or illegal act and the debtor knew or, if he exercised reasonable diligence, could have known of its impossibility or illegality, and the creditor did not know that it was impossible or illegal, the debtor is obliged to compensate the creditor for any damage that he wanted to suffer due to the non-fulfillment of the promise.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Mutual promises to perform legal and illegal acts
57. If by virtue of mutual promises an obligation was assumed to perform, firstly, certain legal acts, and secondly, under certain circumstances, other illegal acts, the part of the promises concerning the legal acts constitutes a contract, while the part of the promises concerning the illegal acts constitutes a void agreement.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Divorce promise, one part of which is illegal
58. In the case of a dissociative promise, one part of which is legal and the other illegal, only the legal part is enforceable.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Accrual of payment to pay off indicated debt
59. If a debtor of a debt, who owes several specific debts to the same person, makes any payment to him, either expressly or under circumstances from which it is inferred that the payment is to be charged in discharge of a particular debt, the payment, if accepted, must be charged in discharge of that debt.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Charge payment to pay off unspecified debt
60. If the debtor does not specify the debt to which the payment made concerns and this cannot be inferred from other circumstances, the creditor may, at will, attribute this payment to any legal debt of this debtor, which is payable to him and due, regardless of whether the recovery of this is prevented or not by the law in force regarding limitation.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Payment accrual in the event that none of the interested parties makes an accrual
61. If none of the interested parties proceed with the imputation of the payment that was made, it shall be imputed to pay off the debts in order of seniority, regardless of whether or not they can be recovered pursuant to the applicable statute of limitations. If the debts have the same order of seniority, the payment is calculated symmetrically.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of renewal, withdrawal and modification of the contract
62. If the parties agree to replace the contract with a new one, or to withdraw from the contract or modify it, the original contract need not be performed.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The creditor may discharge the debtor or waive his claim to be fulfilled
63. The creditor may fully or partially release the debtor from his obligation to be fulfilled or may waive all or part of his claim to be fulfilled or extend the time of fulfillment or accept instead any satisfaction he deems appropriate.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of withdrawing from a voidable contract
64. If the contracting party at whose choice the contract is voidable withdraws from it, the counterparty is not obliged to fulfill any promise that is burdened by him in the contract. The person who withdraws from a voidable contract is obliged, if any benefit was obtained from it by another contracting party, to restore this benefit, as far as possible, to the person from whom it was obtained.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Debtor's obligation from a void agreement or contract
65. If the agreement is proved to be inherently void, or the contract becomes void, the person who received any benefit under said agreement or contract is required to restore that benefit or pay compensation to the person from whom it was received.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Manner of notification or revocation of withdrawal from voidable contract
66. Withdrawal from a voidable contract may be notified or revoked in the same manner and under the same rules as those governing the notification or revocation of a proposal.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of failure of the creditor to provide the debtor with reasonable facilities for performance
67. If the creditor omits or refuses to provide the debtor with reasonable facilities for the fulfillment of his promise, the debtor has no responsibility for the non-fulfilment, which is due to said omission or refusal.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Claim for necessities supplied to or on behalf of a person incapable of contract
68. He who furnishes a person who lacks the capacity to contract, or another person who is legally supported by that person, with the necessities that are suitable for his position in society, is entitled to be compensated from the property of the person in question.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Compensation of person who pays money owed by another
69. A person who has an interest in the payment of money legally owed by another, and as a consequence pays them, has the right to be compensated by the other.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Obligation of a person who receives a benefit from a non-gratuitous act
70. If someone does something legally on behalf of another or delivers anything to him, without intending to do it gratuitously, the latter, if he wanted to gain a benefit, is obliged to compensate the former in relation to the act performed or to return the thing delivered.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability found
71. A person who finds goods belonging to another, and places them in his custody, has the same responsibility as the responsibility of the custodian.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability of person to whom money was paid or thing delivered as a result of mistake or coercion
72. A person to whom money has been paid or anything delivered as a result of error or coercion must return it.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Compensation for loss or damage caused by breach of contract
73.-(1) In the event of a breach of contract, the contracting party who is damaged by said breach has the right to compensation from the culpable counterparty, for the damage or loss suffered as a result thereof, which naturally arose in the ordinary course of things from said breach or which the contracting parties were aware of when the contract was concluded, as a possible consequence of the breach of contract.
No compensation is payable for remote and indirect loss or damage caused as a result of a breach of contract.
(2) The person who is damaged by the non-fulfillment of an obligation similar to contractual ones, is entitled to receive from the guilty party the same compensation, as if it were a breach of contract.
(3) In calculating the loss or damage resulting from the breach of contract, account must be taken of the means available to remedy the hardship caused by the non-performance of the contract.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Compensation for breach of contract which includes a penalty clause
74.-(1) If the contract includes a condition regarding the amount to be paid in the event of a breach thereof or a penalty clause, in the event of a breach of the contract by one of the contracting parties, the other party is entitled, and if it is not yet proven that he suffered actual damage or loss from the breach, to receive from the guilty party reasonable compensation that does not exceed the amount determined in the above manner, or depending on the case, the criminal clause.
A clause for the payment of increased interest for overdue payment may be considered as a penal clause.
(2) The person who executes a bond, personal obligation or other document of the same nature, or pursuant to the provisions of any Law or by order of the Government of the Republic of Cyprus, provides a security document for the performance of a public duty or an act of common interest, is obliged, in the event of a breach of the terms of any such document, to pay the entire amount stated therein:
It is understood that the conclusion of a contract with the Government does not necessarily imply the assumption of a public duty or the promise to perform an act of common interest.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The person who legally withdraws is entitled to compensation
75. The person who legally withdraws from the contract is entitled to compensation for any damage suffered as a result of the non-fulfillment of the contract.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Special performance of contract and conditions
76.-(1) The contract is amenable to special enforcement by the Court if-
(a) is not invalid under this Law or any other Law~ and
(b) is in writing~ and
(c) signed at the end of it by the person who bears the burden of it~and
(d) the Court considers, having regard to all the circumstances, that the imposition of specific performance of the contract would not be unreasonable or otherwise unreasonable or practically impracticable.
(2) The provisions of this article do not affect the special execution of contracts for the sale of immovable property pursuant to the provisions of the Land Sale (Special Execution) Law, or any amendment thereof.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Terms of leases and contracts due to marriage
77.-(1) A contract concerning the lease of immovable property for a period exceeding one year is not valid and enforceable unless-
(a) is in writing~ and
(b) is signed at the end thereof, by each person bound by the contract or by a person who is capable of contracting and who has been duly authorized to sign on behalf of the above person in the presence of at least two witnesses capable of contracting, who also sign the contract as witnesses.
(2) Subject to the provisions of article 26A of this Law, a marriage contract that is not a dowry document or a contract of a similar nature is not valid and enforceable unless-
(b) is signed at the end thereof, by each person bound by the contract or by a person who is competent to enter into the contract and who has been duly authorized to sign on behalf of the above person, in the presence of at least two witnesses, competent to enter into the contract, who also sign the contract as witnesses:
Provided that this subsection shall not apply to obligations due to marriage between Mohammedans entered into in accordance with the practice prevailing in the Turkish Family Courts.

## Definitions of “regular promissory note”, “debtor” and “creditor”, and essential elements of the promissory note
78. "Promissory note of the usual type" is a written promise, given by one person to another, signed by the person who gives it in the presence of at least two witnesses capable of contracting, to pay, on first demand or at a certain time or at a determinable time in the future to a person specified in the note, a sum of money, plus interest specified therein up to a maximum of nine percent per annum and, in the event of judicial action thereon, the related costs, and states the consideration for which the promise is provided.
The person providing the promise is called the "debtor" while the person to whom the promise is provided is called the "creditor".

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Promissory note secured by guarantee, pledge or mortgage
79. A promissory note of ordinary type is not void for the sole reason that it is secured by a guarantee, or pledge or mortgage on immovable property and includes relevant clauses.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Undisputed content of the letter
80. In any judicial measure taken by virtue of a letter of ordinary form, the contents of the said letter shall constitute incontrovertible evidence of the facts set forth therein:
Provided that in any such proceeding, it shall be a sufficient defense that the signature of the debtor or other signer of the note is not in fact his signature, or that the issuance of the note was obtained by duress or fraud or under circumstances amounting to duress or fraud.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Caveat
81. The provisions of this article do not affect, with respect to promissory notes of ordinary type, any power given or which may be exercised by or under the provisions of any of the following laws or any amendments thereof-
(a) of the Usury (Farmers) Law~
(b) of the Traders and Farmers Relations Law.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of “coverage contract”
82. The contract by which one of the contracting parties promises to cover the other for damage that may be suffered by the behavior of the debtor himself or any other person, is called a "covering contract".

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Rights of subpoenaed creditor under cover contract
83. The collateral creditor, acting within the limits of his authority, is entitled to seek from the debtor-
(a) all compensation which he may be required to pay in any judicial proceeding in respect of any matter covered by the promise of coverage~
(b) all costs which he may be required to pay in any such injunction, if in taking it or in asserting a defense thereto, he does not disobey the orders of the debtor, and has acted in a manner which it would be prudent for the creditor to act in the absence of a covering contract, or if the debtor has authorized him to take or assert a defense to the injunction~
(c) all the amount which he may pay under the terms of a compromise reached in a judicial measure, if the compromise reached is not contrary to the orders of the debtor, and is a compromise, which it would be reasonable for the creditor to make in the absence of a cover contract, or if the debtor authorized him to come to a judicial compromise.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## “Guarantee Agreement”, “guarantor”, “originator” and “creditor”
84. "Guarantee contract" is the contract to fulfill the promise or obligation of a third party, in case of non-fulfillment by the third party~ the person who provides the guarantee is called "guarantor", the person on whose behalf it is provided "primary debtor", and the person to whom it is provided "creditor".

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Guarantee return
85. Any act or promise for the benefit of the principal may constitute sufficient consideration for the guarantor to provide the guarantee.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability of guarantor
86. Unless otherwise provided in the contract, the guarantor is liable to the extent that the principal is also liable.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## "Continuous Guarantee"
87. A guarantee that extends over a series of transactions is called a "continuous guarantee".

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Revocation of continuing warranty
88. The continuous guarantee may be revoked at any time by the guarantor, with regard to future transactions, by notification to the creditor.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Revocation of a continuous guarantee due to the death of the guarantor
89. Unless there is a contractual term to the contrary, the death of the guarantor acts, with respect to future transactions, as a revocation of the continuing guarantee.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability of co-principals is not affected by the agreement between them that one will be surety in case of non-performance by the other
90. If two contract with a third party to assume a certain responsibility, and if they also agree between themselves that one of them is liable only in case of non-performance by the other, without the third party being a party to this contract, the liability of each of them towards the third party under the first contract is not affected at all by the existence of the second contract even if the third party knew of its existence.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Discharge of guarantor by changing the terms of the contract
91. Any change in the terms of the contract between the principal and the creditor, which is made without the guarantor's consent, relieves the guarantor of any responsibility for transactions subsequent to the change.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Discharge of guarantor with discharge of principal
92. The guarantor is released from all liability by the conclusion of a contract between the creditor and the principal, by which the principal is exempted, or by an act or omission of the creditor which by law results in the principal being exempted.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Discharge of guarantor in case of compromise, extension of the time of performance, or agreement not to bring an action against the principal by the creditor
93. A contract between creditor and principal by which the creditor compromises with the principal, or promises to extend the time of performance or not to sue the principal, relieves the guarantor from all liability, unless the guarantor consents to the conclusion of this contract.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## An agreement to extend the time of fulfillment between a creditor and a third party does not release the guarantor
94. If the contract to extend the time of performance by the principal is concluded between a creditor and a third party, and not with the principal, the guarantor is not released.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Abstention of the creditor from filing an action does not release the guarantor
95. If there is no provision to the contrary in the contract of guarantee, the mere abstention of the creditor from bringing an action or taking other means of treatment against the first obligor, does not release the guarantor.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Release of one co-guarantor does not release the others
96. When there are co-guarantors, the release of one of the creditor does not entail the release of the others, nor does it release the one released from his liability towards the other guarantors.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Discharge of the guarantor due to an act or omission of the creditor which prejudices the final satisfaction of the guarantor
97. If the creditor performs an act incompatible with the guarantor's rights, or omits to perform an act which is required by his obligations to the guarantor, and as a consequence of this the final satisfaction of the guarantor himself by the principal is impaired, the guarantor is released.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Rights of guarantor in case of payment or performance
98. If the debt, for which the guarantee was given, becomes due or the principal fails to fulfill an obligation covered by the guarantee, the guarantor, by paying or fulfilling everything for which he is responsible, is substituted in all the rights of the creditor against the principal.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Right of guarantor to benefit from collateral in favor of the creditor. Caveat
99.-(1) The guarantor is entitled to the benefit of any security which the creditor had against the principal at the conclusion of the guarantee contract, whether the guarantor knew of the existence of this security or not~ and if the creditor loses or, without the consent of the guarantor, becomes alienated from it, the guarantor is released to the extent of the value of the security.
(2) The provisions of this article or the previous article do not affect the provisions of the Civil Procedure Law or any amendment thereof.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## A warranty obtained by false representation is not valid
100. A guarantee secured by a false representation made by the creditor, or knowingly and with his consent, which concerns an essential part of the transaction, is not valid.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## A guarantee secured by concealment is not valid
101. A guarantee secured by the creditor by keeping silent as to an essential incident is not valid.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Guarantee subject to the accession of a co-guarantor
102. If a person gives a guarantee with the contractual condition that the creditor will not exercise his rights arising from it, until another person joins as a co-guarantor, the guarantee is not valid if that other person does not join.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Implied promise of guarantor coverage
103. In every contract of guarantee there is an implied promise of the principal to cover the guarantor, and the surety is entitled to claim from the principal any amount properly paid by him under the guarantee, but he may not seek an amount wrongly paid.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Co-guarantors are equally liable for contribution
104. When two or more persons are co-guarantors for the same debt or obligation, either jointly or separately, and either by virtue of the same or by virtue of different contracts, and whether with the knowledge of the others or not, the co-guarantors, unless there is a contractual term to the contrary, are equally liable among themselves for the payment of the entire debt due or the part of it that has not yet been paid by the principal.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Co-guarantors liable for different amounts
105. Co-guarantors who are responsible for a different amount are obliged to pay the amount owed equally, within the limits of their respective obligations.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definitions of “goods”, “company”, “depositary in a broad sense”, “depositor” and “custodian”
106.-(1) In this Part-
(a) “goods” means movable property of any kind and includes bills of exchange, promissory notes, notes of ordinary or non-standard type other than those secured by a mortgage on real property, a certificate of stock or bearer certificates of stock in a company~
"company" means a limited liability company incorporated under the provisions of the Companies Law or any amendment thereof or any joint-stock company originally incorporated under the provisions of the Ottoman Commercial Code~
(b) "bequest in a broad sense" is the delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, with the contractual condition that once the purpose for which they were delivered has been fulfilled, they will be returned or disposed of according to the instructions of the person who delivered them. The person who delivered the goods is called the "depositor" while the person to whom they were delivered is called the "custodian".
(2) The person who is in possession of goods belonging to another, if he agrees to keep them as a depositary, becomes a depositary of the goods, and the owner becomes a sub-depositor, even if the goods were not delivered by sub-deposit in a broad sense.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Method of delivery
107. Delivery to the custodian may be done by any act, which results in the goods coming into the possession of the intended custodian or another person authorized to hold them on his behalf.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Obligation of the consignor to notify the defects of the goods consigned
108.-(1) The depositor must notify the custodian of the defects in the goods deposited, which the depositor is aware of and which substantially impair their use or expose the depositor to unusual risks~ if the depositor fails to notify these defects, he is liable for any damage that the depositor directly suffers as a result of said defects.
(2) If the goods were deposited upon hire, the depositor is liable for this damage, regardless of whether or not he was aware of the existence of said defects in the goods deposited.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Custodian custody
109. In all cases of deposit in a broad sense, the depositary is obliged to pay with respect to the goods deposited the diligence which a person of ordinary prudence would pay under similar circumstances for his own goods, of the same volume, quality and value as the goods deposited.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Cases in which the depositary is not liable for loss, etc., of goods deposited
110. Unless there is a special contractual term, the custodian bears no responsibility for the loss, destruction or deterioration of the goods deposited, if he has paid the diligence required by article 109.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Termination of a deposit in a broad sense by an act of the depositary incompatible with its terms
111. The deposit agreement in a broad sense, is voidable at the option of the depositor, if the depositary performs any act regarding the goods deposited which is incompatible with the terms of the deposit.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability of a depositary who uses the goods deposited in a manner inconsistent with the terms of the deposit in a broad sense
112. The depositary, who uses the goods deposited in a manner inconsistent with the conditions of the deposit in a broad sense, is obliged to pay compensation to the depositor for any damage caused to the goods by or during such use.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of commingling with the depositor's consent the goods deposited with the goods of the depositary
113. If the depositor, with the consent of the depositor, mixes the goods deposited with his own, the depositor and the depositor shall have an interest in the resulting mixture in proportion to their shares.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of commingling, without the consent of the depositor, when the goods are severable
114. If the custodian, without the consent of the depositor, mixes the goods deposited with his own, and the goods are susceptible of separation or division, the ownership of the goods remains as it was before~ but the depositary must bear the expense of the separation or division, as well as any damage caused by the mixing.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of commingling, without the consent of the depositor, when the goods are not capable of being separated
115. If the custodian, without the consent of the depositor, mixes the goods deposited with his own, in such a way as to make it impossible to separate the goods deposited from the rest and to return them, the depositor is entitled to compensation from the depositary for the loss of the goods.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Payment of necessary expenses by the depositor
116. If the terms of the deposit in a broad sense provide for safekeeping, transportation, or performance of work on the goods deposited by the depositary for the depositor, and the depositor is not entitled to remuneration, the depositor must pay the depositor the necessary expenses incurred for the purposes of the deposit.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Useful
117. The person who granted something to another for use, may at any time demand its return, if the loan was made for a charitable cause and if he also granted it for use for a certain time or purpose. However, if the user, believing that the loan was concluded for a certain time or purpose, acted in such a way that the return of the thing before the time agreed upon would cause him damage that exceeds the actual benefit he obtained from the loan, the user, if he forces the return of the thing, must compensate the user for the damage caused to the extent that it exceeds the benefit he obtained.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Return of goods deposited with the lapse of time or the fulfillment of the purpose
118. The custodian is obliged, without disturbance, to return or deliver in accordance with the instructions of the depositor the goods deposited, as soon as the time has passed or the purpose for which the deposit was made in a broad sense has been fulfilled.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consignor's liability in case of improper return of the goods consigned
119. If, due to the custodian's negligence, the deposited goods are not returned, delivered or offered for delivery in the appropriate time, the depositary is liable to the depositor, from that time, for the loss, destruction or deterioration of the deposited goods.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Solution of gratuitous bequest due to death
120. Gratuitous bequest in a broad sense is terminated by the death of the bequeather or custodian.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The depositor is entitled to the increase or profit from the goods deposited
121. Unless there is a contractual term to the contrary, the depositary must deliver to the depositor or in accordance with his instructions any increase or profit that may have arisen from the goods deposited.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability of depositor vis-à-vis depositary
122. The depositor is liable to the depositor for any damage, which the depositor wanted to suffer as a result of the depositor not having the right to set up the deposit in a broad sense, or to receive back the goods deposited or to give instructions regarding them.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Bequest in a broad sense from many contemporaries
123. If several co-owners deposit goods, the custodian may return them to one of the co-owners, or according to his instructions, without the consent of all, provided that there is no agreement to the contrary.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The bailee is not liable if he returns the goods deposited to a depositor who has no title
124. If the depositor has no title to the goods deposited, and the custodian, in good faith, returns them to the depositor or according to his instructions, the depositor has no liability to the owner in respect of such return.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Right of a third party who claims the goods deposited
125. If someone other than the misdepositor claims the misdeposited goods, he may take judicial measures to prevent their delivery to the misdepositor, and to issue a decision regarding the title of the misdeposited goods.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Rights of discovery~ right to sue for special remuneration offered
126. The finder of an apollo has no right of action against the owner for compensation for the inconvenience and expense he voluntarily incurred for the preservation of the apollo and the search for the owner~ however, he may retain the find until he receives such compensation from the owner~ in the event that the owner offered a special fee for the return of the apollo, the finder may sue the owner for the this fee, and to retain what is found until he receives the fee.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Cases in which the finder may sell the find
127. When the lost property is a thing which is usually the object of sale, if the owner cannot be found by paying reasonable diligence or if he is asked to pay the finder everything to which the finder is legally entitled, and he refuses, the finder may sell the found-
(a) if it is in danger of being damaged or losing most of its value~or
(b) if the amount to which he is legally entitled amounts to two-thirds of its value.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Special right of attachment of the depositary
128. Unless there is a contractual term to the contrary, the depositary, who according to the purpose of the deposit in a broad sense, rendered a service entailing the payment of labor or skill in relation to the goods deposited, is entitled to retain the goods deposited until he receives the appropriate remuneration for the services rendered.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## General right of lien of bankers, agents and waterfront supervisors
129. Unless there is a contractual term to the contrary, bankers, agents and quay supervisors, may retain as security for a general account balance any goods deposited with them~ but no other person has a right, except by virtue of an express contractual term, to retain as security any goods deposited with him for the said balance.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definitions of “pledger”, “pledger”, “pawnbroker”
130. The delivery of goods to secure the payment of a debt or the fulfillment of a promise is called a "pledge". In this case, the person who delivers the goods is called the "pledger" and the person to whom they are delivered is called the "pledger".

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Right of a pawnbroker to retain the goods pledged
131. The pawnbroker may retain the goods, which have been pledged, not only for the payment of the debt or the fulfillment of the promise, but also for the interest of the debt, and for all the necessary expenses which he incurs in connection with the possession or maintenance of the goods which have been pledged.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## A pledge does not cover a debt or promise other than that for which it was created. Presumption in case of subsequent payments
132. Unless there is a contractual term to this effect, the pawnbroker may not retain the goods pledged for any debt or promise other than the obligation or debt for which the pledge was created~ but this contractual term, unless there is anything to the contrary, is presumed to exist in respect of sums subsequently paid by the pawnbroker.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Right of mortgagor in respect of extraordinary expenses
133. The pledgor is entitled from the pledgor to the extraordinary expenses he incurs for the maintenance of the pledged goods.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Right of mortgagor in case of default of mortgagor
134.-(1) If the pledgor becomes overdue for the payment of the debt or the fulfillment of the promise, for which the pledge was made, the pledgor may take legal action against the pledgor based on the debt or the promise, and seize the pledged goods as collateral security, or sell them after previously giving them to the pledgor reasonable notice of sale.
(2) If the proceeds of the sale are less than what is due under the debt or promise, the pledgee is still liable for the balance. If the proceeds of the sale exceed the said amount owed, the pawnbroker must pay the pawnee the surplus.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Right of pledgor to release pledged goods in case of default
135. If a time was agreed upon for the payment of the debt, or the fulfillment of the promise, for which the pledge was established, and the pledgor is in default, he may release the goods that have been pledged at any later time before their actual sale~ but in this case, he must additionally pay any expenses arising from his default.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Pledge by holder of goods or documents of title
136. A person in possession of any goods, or any bill of lading, port order, warehouseman's certificate, quay supervisor's certificate, or warrant or order for delivery or any other document of title to goods, may set up a valid lien on such goods or documents, provided that the pledgee acts in good faith, and in circumstances which are not such as to create a reasonable presumption that the pledgor is acting without right, and that the goods or documents in question were not obtained from their rightful owner or from another who has their rightful custody, by criminal offense or fraud.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Pledge by a person who has a limited interest
137. If the pledgor has only a limited interest in the goods pledged, the pledge is valid to the extent of that interest.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Conditions for setting up a pledge on bills of exchange, promissory notes, etc.
138. Pledge to-
(a) bills of exchange, or
(b) promissory notes, or
(c) promissory notes whether or not ordinary, other than those secured by a mortgage on real property, or
(d) share certificates or bearer share certificates in a company, to secure the payment of a debt or the fulfillment of a promise;
is not valid and enforceable, unless the contract of the pledge-
(i) is in writing, and
(ii) signed at the end thereof by the pledgor, and
(iii) drawn up in the presence of at least two witnesses, capable of contracting, who subscribe to it as witnesses.
(2) A lien on share certificates or bearer share certificates in a company which are transferable in a manner other than delivery, is not valid and enforceable, unless, in addition to the conditions specified in subsection (1), the following conditions are also met-
(a) the pledgor sends to the company a notice of the creation of the pledge, together with a certified copy of the pledge agreement, and
(b) the company records such pledge in the register of shareholders against the shares to which such notice relates, and
(c) the company delivers to the pawnbroker a certificate that the pledge was recorded in the register in accordance with the above.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Right of the mortgagor in case of default of the mortgagor under a contract drawn up in accordance with article 138
139. Unless there is a clause to the contrary, in a contract of pledge, drawn up in accordance with the provisions of article 138, in the event of default by the pledgor in paying the debt or fulfilling the promise, the pledgor has the same rights and remedies for the pledge against third parties, which the pledgor himself would have had in the absence of the contract of pledge, and any payment made to mortgagor from third parties for this pledge, is valid and effective as if it were made to the mortgagor himself.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Judicial measures of depositor or depositary against wrongdoer
140. If a third party unjustly deprives the depositary of the use or possession of the goods deposited, or causes damage to them, the depositary is entitled to use the same means of treatment, which the owner could use in a similar case, if there was no deposit in a broad sense~ each of them, depositor or depositary, may take legal measures against a third person for the deprivation or damage in question.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Apportionment of treatment or compensation
141. The remedy or compensation awarded in the event of taking said judicial measures is divided between the depositor and the depositary, according to the interests of each.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definitions of “agent” and “represented”
142. "Agent" is the person who is hired to perform an act on behalf of another or to represent another in transactions with third parties. The person on whose behalf this act is performed, or who is represented in this way, is called "represented".

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Ability to hire agent
143. An agent may hire any person who is capable of contracting.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Capacity to represent
144. With regard to the relations between the represented and third parties, anyone can become an agent, but no person who lacks the capacity to contract can become an agent, so that he is liable to the represented in accordance with the provisions contained in this article.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The consideration is not necessary for the establishment of agency
145. No consideration is required for the establishment of a representative office.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The power of attorney may be express or implied
146. The representative's power of attorney may be express or implied.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definitions of express and implied power of attorney
147. The power of attorney is considered express, when it is provided orally or in writing. A power of attorney is implied when it is inferred from the facts of the case ~ anything that has been said or written or the ordinary course of dealings, may be considered as the facts of the case.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Extent of power of attorney
148.-(1) The representative's power of attorney to perform an act also includes power of attorney to perform any legal thing that is necessary for the performance of this act.
(2) The agent's power of attorney to conduct business also contains power of attorney to perform any legal thing that is necessary for this purpose or that is usually done during the conduct of said business.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Power of attorney in case of emergency
149. In an emergency, the agent is empowered to do any act to protect the principal from harm, which a person of ordinary prudence would do in his own affairs under similar circumstances.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## When substitution of an agent is excluded
150. The agent may not by law hire another to perform acts, which he expressly or implicitly undertook to perform in person, unless the hiring of a substitute agent is permitted by business ethics or required by the nature of the agency.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Definition of “Substitute Agent”
151. "Substitute agent", is a person capable of contracting who is hired and acts under the control of the original agent during the conduct of the business of the agency.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Representation by duly appointed agent
152.-(1) If the substitute agent is duly appointed, the represented, as far as third parties are concerned, is represented by the substitute agent, is bound by his acts and is responsible for them, as if it were an agent originally appointed by the represented.
(2) The agent is liable to the represented for the acts of the substitute agent.
(3) The substitute agent is liable for the acts, vis-à-vis the agent, but not vis-à-vis the represented, except in the case of fraud or an offense committed intentionally.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's Liability for Substitute Agent Appointed Without Authority
153. If the representative has appointed a substitute representative, without being authorized to do so, the representative is in relation to the person so appointed in the relationship of represented to agent, and is liable for his actions both to the represented and to third parties~ the represented is not represented by the person so appointed nor is he responsible for his actions, but the person so appointed is not liable against the represented.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Relationship between an agent and a person duly appointed by the agent to carry out the agency
154. If the agent, having express or implied authority to appoint another person to act for the principal in the conduct of the business of the agency, has appointed another person, the person so appointed shall not be considered a substitute agent, but an agent of the principal to the extent of the business of the agency entrusted to him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Obligation of agent upon selection of said person
155. In selecting such agent, the agent must exercise the same care as a man of ordinary prudence would exercise in his own affairs~ if he exercises such care, he is not liable to the principal for the acts or omissions of the agent so chosen.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Approval and consequences of approval
156. When a person performs acts on behalf of another person without his knowledge or authorization, this person may either approve or disavow this act - an approved act carries the same consequences as if it were done by virtue of a power of attorney.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consent express or implied
157. Approval may be express or inferred from the conduct of the person on whose behalf the act was performed.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Knowledge of the facts is a condition for the validity of the approval
158. Approval cannot be validly carried out by a person who has essentially incomplete knowledge of the facts of the case.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of approval, act, done without power of attorney, which constitutes part of transaction
159. The person who approves an act performed on his behalf without a power of attorney, at the same time approves the entire transaction of which this act is a part.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Approval of an act performed without a power of attorney cannot harm a third party
160. An act performed on behalf of another without his power of attorney, which if performed under power of attorney would cause damage to a third party or the amortization of a third party's right or interest, cannot bring about these consequences with approval.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agency solution
161. The agency is terminated by the revocation of the power of attorney by the represented~ or by the termination of the agency by the agent~ or by the termination of the agency's work~ or by the death or incapacitation of the represented or the agent~ or by the declaration of the represented by the court in a state of bankruptcy or insolvency under the provisions of any Law in force, which concerns the bankruptcy or insolvency.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Termination of agency when the agent has an interest in the subject matter of the agency
162. If the agent has an interest in the property, which is the object of the agency, in the absence of an express contractual term, the agency cannot be terminated to the detriment of this interest.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Revocation of power of attorney
163. Unless otherwise provided for under article 162, the represented may revoke at any time the power of attorney he gave to the representative before it is exercised in a way that binds the represented.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Revocation of power of attorney after its partial exercise
164. The represented cannot revoke the power of attorney he gave to the agent after it has been partially exercised, regarding actions and obligations arising from actions already performed in relation to the representation.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Compensation for revocation of power of attorney or termination of agency
165. If there is an express or implied contractual term for a certain duration of the agency, the represented or agent must compensate the other for the untimely, without sufficient reason, revocation of the power of attorney or termination of the agency, as the case may be.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Notice of revocation or termination
166. In case of revocation or denunciation, reasonable warning must be given to this end~ the person who revokes or denounces, as the case may be, without reasonable warning, is obliged to compensate the damage caused to the other party as a result thereof.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Revocation and termination may be explicit or implicit
167. Revocation and termination may be express or inferred from the behavior of the represented or representative respectively.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## When does the termination of power of attorney become active with respect to the agent and third parties
168. The termination of the power of attorney, with regard to the agent, does not become active before it becomes known to him, and with regard to third parties before it becomes known to them.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's obligation in case of termination of the agency by death or insanity of the represented
169. If the agency is terminated by the death or disability of the reasonable agent, the agent must take on behalf of the representatives of the deceased agent every reasonable measure for the protection and maintenance of the interests entrusted to him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Termination of Substitute Representative's Power of Attorney
170. Subject to the rules included in this Part and which concern the termination of a representative's power of attorney, the termination of this power of attorney also results in the termination of the power of attorney of all substitute representatives appointed by him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's obligation in the conduct of the business of the represented
171. The agent is obliged to carry out the operations of the represented in accordance with his instructions or in the absence of such instructions, in accordance with the commercial customs prevailing in operations of the same kind, in the place where the agent carries out these operations. An agent who acts in violation of the above must, if damage occurs, restore it to the represented person, and if profit accrues, he must be accountable for it.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's skill and diligence
172. The agent is obliged to carry out the work of the agency by paying the skill, which is generally possessed by persons who carry out similar work, unless the represented knows that he lacks this skill. The agent is bound to exercise reasonable care and skill at all times~ he is also bound to indemnify the represented for the direct results of his omission, lack of skill or misconduct, but not for loss or damage caused indirectly or unforeseeably in the ordinary course of things as a result of said omission, lack of skill or misconduct.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's obligation to submit accounts
173. If summoned by the represented, the agent is obliged to submit appropriate accounts to him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's obligation to communicate with the represented
174. The agent is obliged to pay, in difficult circumstances, all reasonable diligence to communicate with the represented and take his orders.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's right when the agent deals on his own account without the agent's consent
175. If the agent, during the conduct of the business of the agency, transacts on his own account, without first obtaining the consent of the represented and without notifying him of all the material circumstances that have come to his knowledge about the case, the represented may terminate the transaction, if it becomes apparent either that the agent dishonestly concealed from him any material fact, or that the agent's transactions have become detrimental to him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Entitlement of principal to benefit acquired by agent acting on own account, without knowledge of principal
176. If the agent, without the knowledge of the represented, during the conduct of the business of the agency transacts on his own account instead of on behalf of the represented, the represented is entitled, regardless of the provisions included in Part VIII of this Law, to demand from the agent any benefit that he wanted to derive from the transaction.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's right to retain money due to him from sums received on behalf of principal
177. The agent may withhold from any amount received on behalf of the represented during the performance of the agency's work, any sum of money due to him, in relation to payments or expenses made by him, and to which he was properly subjected during the conduct of said work, and also such remuneration as may be due to him for the performance of the work of the agent.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's obligation to pay sums received on behalf of agent
178. Subject to the above deductions, the agent is obliged to pay to the represented any amount received on his behalf.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## When the agent's fee becomes due
179. If there is no special contractual term, no remuneration is due to the agent for the performance of any act, until the act is completed~ the agent may, however, keep money collected from him from the sale of goods even if not all the goods sent to him for sale have yet been sold or even if the sale has not yet been actually completed.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The agent is not entitled to remuneration for work performed incorrectly
180. An agent who is guilty of improperly conducting the business of the agency is not entitled to remuneration for the part of the business that he conducted badly.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's Right of Retention
181. Unless there is a contractual term to the contrary, the agent is entitled to retain the goods, documents and other assets, movable and immovable of the represented person taken from him, until he is paid any amount due to him as a commission or for the expenses and services in relation to the above or until explanations are given to him about this amount.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent coverage against the consequences of legal acts
182. The represented is obliged to cover the agent for the consequences of all legal acts performed by the agent in the exercise of the power of attorney granted to him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent coverage for acts performed in good faith
183. The principal is obliged to cover the agent for the consequences of an act performed by him at the instruction of the principal, as long as he performed it in good faith, even if it also caused damage to the rights of third parties.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The represented person is not liable for a criminal act of an agent that was carried out on his order
184. The principal is not obliged to the agent, either on the basis of an express or implied promise, to cover him for the consequences of a criminal act committed by the agent on the instructions of the principal.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Agent's compensation for damage caused by agent's omission
185. The represented must compensate the agent for damage caused to him due to the omission or lack of skill of the represented.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Enforceability of contracts concluded with an agent and their legal consequences
186. Contracts concluded with an agent and the obligations arising from acts of the agent are enforceable in the same way and bring about the same legal consequences, as if they were concluded or performed by the represented person himself.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Extent of commitment of the representative in case of exceeding the power of attorney
187. If the agent acts in excess of the power of attorney given to him, and when the part of what was carried out, which falls within the power of attorney, can be separated from the part that was carried out in excess, then only the part that falls within the power of attorney is binding in terms of the relations between him and the represented.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## When the representative is not bound in case of exceeding the power of attorney
188. If the representative acts in excess of the power of attorney given to him and the part of what was carried out in excess of the limits of the power of attorney cannot be separated from what was carried out within its limits, the represented person is not obliged to recognize the transaction.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of Notice Communicated to Agent
189. Notice communicated to the agent or information received by the agent in the course of carrying out work for the principal, brings about the same legal consequences, with regard to the relations between the principal and third parties, as if the notice had been communicated to the principal or the information had been received by him.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## A representative cannot personally execute a contract concluded on behalf of a represented person, nor is he bound by it
190.-(1) In the absence of a contractual term to this effect, the representative cannot personally execute a contract concluded by him on behalf of the represented, nor is he personally bound by it.
(2) Such contractual term shall be presumed to exist in the following cases-
(a) when the contract is drawn up by an agent for the sale or purchase of goods, on behalf of a merchant resident abroad~
(b) where the agent does not disclose the name of the representee~
(c) when the person represented, even though his name has been disclosed, cannot be sued.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Rights of parties to a contract entered into with an agent who did not disclose his status
191.-(1) If the agent concludes a contract with a person whom he does not know, nor has reason to suspect that he is acting as an agent of another, the represented party may demand the fulfillment of the contract~ the other contracting party has the same rights vis-à-vis the represented person as he would have vis-à-vis the agent, if he was acting on his own behalf.
(2) If the principal makes himself known before the contract is fully performed, the contracting party may refuse to perform it, if he proves that he would not have entered into the contract if he had known who the principal was in the contract, or if he had known that the principal was not acting on his own behalf.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Performance of a contract entered into with an agent who appears to be acting on his own behalf
192. If a person enters into a contract with another, without knowing and without having a reasonable reason to suspect that the contracting party is acting as an agent of another, the represented party, if he demands fulfillment of the contract, may achieve it only subject to the reservation of the rights and obligations that exist between the agent and the contracting party.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Rights of a person dealing with an agent who is personally liable
193. In cases where the agent has personal liability, the person dealing with him may turn against either him or the represented person, or both.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequence of forcing an agent or represented to act in the belief that the other alone will be liable
194. If the person who entered into a contract with an agent, induced the agent to act in the belief that only the represented would be liable to him, or induced the represented to act in the belief that only the agent would be liable to him, he cannot then turn against the agent or the represented respectively.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Fiduciary liability
195. The person who falsely represents himself as the authorized representative of another, and in this way induces a third party to transact with him in the capacity of representative, is obliged to compensate the third party for any loss or damage suffered by him from this transaction, if the person presented as represented does not approve of his actions.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## The fictitious agent is not entitled to performance
196. The person who entered into a contract with another as an agent, is not entitled to demand performance of the contract, if in fact he was acting, not as an agent, but on his own account.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Liability of agent who leads third parties to believe that the agent was acting within his authority
197. If the representative, without a power of attorney, performed acts or assumed obligations towards third parties on behalf of the represented, the represented person is bound by these acts or obligations, if verbally or by his behavior he led third parties to believe that these acts and obligations were carried out within the limits of the representative's power of attorney.

*(CHAPTER 149)*

## Consequences of agent's misrepresentation or fraud in agreements
198. A false representation made or fraud committed, by an agent during the conduct of the business of the agency brings about the same legal consequences in agreements drawn up by the agent, as if the false representation was made or the fraud was committed by the represented ~ but a false representation made or fraud committed, by an agent in matters that do not fall within the limits of his power of attorney, does not affect the represented.
